# E005 — Outline (8 chapters, ~1,150 words each; 60 min). Form D: the clock in Courtroom Two runs the story (the courthouse clock is four minutes slow and Vera notices every time).

Rules for the writer: first person, past tense, Vera telling it looking back from September. Every chapter returns to the courtroom after its flashback and moves the hearing forward. Chapters 2+ begin with one natural line reminding where we are. Keep humor dry. Brent is a person, not a monster. No hidden-ownership trick, no document-reading at a party, no "lawyer reveals".

1. **Nine O'Clock, Courtroom Two** — Cold open in the courtroom: Brent's lawyer reads the petition aloud in a flat voice. Vera counts the room (fourteen in the gallery, eleven steps from the door). Brent pats her hand and says "Mom, nobody's blaming you." She knows who she is: the bus, the thirty-seven years, the head counts, the pear farm, Lloyd. One line promise: she will tell what the judge asked and what she answered. Ends: the lawyer reads "six calls to the sheriff about prowlers who did not exist." Vera: "There were six calls. There were also prowlers. I counted them."
2. **The Young Man in the Baseball Cap** — Flashback: the Thursday the process server came to the farm stand; Pearl, jam jars; the paper; "Petition for Appointment of a Guardian"; Brent's call that night ("It's for your own good, Mom"); the Christmas refusal. Back to courtroom: the clerk swears in Brent as the first witness, he smiles at her. Ends with him testifying about "the stove incident."
3. **A Visit Without Notice** — Flashback: Imani Okafor's unannounced visit two weeks before; tea; a long walk through the north rows; Imani counts something and says nothing; Vera notices her notes. Back in court: Brent's testimony about "wandering" at dusk and the missed property tax bill. Vera's young lawyer whispers "Do you want me to object?" She says "Let him finish." Ends: Brent says "she thinks men are hiding in her trees."
4. **Six Evenings** — Flashback: the first evening, the white truck with the ladder rack and the ghost of a peeled sign, two men with a tripod in the north rows at dusk, forty-one orange stakes; her calls, Danny's visits, five times the truck gone. She wrote the plate down on the back of an egg carton lid ("I count, I do not guess"). Back in court: Danny Ocampo called, bashful, "Miss Vera... Mrs. Halvorsen"; he answers honestly: he found nothing each time except, on his fourth visit, orange stakes with ribbon. The lawyer for Brent: "No prowlers found." Danny: "No, sir, none present when I arrived." Ends: Danny steps down, and Vera sees Tucker Rand lean forward in the back row.
5. **The Mailbox in Medford** — Flashback: the property tax notice; the post office; the postmaster (a former passenger) tells her about the change of address filed in her name, reroute to a post office box in Medford. Vera sits in her truck a long while. Quiet scene of the farm in March. Back in court: recess called at ten forty. In the hallway she overhears Brent and Tucker Rand by the water fountain: "Once she's under, the timing works." "Our people already got what they needed from the ridge." Vera hears everything, counts, and decides calmly. Ends: she sits back down; Pearl whispers "You look like you're about to drive a bus."
6. **Recess** — Courthouse hallway and bench: Pearl, peppermints, bad coffee, Danny on his break; Vera tells her young lawyer only one thing: "When the judge asks me something, don't stop me." Flashback (short): Lloyd on the porch, "you borrow it from the next fellow." The records clerk with the sheriff's call log in a folder, which the lawyer has subpoenaed (the lawyer did his job quietly; show it, no reveal). Back in court: Imani's report is already in the record; the judge says she has read it. Judge Marsh takes off her glasses. "Mrs. Halvorsen. I have one question." Chapter ends on that line.
7. **The Question** — The judge's question: "You called the sheriff six times in five weeks. Tell me exactly what you saw." Vera answers like a driver filling out an incident report: six evenings, times, two men and then three, one white truck with a ladder rack, a plate ending seven four one beginning K, T, R; forty-one orange stakes, which she counted twice; and, without being asked, "the same three letters on the truck's door where somebody peeled off a sign; I could read the ghost: B, A, S." The records clerk confirms the call log: same times. The judge re-calls Brent. The one question to Brent (see bible). Brent's attempts: "a timing matter." "That is not an answer to my question." He says yes. The silence. The judge's ruling: petition denied; "your mother has been telling the truth about her own back acres since late February"; the transcript will go to the proper office. Brent looks at Vera; she's counting the steps to the door out of habit. Ends on the gavel and Vera standing up on her own.
8. **Pears in September** — After: Tucker Rand's company withdraws within the week (one flat paragraph). The land trust visit; Vera signs a conservation agreement ("you borrow it from the next fellow"). The u-pick day for kids she used to drive, now with kids of her old kids; she counts heads, twice. Brent's two voicemails; she plays the second one and doesn't answer. Pearl's last peppermint. Final: the porch, the phone, the trees; she says she'll decide tomorrow and she may call; ask the listeners: would you call him back, and where are you listening from. Warm, open ending. Short wise thought without preaching.
