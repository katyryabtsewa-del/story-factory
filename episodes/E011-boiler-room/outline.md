# E011 — Outline (60 min, 8 chapters, 1,100–1,200 words each)

Form C: present line = the night of Dec 22–23 and the open meeting; past line = how it got here. Each chapter braids both, past always short and in service of the present. Tense, practical, dry humor; Connie never gloats. No festive scene, no grandchild, no lawyer, no document read aloud at a party, no hidden owner. Skeleton: cold open → who she is → three waves (November weekend/board meeting → the Run of Show → the call) → midpoint (the report in the drawer) → quiet plan (keeping the building alive, then the meeting) → payback by witnesses → new life.

1. **Overflow** — Cold open: Monday Dec 22, 9:44 p.m., her kitchen in Fort Wayne, the phone: "Missus Rudd, it's making that sound again." She is already reaching for her coat. One-sentence promise: "By morning ninety-six households would know exactly who had been keeping their building warm and who had been keeping it cheap, and I want to say now that I never meant it to go that way." Flash to the spreadsheet three weeks earlier: "Christmas Run of Show," her name under "Overflow, December twenty-seventh, ninety minutes, optional." Who she is: Connie Rudd, sixty-nine, thirty-four years in the boiler plant of Saint Brendan's, Ferris, the space heater she doesn't trust. Neil's one-line reply-all. Isaiah on the phone in the lobby describing what he hears; she asks three questions. Ends: she writes the miles on the back of an envelope, hundred and sixty, puts the pipe wrench in the trunk, and says out loud to the empty kitchen: "Well. I wasn't invited to Christmas."

2. **The Boiler Thing** — Drive (icy, minus nine, tail lights, Isaiah talking her through it on speaker) braided with the November weekend: the knocking radiator in 4B, Vanessa's "Constance," the boiler room with the propped door, meeting Dmitri and Isaiah, her reading of the gauges, the sludged cutoff, the stinking condensate tank, her two-page letter, the Saturday board meeting: Connie's two minutes in open comment, Vanessa's polite answer ("Mrs. Rudd is my mother-in-law and a retired hospital engineer; the report speaks for itself"), the laughter, the vote four to one. Esther Bloom's one dissent and the look she gave Connie. Vanessa in the elevator: "You made me look like I can't run my own building." Ends: on the Skyway, her phone lights: Dmitri, whispering: "Ma'am, it locked out again. Third time."

3. **Twelve Forty-Six** — Arrival at 12:46 a.m. Isaiah at the door with a flashlight and the lobby already cold. Dmitri in the boiler room, sleeves wet, panic under politeness. She does what she was trained to do: sit, listen, ask him to tell her the last four hours; looks at everything before touching anything. The facts of the plant: Boiler Two red-tagged since October, Boiler One carrying the whole building, the sludge in the low-water cutoff, the feed pump labouring. The vendor (Mister Ruiz) is on a highway somewhere with a truck stuck, earliest dawn. Her rules for the night: nobody lies about what time it is, nobody does anything alone, and nobody touches a valve without saying its name out loud. First blowdown, the long roar, the black water; the cutoff clears; they relight. It holds. Eleven minutes. Then the pump seal lets go. Flash to Ferris, a lineman, telling her "you do the next right thing and you say it out loud." Ends: Isaiah: "Missus Rudd. The folks on six are knocking on the wall."

4. **Sixth Floor** — Priorities: the north wing is dying first; forty residents over seventy; Esther Bloom on three in her coat and her cane at her own door, not asking for anything, asking exactly the right questions. Connie and Isaiah and Dmitri triage: open the Gallery and lobby fireplace as a warming room, collect space heaters on separate circuits, move the man in 6F, who is on oxygen, down to the Gallery with his portable tank, drain the exposed standpipe on six with neighbors holding buckets in their pajamas; Dmitri finds a spare seal kit in a toolbox he'd never opened; Connie teaches a thirty-four-year-old tenant with a podcast voice to hold a flashlight "like it matters." Neighbors begin to talk to each other for the first time. Past: what thirty-four years in the boiler room taught her about being the only woman in the room: she was told to "go find the coffee" the first year. Ends: Esther at the foot of the basement stairs, handing her a manila folder: "You'll want this. He's been told to keep it in the drawer."

5. **The Drawer** — 4:10 a.m., the Ketterman report (twelve pages) and the minutes with Vanessa's motion; Dmitri's confession: he kept a copy and told nobody because he was told it was "handled." Connie reads it twice. The midpoint: she realizes this was not an accident and not stupidity but a decision made on a spreadsheet. She does not call Vanessa. She does not photograph the pages for revenge. She asks Dmitri whether he wants his name on a thing like this; he does; she tells him how to do it right (write down what you saw, with times, and tell the whole board, not one person). Isaiah's quiet sentence. Past: Ferris's last winter, a storm, an outage, a woman on the coop's list who did not make it and the day he came home and sat in the truck for an hour: "Somebody knew and it got filed." Connie decides in the calm way: the building comes first, then the truth, in that order. Ends: the bang of the second blowdown, and then the sound she has been listening for all night: steam in the north riser.

6. **Daybreak** — 6:05 a.m., steam on six; the cheer is small and tired; the Gallery is full of people in coats and sleeping bags; Esther slips notices under every door for a three o'clock open meeting. Past/present: the Run of Show again, what it is like to be an "optional" in your own son's life, and how Connie felt on the drive when she passed the exit for the hospital. 7:20: Vanessa arrives in a car-service sedan in a camel coat with forty missed calls, finds her mother-in-law in canvas coveralls in the lobby with grease to her elbows and a line of residents thanking her. "Constance. What are you doing here?" "Keeping your building warm." Vanessa: "I didn't ask you to." Connie: "I know." 7:40: Graham Tolliver arrives; in the lobby he asks Dmitri, then Isaiah, then Esther a plain question: what happened, and for how long did anyone know? Ends: Tolliver turns to Vanessa and says, quietly, "Four-B. Ten minutes. Bring the report."

7. **Open Meeting** — 3:00 p.m., the Gallery, a hundred and thirty residents, folding chairs, Esther chairing with a coffee can for a gavel. Neil arrives at noon from O'Hare, finds his mother in the boiler room, the conversation: "I should have answered the email" and "I answered it, Mom, I just answered it with the wrong person." Vanessa, before anyone can ask, stands and resigns as treasurer: "I recommended a deferral I believed was prudent. I was wrong about the risk, and I presented the report as smaller than it was." It costs her something and it is the one honest thing she does that day. Dmitri speaks. Isaiah speaks, three sentences. The vote: replacement of Boiler Two, restored service contract, an independent engineer, Esther chair. Special assessment: they take it on the chin. Tolliver last, brief and not kind: he says what he is going to do about Vanessa's reporting to him on the asset side (a new "learning assignment" in operations review under someone else) without raising his voice. Connie is asked to say something and says two sentences about what a boiler is for. Ends: Esther, in the aisle, to Connie: "Dear, you still weren't invited to Christmas. Come to mine."

8. **Overflow, Revisited** — What happened next, ordinary. Christmas Day in Fort Wayne: a neighbor's table; Isaiah's phone call; Neil on December twenty-sixth, alone, on her couch, learning how to bleed a radiator in her cold back bedroom. January: Vanessa's three-line note, honest. March: the new boiler is craned through the sidewalk hatch; Dmitri's new title; Esther chairing; Connie's Saturday class "Boilers for Beginners" at the trades program: twelve women over forty and two teenagers. April: Connie and Vanessa in the empty Gallery, one honest conversation, no hug: "I didn't think you'd come." "I know. That's the part I'd like you to think about." Last lines: the sound a building makes when it is well. Closing question to listeners: when someone you love has told you not to come, but you can hear the sound they can't, do you go? And where are they listening from.
