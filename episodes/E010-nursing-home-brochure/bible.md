# E010 — Story bible

Narrator: Peg, first person, past tense, telling it the following April from her own kitchen table. Calm, dry, exact; a dispatcher's ear (hears the background of every phone call, reads things back, notes the time). Never raises her voice, never sermonizes. Form A with an investigative spine; tone quiet-tense with dry humor. Not a revenge party: consequences arrive through a state office, a notebook and a night aide. No hidden deed, no lawyer reveal, no document read aloud at a party, no grandchild ally.
Signature habit and phrase: "Let me read that back." Dispatchers repeat the address and the problem so nobody misunderstands. She writes the time in the margin of a small spiral notebook (green cover, kept in her cardigan pocket) before she writes anything else.

## Characters (8 named; unnamed extras: the intake nurse/director of nursing, other residents, a waiter, a state surveyor, a bank clerk)
**Margaret "Peg" Okerlund** — narrator, 70 (born October 3, 1956). Widow of Hank (a highway-department grader operator, died four years ago, heart, at sixty-nine). Thirty-six years a 911 dispatcher at the Gunn Mesa County Communications Center, Alder Bench, Colorado (fictional town and county, high-country valley, aspens, cattle, first snow in October); retired at sixty-four. Known on the radio as "Dispatch Four." Calm low voice that people on the worst night of their lives could follow. Short silver hair, reading glasses pushed up on her head, a green cardigan with a pocket for the notebook. Drives Hank's old tan Ford Ranger. Owns her house outright, openly: a small stone-and-frame house on Willow Bench Road (about four hundred eighty thousand dollars). Income: county pension about three thousand one hundred dollars a month plus Hank's. Savings about two hundred ten thousand dollars. One child, Renata. Her one fault: for four years she let Renata "help" with everything because it was easier than arguing.
**Renata Okerlund-Reyes** — daughter, 44. Runs "Hearthstone Senior Advisors," a one-woman "placement advisor" business in Grand Junction-area strip mall (just say "in the valley"). Fixer, organized, brisk, says "Mom, I'm just trying to get ahead of it" and "I know what's best here." Not cruel; frightened and in debt: her husband's trucking company owes a bank one hundred ninety thousand dollars, balloon due December thirty-first. She is paid a referral fee by facilities (seven thousand five hundred dollars per memory-care move-in at Silverleaf, four thousand for assisted living), which she genuinely believes is how the system works. Has Peg's old debit card (Peg gave it to her two years ago for grocery pickup and prescriptions) and used it to pay the two thousand five hundred dollar "reservation deposit." Her husband (unnamed, "Renata's husband") is a large, quiet man who stares at his hands.
**Gideon Pratt** — Silverleaf Terrace admissions director, 49. Soft vest with the company logo, whitened smile, a lanyard, a sales voice that calls everything "home." Calls Peg "Miss Peggy." His bonus is tied to filling Birch Court (the locked memory-care wing, thirty beds, nineteen filled, owner's lender wants twenty-six by the end of December). He gives the intake screening himself ("I'm certified") and writes lower scores, then has the nurse's signature stamped on later. The real predator. Never shouts; says "let's not make this about paperwork."
**Imelda Ortiz** — night aide on Birch Court, 52. Eleven years at Silverleaf, one aide for twenty-four residents after ten p.m. Short, strong, pink scrubs with cartoon cats, a rosary in her pocket, bad knees, fast hands. Tired and honest. Has watched other residents arrive sharp and fade in a locked wing. Afraid for her job; tells anyway.
**Cyril Abernathy** — resident, 91. Retired county surveyor, in Birch Court "for wandering" (he walked to the road to check a survey marker). Sharp as a tack, hearing aid, a plaid blanket, a bag of surveyor's pencils, speaks in measurements ("it's forty paces to the door and nobody's ever opened it for me"). Keeps a log of the building by pacing it. Falls at 2:40 a.m. in her first week; the fall is not reported.
**Lu Banks** — Peg's oldest friend, 73. Retired dispatcher (Peg trained her, in 1984). Big laugh, smoker's voice she's quit, a house full of cats, a spare room. Peg's sounding board and driver.
**Odette Watanabe** — volunteer coordinator for the regional office of the state Long-Term Care Ombudsman program, 58, retired high-school counselor. Round glasses, a canvas tote of leaflets, hands you a granola bar before she asks a question. Explains in general terms: residents have rights to see their own records, to refuse to be moved, to complain without retaliation; the ombudsman advocates for residents and can bring complaints to the state health department, which can inspect unannounced and restrict admissions. Not a lawyer, not law enforcement.
**Hank Okerlund** — late husband (mentioned only): sang off-key to the radio, said "write it down, Peg, you'll forget the good parts too."

## Money
Respite: two hundred fifty dollars a night. Birch Court: fourteen thousand dollars entrance fee, seven thousand nine hundred dollars a month. Referral fee: seven thousand five hundred dollars. Renata's husband's balloon: one hundred ninety thousand dollars due December thirty-first. Deposit: two thousand five hundred dollars, returned.

## The screening (use plainly, no brand names)
A thirty-point memory screening: ten minutes of questions, drawing a clock, remembering five words, naming animals. Peg's real result: twenty-nine of thirty. The form: nineteen of thirty, "moderate concern, recommend secured memory care." Peg's notebook: start 10:12, end 10:31, her answers, the five words (lantern, spoon, river, saddle, ash), the clock she drew, and the line "G.P. turned the form over before he scored it."

## Ending state
Peg's house stays hers. Renata repays nothing to Peg but the two thousand five hundred dollars comes back from Silverleaf's refund. Renata contacts every family she placed at Silverleaf. Condition for Peg's forgiveness: Renata comes on Thursdays and sits with the residents. Conditional forgiveness, not a hug.
