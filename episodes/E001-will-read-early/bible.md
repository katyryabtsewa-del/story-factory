# E001 — Story bible

Present day of the story: September to December of this year; epilogue the following June. The story is told by Diane looking back from that June.

## Characters (8 named — do not add more named characters)

**Diane Whitaker** — narrator, 69. Widow. Born and raised in Harlan Creek, Ohio. With her husband she ran the Blue Kettle, a diner on Main Street, for thirty-one years; they sold it eight years ago. Famous in town for her pies (sour cherry, buttermilk, apple with a cheddar crust). Calm, observant, dry humor, slow to anger and impossible to fool. Speaks plainly, short sentences when she is angry. Habit: when she is thinking, she wipes the counter that is already clean. Wears her reading glasses on a beaded chain Olivia made her. *(Added in ch. 2:)* Her late parents and grandmother stay unnamed: her father bought the sapphire ring in nineteen fifty-one; her grandmother pieced a quilt and left the vinegar pie recipe.

**Walter Whitaker** — her late husband, died fourteen months before the story begins (heart attack, in July of last year) at seventy-two. Carpenter's hands, ran the grill at the Blue Kettle, quiet and careful, wrote everything down. Built the kitchen table himself in nineteen eighty-four. Fished every Saturday with Harold.

**Kevin Whitaker** — eldest son, 44. Sales manager at a car dealership in Columbus. Big house in a Columbus suburb, a boat he cannot afford, credit card debt, a bonus his employer clawed back. About eighty-five thousand dollars in debt that nobody in the family knows about. Charming in public, impatient in private, calls his mother "Mom" in a soothing voice when he wants something. Leader of the plan. *(Added in ch. 5–8:)* Knew the date of Diane's checkup from the calendar on her refrigerator (Brittany spent three Saturdays in that kitchen). Named the group chat "Operation Sunset" (Olivia: "He names everything like it's a sales contest."). Assumes the trust says "three children, three equal shares"; has never read it. Called Diane's Thanksgiving idea "really mature of you."

**Brittany Whitaker** — Kevin's wife, 42. Former pharmaceutical sales rep, now "between things." Polished, organized, status-hungry, speaks in a bright, sing-song voice and calls Diane "Mama Di," which Diane hates. Made the spreadsheet. The real engine of the scheme. *(Added in ch. 2:)* Married to Kevin for seventeen years; had never once driven down to see Diane on her own before the "declutter" Saturdays.

**Rachel Whitaker** — middle child, 41. Dental office manager in Indianapolis, divorced last year, one son (ten years old, unnamed, lives with her). Anxious, avoids conflict, wants approval from her older brother. Went along with the plan for the money and the jewelry; uneasy about it. Partly redeemed at the end. *(Added in ch. 5:)* Her son is learning the trumpet. When she nearly confesses she says, "Never mind. It's nothing. I'm just tired." The work wedding has already happened by October 29; she says she "keeps forgetting to mail" the ring and will bring it at Thanksgiving. *(Added in ch. 8:)* Her son spends this Thanksgiving with his father, so Rachel comes alone. Diane's last words to her before Thanksgiving: "don't forget your grandmother's ring." She calls Harold "Mr. Greene."

**Tommy Whitaker** — youngest son, 36. Mechanic, owns a small two-bay repair shop in Harlan Creek. Lives ten minutes away, comes to Sunday supper every week, fixes the porch and the gutters, drives Diane to appointments. Not married. Gentle, few words, the siblings look down on him ("the mechanic"). Knows nothing about the scheme until Thanksgiving. *(Added in ch. 5:)* Slow to anger like Walter, "but when it comes, it comes all at once"; that is why Diane keeps him out of it. At the October 30 supper he brought rock salt for the porch steps, changed every smoke detector battery, and checked the back door lock twice.

**Olivia Whitaker** — Kevin and Brittany's daughter, 16. Junior in high school. Wants to be a chef. Spends many weekends with Diane baking; Diane is teaching her the Blue Kettle pie recipes. Brave, honest, a little shy. Has the old family iPad that is still signed in to her father's account, which is how she sees the group chat. *(Added in ch. 1–2:)* Kevin and Brittany's only child (this matters for the trust: Kevin's share passes to her alone). Has her driver's license and drives herself down from Columbus. Keeps a recipe notebook and writes down Diane's Blue Kettle stories. In ch. 2 she notices her mother's color-coded dots ("Mom uses these. She color-codes everything.") and goes quiet; this seeds her later decision to show Diane the chat. *(Added in ch. 6:)* On October 30 Kevin and Brittany were in Cincinnati overnight for a dealership awards dinner, so nobody knew she drove down. She uses the old iPad for cooking videos; a message with Diane's name popped up and she opened it. She slept in Kevin's old room, alarm at five, and left at six the next morning with a thermos of cocoa. Diane told her: do not delete anything, do not answer anything, do not tell anybody, put the iPad back, and stop looking. Diane's promise: nobody will find out she showed the chat "unless you decide to tell them yourself." *(Added in ch. 8:)* Diane has told her only that Harold will read some papers at Thanksgiving and that what she showed might come up; Olivia chose to be in the room ("you won't be standing in it alone"). She does not know Kevin's share will pass to her; she learns it at the reading in ch. 10.

**Harold Greene** — 74, estate lawyer in Harlan Creek, semi-retired, Walter's best friend and fishing partner for forty years. Bow tie, unhurried, very dry. Drew up the Whitakers' trust six years ago. Keeper of Walter's sealed letter. *(Added in ch. 1:)* Owns a cabin on the lake north of town; gave Walter a key in nineteen ninety and never asked for it back, so Walter always called it "the cabin." That is why Brittany's spreadsheet lists "the lake cabin": the Whitakers do not own one. Diane "made a note to tell Harold he had been divided up too" (a possible dry callback in ch. 7). *(Added in ch. 7:)* Callback used: "Tell them the roof leaks over the bunk beds, and the mice have a lease." Tall and thin; wore an orange bow tie on Halloween. His coffee "has been terrible for forty years." Uses a yellow legal pad and a fountain pen. Explains the trust as "a big strong box with a lid"; the trustee "holds the key." On being trustee for Olivia: "Mostly Tommy. I don't intend to live forever, but I intend to give it a good try." Calls Diane "Diane"; Rachel and Olivia call him "Mr. Greene."

## Places
- **Harlan Creek, Ohio** — small fictional town, about an hour and a half from Columbus. Main Street, the old Blue Kettle diner building (now a coffee shop), Saint Anne's church, the high school.
- **Diane's house:** 214 Linden Street. Two-story white farmhouse with a wraparound porch, paid off. Big kitchen with Walter's oak table. Value about three hundred forty thousand dollars.
- **Maple Grove Senior Living** — an assisted-living community outside Columbus that Kevin and Brittany want her to move into. *(Added in ch. 3:)* On a four-lane road between a mattress store and a dentist's office; a television-screen fireplace in the lobby; "Chef's Choice, Salisbury Steak"; a one-bedroom facing the parking lot with a microwave and a two-burner cooktop that shuts off after twenty minutes. Rent starts at five thousand two hundred dollars a month, before "levels of care."
- *(Added in ch. 7:)* **Harold's office:** up a stairway above the hardware store on Main Street (the stairs smell of birdseed and fresh-cut keys). One whole wall of photographs of Harold and Walter holding up fish; in the middle, Walter laughing so hard he nearly dropped a bass back into the lake. An old green safe in the corner holds Walter's envelope and, from November 3, the signed originals of the amendment.
- *(Added in ch. 5–8:)* In Diane's house: a calendar on the refrigerator with her appointments; Kevin's old bedroom upstairs, where Olivia sleeps; a drawer by the stove with a stack of old green Blue Kettle order pads with carbon paper.
- *(Added in ch. 4:)* Saint Anne's is four blocks from Diane's house, one left turn. Diane's doctor's office is a low brick building on the edge of town. The old Blue Kettle building is red brick with a big front window; an espresso machine now stands where Walter's grill was.

## Money
- House: about three hundred forty thousand dollars, no mortgage.
- Proceeds from selling the Blue Kettle eight years ago: four hundred ten thousand dollars, invested; now about five hundred twenty thousand.
- Walter's life insurance: two hundred fifty thousand dollars, paid into the trust.
- Total the children think about: "over a million."
- Kevin's hidden debt: about eighty-five thousand dollars. He asks Diane for sixty thousand dollars for a "car wash franchise opportunity."

## The trust (the payoff mechanism)
- **The Whitaker Family Trust**, created six years ago, after Walter's first heart scare. Revocable living trust. House, investments and later the life insurance are inside it. Diane has been sole trustee since Walter died. She can change it at any time.
- Original terms: everything to the three children in equal shares after Diane dies.
- **Amendment signed on November third**, in Harold's office, with a notary and two witnesses from Harold's staff:
  1. **The sentence that matters:** "Any beneficiary who, during my lifetime, attempts to have me declared incompetent, or attempts to take control of my person or my property without my consent, shall be treated for all purposes of this trust as though he or she had died before me."
  2. Under the trust, the share of a child treated as having died before Diane passes to that child's own children. So Kevin's share goes to **Olivia**, held in trust until she is twenty-five, with Harold and later Tommy as trustees.
  3. The house is left to Tommy, with Diane keeping the right to live in it for life.
  4. Fifty thousand dollars set aside now for the **Blue Kettle Scholarship** for Harlan Creek High students who want to cook for a living.
  5. Rachel is not covered by the clause: she never signed, called or filed anything. Her share stays.
- *(Added in ch. 7–8:)* The sentence is on page two of the amendment. Diane signed with the heavy old pen from the Blue Kettle register and initialed beside the sentence; the notary's seal "made a sound like somebody cracking a walnut." Harold's plain-English version (ch. 7): "If any of your children tries to get a judge to call you incompetent, or tries to take charge of you or your things without your say-so, then as far as this trust is concerned, that child died before you did. Not really dead, of course. Dead on paper." The share "goes down one step, to that child's own children." On whether it reaches back to October: "During my lifetime is a long time, Diane. It covers this October as well as next month." Olivia's share is held until twenty-five "so nobody can talk a girl out of it at eighteen." Diane's reason for the house: Kevin wrote that Tommy gets nothing extra. Diane on Rachel: "Going along is wrong, but it isn't what your sentence covers."
- Diane also gets a full cognitive evaluation from her doctor (results: completely normal, "sharper than most of my forty-year-olds") and a written letter from the doctor. This makes any guardianship attempt hopeless. *(Added in ch. 8:)* The test took most of two hours on Friday, November 4 (clock face set at ten past eleven, five words recalled, counting backward from one hundred by sevens). The doctor phoned Diane herself on Wednesday, November 9: "it's completely normal. Better than normal. You are sharper than most of my forty-year-olds." One copy of the signed letter went to Harold; the other is in Diane's purse next to the printout of Brittany's call.

## Evidence against Kevin and Brittany
- The spreadsheet on Brittany's tablet: title "Mom — Exit Plan," columns Item, Value, Who. *(For narration, ch. 1 reads the title as "Mom," then a dash, then "Exit Plan," compared to the dash on a headstone. Keep that wording; never put the dash symbol in the script.)*
- *(Added in ch. 2:)* The colored sticker dots (yard-sale price stickers): blue is Kevin (twenty-six dots: china hutch, grandfather clock, and more), green is Rachel (eleven: jewelry box, sewing machine, Diane's grandmother's quilt), yellow is Tommy (four, all in the garage: lawn mower, chest freezer, Walter's workbench). One blue dot sits on the underside of Walter's table, right beside the name and year Walter burned into the wood. Diane left it in place on purpose. Brittany's cover story: "my inventory system, for insurance." Diane logged every dot, room by room, in one of Walter's spiral notebooks.
- *(Added in ch. 3:)* The draft listing the realtor left under the fruit bowl: a printed page with a photo of the house from the street (red geraniums on the porch rail), "Two fourteen Linden Street. Charming farmhouse, great bones.", "Coming Soon," and "Spring." Diane keeps it in her recipe box, filed under S, between sour cherry and sweet potato.
- *(Added in ch. 4:)* The doctor's office phone message, dated the morning after the Maple Grove tour: daughter-in-law Brittany Whitaker called; patient leaving the stove on; patient got lost driving to church; "Family is worried about patient living alone." At Diane's request the doctor printed it and signed the bottom; Diane keeps it in her purse. The cognitive evaluation is scheduled for the first week of November (results in the second week, as in the timeline).
- *(Added in ch. 4:)* Diane's own record: a green spiral notebook bought at the drugstore after the doctor visit, logging everything since September fourteenth.
- Brittany's phone call to the doctor's office claiming Diane "leaves the stove on" and "got lost driving to church." The doctor's office wrote it down.
- The Columbus law firm's letter "on behalf of concerned family members," proposing a durable power of attorney or "court-supervised options." *(Added in ch. 5:)* Two pages on heavy paper; mentions "recent incidents" involving her safety; the word "incompetent" is in it. Stapled behind it, a six-page durable power of attorney naming Kevin as agent, with five little red plastic sign-here flags. Diane filed it in her recipe box under C, for Columbus, right behind chicken and dumplings.
- *(Added in ch. 7–8:)* On October 31 Diane laid all of the evidence on Harold's desk. Harold has copies of everything for Thanksgiving; Diane has her originals back (the doctor's printout is in her purse again in ch. 8).
- The family group chat "Operation Sunset" (Kevin, Brittany, Rachel), seen on Olivia's iPad. Key messages:
  - Brittany: "Once she's at Maple Grove we list the house in March. Spring market."
  - Kevin: "Tommy gets nothing extra. He's had enough free dinners."
  - Brittany: "The stove story will work. She's 69, nobody questions it."
  - Rachel: "I don't feel good about the doctor thing."
  - Brittany, the week before Thanksgiving: "Champagne's on me. Thanksgiving is payday."
  - *(Added in ch. 6, paraphrased only, never quoted:)* The chat starts the week after the memorial lunch; a photo of the grandfather clock asking what it might bring; a long back and forth about the Columbus law firm and who pays its fee; Maple Grove again and again. Nobody answered Rachel's message for most of an hour; then Kevin told her to relax, that it was "for Mom's own good," Brittany sent a smiling-face picture, and Rachel wrote nothing more that night. The members are Kevin, Brittany and Rachel.
  - *(Added in ch. 6:)* Diane photographed every message with her own phone (forty-one photos), not screenshots, so nothing was saved on Kevin's iPad. *(Ch. 8:)* On Saturday, November 19, at ten at night, Olivia sent the "Champagne" message as a photo of the iPad screen taken with her own phone, with a note: "I wasn't looking, Grandma, I promise. It popped up while I was watching a pie crust video."

## Walter's letter
Sealed envelope Walter gave Harold six years ago: "For Diane, if the kids ever start circling. Read it to all of them." Content (Harold reads it at Thanksgiving): Walter remembers each child as a boy or girl with love; reminds them their mother fed half the town for thirty-one years and kept the books to the penny; says nothing in this world ever got past her, so if they are hearing this, they already lost; asks them to be the people they were at that kitchen table. Ends: "Diane, the pie is better than mine. It always was."
*(Added in ch. 7:)* A long envelope, yellowed at the edges; the words "For Diane, if the kids ever start circling. Read it to all of them." are written on the front in Walter's square, careful handwriting. Walter gave it to Harold the week the trust was signed and told him he "would know when." Harold showed it to Diane on October 31 without handing it over; she did not open it ("in all our years of marriage I never once opened his mail"), and it went back in the safe.
