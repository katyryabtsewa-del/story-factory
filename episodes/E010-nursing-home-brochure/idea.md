# E010 — idea

**Title (working):** My Daughter Gave Me a Nursing Home Brochure for My 70th Birthday. I Moved In for One Week and Wrote Down Everything.

Length: 60 min

**Logline:** Peg Okerlund, 70, spent thirty-six years as a county 911 dispatcher in the Colorado high country, the calm voice at three in the morning. At her birthday dinner her daughter Dana, a "senior placement advisor," hands her a brochure for Silverleaf Terrace with a deposit already paid. Peg does not argue. She says she'd like to see the place properly, and asks for the one-week respite stay the brochure advertises. A dispatcher's habits go with her: a pocket notebook, times for everything, and the read-back.

**Variety plan (idea 1, rewritten per variety.md п.7; moved to the back of the queue):** NO hidden ownership (her house is openly hers; nobody "forgot" a document); NO grandchild ally; NO lawyer reveal; NO document read aloud at a party. Heroine a retired 911 dispatcher (new profession), Colorado in October (new state/season), villain is a daughter plus an admissions director (not son/daughter-in-law), mechanism is an undercover week + altered screening form + night-shift aide + state ombudsman (new). Form A with an investigative spine, quiet tense tone with dry humor; ending: payback on the predator, conditional forgiveness for the daughter, Peg's new life as a volunteer ombudsman.

**Villain:** Dana (44) is not a cartoon: a fixer in debt (her husband's trucking business), paid a referral fee for every memory-care move-in, convinced she is "helping." The real predator is Gideon Pratt, Silverleaf's admissions director, whose bonus depends on filling an empty memory-care wing and who "adjusts" screening scores.

**Mechanism:** Peg's cognitive screening at intake takes nineteen minutes and she answers all but one question; the form filed says nineteen out of thirty and carries the signature of a nurse who was not in the room. Peg's notebook has start and end times, the questions, her answers. A night aide (Imelda) has seen the same trick on other residents. A volunteer ombudsman coordinator (Fern) knows exactly which state office takes it from there.

**Ending:** Silverleaf's memory wing is put on an admissions hold, Gideon is gone, Dana's firm loses its contracts. Dana comes to Peg's kitchen without her paperwork. Peg offers one condition and one Thursday: sit with the people on the other side of that brochure. Peg becomes a certified volunteer ombudsman; the last scene is the first Thursday.
