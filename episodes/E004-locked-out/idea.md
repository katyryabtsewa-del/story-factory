# E004 — idea

**Title:** My Stepdaughter Changed the Locks During My Husband's Funeral. She Forgot About the Safe Deposit Box.

Length: 70 min

**Logline:** On the afternoon of her husband's funeral, 68-year-old Loretta Kessler, a retired court reporter in a lake-effect snow town in Pennsylvania, comes home from the cemetery lunch to find a locksmith's van in the driveway, new locks on her door, and her stepdaughter Tessa on the porch announcing that the house is "Dad's, and now mine." In Ray's last coat she finds a small brass key with a number stamped on it that nobody can explain. The safe deposit box it opens holds the real reason Tessa was in such a hurry: a forged home-equity loan, Ray's quiet record of it, and a choice he left to Loretta.

- **Form B (mystery):** opens on the locks and the unexplained key; each chapter answers one question (what is the key, why the hurry, what did Ray know, why did he keep silent).
- **Villain:** stepdaughter Tessa Kessler (41), owner of a failing pet-boarding business, in debt to a private lender. Not a monster: frightened, proud, resentful of a stepmother she never gave a chance. Second pressure: lender's man Dale Voss.
- **Humiliation:** locks changed during the funeral; belongings bagged on the porch in the snow; whispers at church that Loretta "worked on a dying man"; the house "sold on paper" before the will is even read.
- **Payoff mechanism:** the safe deposit box (Ray's notebook, copies of the forged home-equity paperwork with a signature a locksmith knew was not his, his acknowledgement signed by Tessa, two sealed letters) + the house is held jointly by the married couple, so a loan signed by one "owner" was never valid. The confrontation is a formal "on the record" meeting: Loretta takes it down on her steno machine, openly, as she did for thirty-three years. The lender's man talks himself into a corner.
- **Ending: partial reconciliation / bittersweet.** No jail, no triumph. Loretta gives Tessa the chance Ray wanted her to have, on written terms. Tessa cuts the new key for the front door herself.
- **Allies:** neighbor-friend Winnie Gault, bank branch manager Cecilia Rowe, Ray's young shop apprentice Gus Pelletier. No grandchild-ally, no lawyer reveal at a party.
- **Variety:** different villain (stepdaughter, plus an outside lender), Pennsylvania winter, funeral and locks, tone bittersweet, partial reconciliation, heroine a retired court reporter (not a cook), form B.
