# E005 — Story bible

Story year unstated; use relative time only. Vera tells it looking back from the following September, as the first pears come in. Form D (one day, real time): the present action is ONE Tuesday morning in late March, from nine o'clock until about half past eleven (verdict at eleven twenty-one by Vera's watch, seventeen past by the clock), in Courtroom Two of the Tamarack County Courthouse. Everything else is flashback woven in (the five weeks before), always returning to the courtroom clock. Tone: tense, with Vera's dry, patient humor. NO party, NO documents read aloud at a celebration, NO lawyer-reveal ending, NO hidden-ownership trick. The orchard is openly Vera's and everybody knows it.

## Characters (8 named. Do not add more named characters)

**Vera Halvorsen** — narrator, 69. Born and raised in Tamarack Flats, Oregon (fictional town in the pear country of southern Oregon, dry hills, river valley, volcano in the distance). Drove a school bus for the Tamarack Flats school district for thirty-seven years (retired at sixty-three, six years ago). Counts heads twice, on and off the bus, and still counts everything: stairs, cars, people in a room, stakes in a field. Calm, slow-spoken, observant, dry humor ("I once drove forty-one third graders through a hailstorm with a substitute who did not know where the heater was"). Never raises her voice. Silver-white hair pinned up in a braid, round tortoiseshell glasses, canvas barn jacket, wears her late husband's wristwatch (too big, on the tightest hole). Knew three generations of local children by name. Widow of Lloyd, six years. Only one child, Brent. Owns Halvorsen Pear Farm: twenty-four acres of Bartlett, Bosc and Comice pears and a roadside farm stand, in her own name since Lloyd's death, openly and in the county records (no secret).

**Lloyd Halvorsen** — late husband, died six years ago of a stroke at seventy. Orchardist, third generation. Quiet, loved the trees, said "you don't own an orchard, you borrow it from the next fellow." (Mention once or twice only; he is not a plot device, no letters, no hidden papers.)

**Brent Halvorsen** — son, 44. Regional manager at Larch Hollow Land Brokers in Camas Junction (fictional), an hour away. Good suits, sandy thinning hair, quarter-zip pullover under a blazer, a quick smile he uses like a tool. Talks about "unlocking value." Not a monster: ashamed of what he's done and trying to make the lie fit. Calls his mother "Mom" and pats her hand when others are watching. Signed an option agreement with Copperleaf Homes six months ago, in which he promised the developer access to the back acres for surveying and "authority to deliver" his mother's land, in exchange for a sixty thousand dollar advance on a finder's fee plus a two percent fee at closing. Vera never agreed to sell. When she refused him twice (the second time at Christmas, flat and kind), he decided the problem was that she could not be trusted to decide.

**Tucker Rand** — 48, land acquisition director for Copperleaf Homes, a regional developer. Soft voice, pressed khakis, a fleece vest with the company logo, shiny dentist smile, calls everything "a timing matter." Sits in the back row of the gallery "as an interested party", wears a tie to court. The surveyors who set the stakes were hired by his company. He does not testify.

**Pearl Dunbar** — 71, Vera's neighbor of forty years and friend since the school bus days (Pearl's kids rode it). Runs the neighboring farm stand (honey, jam, eggs). Widow of a plumber, drives a mint-green pickup with a dented tailgate, a loud whisper, a purse the size of a feed sack with peppermints in it. Comes to court in a church dress and tennis shoes.

**Danny Ocampo** — 36, Tamarack County sheriff's deputy. Rode Vera's bus from kindergarten to eighth grade (she remembers he was the boy who never lost a mitten and always said thank you). Respectful, steady, a little bashful; still calls her "Miss Vera." Took five of the six calls, found the truck gone each time, wrote it all down honestly. Testifies briefly, answers only what he's asked.

**Imani Okafor** — 41, court visitor, a social worker appointed by the court to investigate the petition. Calm, still, brown cardigan, a leather folio, a reading voice like warm water. Shows up at the farm without notice one week before the hearing, stays ninety minutes, drinks the tea, asks Vera to walk the back acres with her. Gives nothing away. Her written report to the judge says Vera is capable and notes forty-one orange survey stakes along the north boundary of the farm and fresh tire ruts in the dry grass.

**Judge Eleanor Marsh** — 63, circuit court judge presiding over the probate hearing. Reading glasses on a cord, dry, short, fair. Does not like surprises or long speeches. Asks the one question.

## Places and things
- **Tamarack Flats, Oregon** — fictional town; population about five thousand. Never describe real places. County seat: also fictional, "Tamarack County," courthouse a brick building with a clock that runs four minutes slow.
- **Courtroom Two** — oak benches, a flag, a heater that ticks, high windows, a gallery of about fourteen people today. Vera counts: she counts fourteen in the gallery (Pearl, Tucker Rand, church ladies, three farmers, others) and eleven steps from the door to the front bench.
- **Halvorsen Pear Farm**, 2400 Orchard Hollow Road (never give a real number system; say "the old farmhouse at the end of Orchard Hollow Road"). Twenty-four acres, a white farmhouse with a deep porch, a red barn, a roadside stand with a hand-painted sign. The back (north) acres along a ridge, rows of old Bosc trees.
- **The stakes:** forty-one orange survey stakes with pink ribbon, driven along the north boundary and into the back rows, none placed by Vera or Lloyd.
- **The truck:** a white pickup with a ladder rack and a magnetic sign peeled off (a ghost of the outline on the door). Plate: the first letters K, T, R and ending in seven four one (Vera says: "a plate beginning K, T, R and ending seven four one"). The truck belongs to Basalt Line Surveying, hired by Copperleaf Homes.
- **The sheriff's call log:** six entries on six evenings over three weeks (Monday, Wednesday, Friday for two weeks, starting late February), each logged by the dispatcher as "report of suspicious persons, rural property, caller known to dispatch." Times: six forty-two, six fifty-one, six forty, seven oh five, six forty-eight, six fifty-five p.m. Do not give calendar dates; say "the first evening", "the second evening", "late February".
- **Danny's reports:** he went out five times (the sixth call was taken by another deputy who was late). Found the truck gone every time. Noticed the stakes on his fourth visit and put it in a note: "orange stakes with ribbon north boundary, no known contractor."
- **The petition:** "Petition for Appointment of Guardian and Conservator" filed by Brent three weeks ago, listing: (1) six "delusional" calls to the sheriff about prowlers; (2) a pot left on the stove (one afternoon, she was on the porch with Pearl, it burned out); (3) a missed property tax installment (really: Brent had filed a change-of-address with the post office, rerouting her mail to a postal box in Camas Junction; she learned of it from the postmaster); (4) "wandering" (she walked the north rows at dusk, which she has done for forty years). Asks the court to appoint Brent as conservator of her estate with power to sell real property, and to place her in "supported living."
- **The option agreement:** between Brent and Copperleaf Homes, signed six months ago; it gives Copperleaf Homes access for "land surveys, soil testing and site planning" on the north acres and a right to buy all twenty-four acres at a price of one million four hundred thousand dollars "upon proper authority being delivered." Sixty thousand dollar advance to Brent, already paid, spent in part on paying off his own credit cards and a car loan.
- **The farm money:** the farm stand and fruit contracts bring in about one hundred forty thousand a year, taxes and wages leave her around forty thousand; her savings about eighty thousand dollars. Fine, not rich.
- **The land trust:** Rogue Basin Land Trust (fictional), a small local nonprofit; epilogue: Vera signs a conservation agreement for the orchard (cannot be developed, ever, stays farmed). Her choice.
- **Brent's voicemail:** left the Sunday after the hearing and again two weeks later; she saves them and has not called back. (Open ending: she holds the phone, the porch light, the pears ripening, a decision for tomorrow.)

## Legal truth in general terms (do not go beyond)
- A person may ask the court to appoint a guardian or conservator for someone said to be unable to manage their affairs. The court appoints a lawyer for the person (the respondent), sends a court visitor to investigate, and requires strong evidence ("clear and convincing") that the person cannot manage on their own and that no lesser step would do.
- The judge may question anyone in the courtroom. Vera is allowed to speak.
- The judge asks Brent one question while he is on the stand (re-called): "Mister Halvorsen, did you or anyone acting for you give those men permission to enter your mother's land?" If yes: he admits he gave away access to land that isn't his and that her calls were not delusions. If no: someone trespassed, and her calls were true. Either answer ends the petition. He tries a third: "It was a timing matter, Your Honor," and the judge: "That is not an answer to my question." He says yes. Then the judge: "Then your mother has been telling the truth about her own back acres since late February."
- The petition is denied (dismissed). The judge also says she'll send the transcript to the county's civil authorities as appropriate, one flat sentence, no drama. No arrests in the courtroom, no police scene.
- Vera chooses not to pursue anything against Brent beyond the dismissal; the option agreement is unenforceable (he never had authority) and Copperleaf Homes withdraws within the week (one line).
