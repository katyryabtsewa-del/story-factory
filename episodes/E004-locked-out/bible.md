# E004 — Story bible

Story year unstated. Loretta tells the story the following June, looking back. Events run from Saturday, January eleventh (funeral) to the end of February; epilogue in June. Use relative time only, never state the year.
Form B (mystery): the key is introduced in chapter 1 and every chapter answers one question. Tone: bittersweet, dry humor from Loretta, tension in the middle, warm quiet ending. NO celebratory scene, NO party reveal, NO lawyer reading documents aloud.

## Characters (7 named. Do not add more named characters)

**Loretta Kessler** — narrator, 68. Widow of eleven days at the start. Born and raised in Port Alder, Pennsylvania. Official court reporter (stenographer) at the Port Alder County Court of Common Pleas for thirty-three years; retired at sixty-three. Still owns her steno machine (a Stenograph with a cracked leg strap she has never replaced). Habits from the job: writes everything down, never interrupts, notices exactly what people say and what they leave out, says "let the record show" half as a joke. Dry, precise, unhurried, funny without trying. Tends to answer rudeness with perfect politeness. Widowed first at forty-one (first husband, a mailman, Dennis, died of cancer; unnamed in the plot beyond "my first husband"). No children of her own. Married Ray nineteen years ago.

**Raymond "Ray" Kessler** — late husband, died eleven days before the funeral, at seventy-four, of heart failure after a short hospital stay (do not dwell). Locksmith for forty-six years, owner of Kessler Lock and Key on Canal Street, the only shop in Port Alder that still cut keys by hand. Big quiet man with a brass key ring on his belt, hummed hymns out of tune, wrote notes on the back of key tags, never said much and meant all of it. Widowed once (Tessa's mother, Joan, died when Tessa was nineteen). Met Loretta when he re-keyed every courtroom door in the courthouse; she was the only person who stayed to watch him work. Made her a brass key for their first anniversary stamped with a tiny L.

**Tessa Kessler** — stepdaughter, 41. Ray's only child. Owns Happy Tails Resort, a pet boarding and grooming business in a rented building on Route 20 outside Port Alder, expanded too fast (second building, a fancy "spa wing") and borrowed from a private lender when the bank said no. Sharp, tired, proud, brittle; talks fast; calls Loretta "Loretta" never "Lori" and never "Mom". Was twenty-two when her father remarried, two years after her mother's death; decided at once that Loretta was replacing Joan, and never let it go. Kept Loretta out of family photos, called her "Dad's wife." Not a monster: ashamed, cornered, and sure she will lose everything her father built. Taught as a girl by Ray to cut keys; hands remember it.

**Dale Voss** — 50, "senior funding partner" at Lakeshore Bridge Funding, a private lender in a glass office park near the highway. Soft voice, expensive coat, never raises it, calls everything "a timing issue." Lent Tessa money against her father's house without checking who actually signed. Walks into the trap by being casual about it.

**Winifred "Winnie" Gault** — 70, Loretta's neighbor across the street and best friend of twenty-six years. Retired school secretary, flowered snow boots, a thermos of tea in her car at all times, loud whisper. Takes Loretta in; sits in the car outside the bank; arrives with an extension cord and opinions.

**Cecilia Rowe** — 54, branch manager of Merrow Valley Bank on Water Street. Neat, careful, unflappable; has seen every kind of family. Follows the rules to the letter, which turns out to be exactly what helps Loretta. Explains the box rules and, later, the bank's fraud process in plain words.

**Gus Pelletier** — 27, Ray's apprentice and the only employee of Kessler Lock and Key. Tall, anxious, ears red in the cold, loves the work. Ray had told him once, in a different context, "if anything happens, the box key is on my coat." Knows Ray's handwriting and Ray's key blanks. Realizes the lock Tessa had installed was put in by someone who "doesn't know a deadbolt from a doorknob" (a cheap builder-grade lock, installed backwards, by a man Tessa found online).

## Unnamed people

- Pastor at the funeral, a woman.

- The notary at Lakeshore (a young woman who stamped the forged papers without looking up).
- Church ladies who whisper (a group).
- Tessa's employee at Happy Tails, a teenager who feeds the dogs.

## Places and things
- **Port Alder, Pennsylvania** — fictional small city on Lake Erie with lake-effect snow. Streets: Canal Street (Ray's shop), Birchwood Lane (their house), Water Street (bank). Never describe real places.
- **The house, 18 Birchwood Lane** — white two-story with green shutters, deep front porch, a brass mail slot Ray installed. Bought by Ray and his first wife; after the wedding nineteen years ago, Ray and Loretta retitled it jointly as a married couple (husband and wife as one owner, "tenants by the entirety"). Mortgage paid off eleven years ago. Value about three hundred ten thousand dollars. Loretta's name has been on the deed for nineteen years; Tessa never knew or never checked.
- **Kessler Lock and Key**, Canal Street: key-cutting machines, a wall of key blanks on pegs, a bell on the door, the cat that sleeps on the safe.
- **Merrow Valley Bank, Water Street branch:** old marble floor, a vault with a steel door; the safe deposit boxes in the back. Box number four hundred seventeen.
- **The brass key:** small, flat, stamped 417, tied to a key tag in Ray's handwriting that says only "L. when you need it." Found by Loretta in the lining of Ray's good funeral overcoat (the pocket the funeral director did not check), the coat she holds on her lap at the cemetery.
- **Loretta's steno machine** and the case it lives in. She carries it into the meeting.
- **Lakeshore Bridge Funding,** office park off the highway, glass doors, a bowl of mints.

## Money (keep exactly)
- Home-equity loan taken out in Ray's name against the house, ten months ago: one hundred eighty thousand dollars. Lakeshore's rate is steep; the balance with fees and interest is now one hundred ninety-one thousand dollars. Lakeshore sent a "notice of default" letter to the house, and Tessa has been intercepting the mail.
- Tessa used the money: ninety-five thousand on the second building and the spa wing, forty-eight thousand to pay off a previous private loan, thirty-seven thousand on payroll and rent to keep Happy Tails open.
- Ray's own repayments after he found out: thirty-one thousand from the shop savings across seven months, in small payments the lender accepted without question.
- The shop and its inventory: Ray's will, in the box, leaves it to Tessa (and gives Gus a first right to buy in or manage). Valued at about ninety thousand dollars.
- Loretta's own money: a modest pension and savings (about sixty-two thousand dollars). Ray never touched it.

## Legal truth in general terms (do not go beyond)
- The house is owned jointly by Ray and Loretta as married co-owners, so when Ray died it became hers entirely, by survivorship, without any will. Tessa had no right to change the locks.
- A loan against the house needed both owners' signatures. Loretta never signed anything. Ray's signature on the paperwork was forged: the loop of the R was wrong, and he never signed in blue ink. The notary at Lakeshore stamped it without checking identification. The loan is therefore not enforceable against the house; Lakeshore's real problem is that it has no valid lien and has been lending without verifying identity.
- Cecilia explains in plain words that the bank can open a fraud review, and that Loretta, as the owner, may decide how to proceed with the person who signed.
- Pennsylvania is a two-party consent state. Loretta never secretly records anyone. She announces openly that she is taking down a verbatim record, as a court reporter, and anyone who stays in the room has agreed. Voss stays.
- No courtroom drama, no police scene, no handcuffs. Possible consequences are discussed, not shown.

## Timeline
- Thirty-eight years ago: Ray marries Joan. Tessa born three years later. (Tessa is forty-one now; adjust: Tessa born forty-one years ago; Joan dies twenty-two years ago.)
- Twenty-two years ago: Joan dies, Tessa nineteen.
- Twenty years ago: Ray re-keys the courthouse doors and meets Loretta.
- Nineteen years ago: Ray and Loretta marry; the house retitled in both names.
- Three years ago: Ray rents box 417 at Merrow Valley Bank in both names "for the good papers" (Loretta thought it was the old safe in the shop; she has never seen the key).
- Two years ago: Tessa expands Happy Tails.
- Last March: Tessa goes to Lakeshore after the bank refuses. Signs Ray's name on a home-equity paper in April (ten months ago from the funeral).
- November (two months before the funeral): Lakeshore's default notice arrives at the shop address (Ray's business mail). Ray opens it, drives to Happy Tails, learns the truth. Tessa breaks down. Ray decides to keep it from Loretta, partly for Tessa and partly because of his heart. He pays what he can. He has Tessa sign a one-page acknowledgement of the debt in his own writing. He writes two letters.
- December: Ray is hospitalized with heart failure; dies a few days into January (eleven days before the funeral).
- Saturday, January eleventh: the funeral. Tessa has a locksmith change the locks while the family is at the cemetery lunch.
- Following week: Loretta stays with Winnie. Monday: the bank. The week after: the meeting. Late February: the terms.
- June: epilogue. Loretta's garden, Tessa cuts a new key.

## The box (what is in it, exactly)
1. A cloth-bound notebook, Ray's handwriting, dated from November: what he found, what he paid, who he spoke to. Plain, short entries. Last line: "I should have told Lo the first night. I wanted Tessa to be the one to say it."
2. Photocopies of the forged paperwork from Lakeshore (the loan agreement with "Raymond J. Kessler," blue ink loops wrong) and Lakeshore's default notice.
3. A single page in Ray's hand, "Acknowledgement," signed by Tessa and Ray and dated in November: Tessa admits she signed her father's name and promises to make it right.
4. Ray's will, simple and recent: the house goes where it already is (Loretta, by survivorship, he notes); the shop to Tessa with Gus's right of first refusal; the shop's savings to pay down the debt.
5. A cream envelope: "For Loretta." A second cream envelope: "For Tessa." Both sealed.
6. His brass key ring with the L-stamped duplicate of the house key (Loretta has hers; this is the spare he cut for Tessa when she was nineteen and never gave her).

## Letters (themes only, writer composes)
- To Loretta: apology for the secret, no excuses, a small joke about her red pen, an ask: "Give her one chance I gave her too late. Then do what you think is right and I'll be proud of it."
- To Tessa: private. Loretta never reads it, and we never see its words. After Tessa reads it, we see only her hands and what she does.

## Style notes for the writer
- Loretta's voice: precise, dry, uses courtroom habits as comedy and as armor ("let the record show"). Never self-pitying.
- The key is introduced in the cold open and returned to at every chapter end. Each chapter ends with a question or small discovery.
- Details of the locksmith trade (pin tumblers, bump keys, key blanks, deadbolt backwards) make it feel real: keep them short, in plain words.
- Use "Missus," "Mister," "Doctor," no abbreviations. Numbers and dates in words.
- No cooking subplot. Loretta eats a sandwich and a lot of tea, that is all.
