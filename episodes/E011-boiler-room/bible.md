# E011 — Bible

Form C (double timeline). Present line: the night of Monday December 22 into Tuesday December 23 and the open meeting that afternoon. Past line: how it got here (Connie's trade, her husband Ferris, the November weekend, the email, the board meeting, the "Run of Show"). Tone tense and practical, dry humor. First person, past tense, Connie narrating. NO festive scene: Christmas appears only in the epilogue.

## Heroine
**Constance "Connie" Rudd**, 69. Fort Wayne, Indiana (small brick house on Hessen Street, a vegetable bed, a space heater she doesn't trust). Thirty-four years a stationary engineer (licensed boiler operator) at Saint Brendan's Hospital in Fort Wayne, the first woman in the boiler plant there; retired four years ago as chief operating engineer of the plant. Widow: husband Ferris, a lineman for the electric co-op, died six years ago (a stroke, a Tuesday in March). Straight talker, gruff, funny, patient with machines and short with people who talk past her. She does not cook for a living and says so. Wears a canvas coat with a hospital name patch she never removed, reading glasses on a cord, carries a flashlight and a pipe wrench in the trunk "out of habit." Knows buildings by sound and smell.

## Family
- **Neil Rudd**, 41, her only son, a civil engineer turned project manager at a consulting firm. Kind, conflict-avoidant, defers to Vanessa. Is stuck at O'Hare on the night of December 22 (flight cancelled coming back from a site visit in Minneapolis). His arc: finally says something plain.
- **Vanessa Ames Rudd**, 38, Neil's wife (married nine years). Regional asset manager at Corvane Partners (fictional real-estate firm), treasurer of the Ostrander condo association. Polished, fast, never raises her voice, talks in "optics," "alignment," "deliverables." Her mother grew up poor and she never forgets it, which she'd never say. She believes she's protecting the unit's value and Neil's future. Speech: crisp, complete sentences, "Constance" never "Connie," "with respect."
- No children (Neil and Vanessa have none; do NOT add a grandchild).

## Allies
- **Isaiah Penn**, 61, night doorman at the Ostrander for twenty-two years, ex-Navy. Slow, kind, writes things on the back of his hand. Calls Connie "Mrs. Rudd" even after she tells him not to. In November she shared his coffee and talked boilers with him and talked boilers with him; he took her number. He calls her on December 22.
- **Dmitri Lazar**, 27, the building's only engineer, hired two years ago from a technical college, no mentor, afraid for his job. Competent but alone; the management company cut the service contract. Quiet, polite, says "ma'am." He kept a copy of the engineering report in a drawer.
- **Esther Bloom**, 84, third floor, retired high school chemistry teacher, widowed, uses a cane and an oxygen concentrator (never plug-dependent in this story: she has a portable tank backup), sharp tongue, noticed everything. Chairs the "Seniors' Corner" of the association. Is the one who calls the open meeting.

## Antagonist-adjacent

Arrives home Tuesday morning from his own cancelled flights. His reaction is the "consequence" for Vanessa: he asks what was deferred and why he wasn't told, in front of the neighbors who have just spent the night shivering.

## Place
**The Ostrander**, a 1927 brick courtyard building, six stories, ninety-six apartments, two hundred and ten residents (about forty are over seventy), on Wolcott Avenue on Chicago's North Side (fictional building; keep street name generic: "a side street off Ravenswood"). Steam heat: a two-boiler plant in the basement boiler room, Boiler One (1998 vintage, original to the conversion) and Boiler Two (older, "red-tagged" in October by the insurance inspector). Condensate return tank and feed pump. Low-water cutoff on Boiler One. Riser valves on each wing; the north wing is the coldest. The lobby has a gas fireplace (works without power) and a big party room called "the Gallery" with two couches and a kitchenette. The unit of Neil and Vanessa: 4B (Vanessa: "four-B").

## What had been deferred (keep simple, plausible, "in general terms")
- 2021: a reserve study recommended replacing Boiler Two and the condensate system within five years.
- August this year: an engineering firm's report (the "Ketterman report", twelve pages) said Boiler Two was at end of life and Boiler One carried the full load in a cold snap with no margin, and urged restoring the quarterly service contract (cancelled last January to save about nineteen thousand dollars a year).
- October: the insurance inspector red-tagged Boiler Two.
- Board meeting, Saturday November fifteenth (during Connie's visit): Vanessa, as treasurer, presented a motion to "defer the capital plan to the second quarter pending a refinancing review." Passed four to one (Esther Bloom against). The refinancing review was Vanessa's: a reduced assessment keeps the unit's market value high for the spring listing she and Neil have discussed. Estimated replacement: about three hundred and forty thousand dollars, a special assessment of roughly three thousand five hundred dollars per apartment.
- Vanessa did not hide the report from the board (they had it) but she summarized it as "within acceptable risk" and did not tell Graham.

## Rules for the script
- No "Mrs." or "Mr." abbreviations: write "Missus Rudd," "Mister Tolliver."
- Boiler technicalities in plain words, accurate enough: a low-water cutoff is a safety device that shuts the burner if the water gets too low; a sluggish or sludged float can falsely lock it out; blowing down flushes sediment. Do not invent magic; no hero explosions; the point is patience and skill.
- Names on different letters: Connie, Neil, Vanessa, Isaiah, Dmitri, Esther, Graham, Ferris. (Ferris and Neil/Esther fine; "Ferris" and "Fern" from earlier episodes are gone.)
- No recipes, no cooking scenes.
