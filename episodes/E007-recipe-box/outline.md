# E007 — Outline (60 min, 8 chapters, 1,100–1,200 words each)

Skeleton adapted: cold open (flash-forward) → who she is → three waves → midpoint (the letter) → the quiet walk of the route → the Saturday on the lawn → new life.

1. **Dawn at the Dump** — Cold open: May, rain, 5:40 a.m., Bernie in Walt's rubber boots at the Larkin County transfer station, Del Haskell handing her a rake. A promise in one line ("By Saturday noon thirty people would be standing on my lawn, and not one of them would be there for a house showing"). Back one week: who she is, the route, the green tin box, Walt gone fourteen months, Marnie's first helpful visits. Ends with her opening the kitchen cupboard at 4:55 a.m. and finding the box shelf empty and a dumpster receipt under the magnet.
2. **Clear Space** — The past six months compressed: Marnie's "help" (pantry in October, closet in January), wave one: the dentist day, Bernie returns to a kitchen with matching canisters and labels ("Flour," "Sugar," "Misc."), Walt's coffee mugs gone to the church thrift. Greg: "She means well, Mom." Bernie takes it like a mail carrier in a dog yard. Ends: Marnie smiling, "Next is the workshop."
3. **Brochures and a Label Maker** — Wave two: Marnie lists Walt's tools online; Bernie finds the lathe sold; condo brochures for Fieldstone Terrace appear on her table; dinner in West Des Moines where Greg says "just look at it." Bernie's first firm no, ignored. Marnie tells the story of her mother, almost; Bernie notices something but not enough. Ends: Greg, quietly, on the porch: "I signed a paper so we could talk about it."
4. **The Receipt Under the Magnet** — Wave three, the dumpster: Monday evening the thirty-yard rolloff in the driveway (Bernie assumed it was for the garage), Tuesday she realizes. Back to the dawn at the dump, Del lets her in despite regulations and gives her a tarp; the dig through wet cardboard, a stranger's life; Bernie almost quits; the green tin appears, dented, upside down, lid shut. Ends: she opens it; the cards are soaked.
5. **Card One Hundred Seventeen** — Drying on Del's picnic table; the slit in the lining; the sandwich bag; the letter, quoted in part; the key taped under the lid. Greg arrives in a rain jacket without a word and stands beside her, and neither mentions it; he helps carry the tin. The dots on nineteen cards. Ends: "Go and get the red toolbox before somebody sells it," and the realization the toolbox is on the marketplace and a buyer is coming Wednesday at nine.
6. **The Man from Ames** — Wednesday morning comedy and tension: Bernie at the machine shed, the toolbox already on the lawn with a trailer in the driveway; the buyer, a decent man, hands it back for his forty-dollar deposit and takes the old band saw; inside the box: the ledger, the passbook, eighteen thousand four hundred dollars, the note "Walk the route first." Hilda Tolliver's name at the top of the ledger. Marnie arrives and sees Bernie with the toolbox; the first real fight: calm, cold; Marnie says "You can't live in a museum." Bernie replies about museums and mail routes; Marnie leaves to arrange the open house for Saturday. Ends: Bernie opens the route map.
7. **Walk the Route First** — Thursday and Friday: Bernie drives Route Three with the dotted cards on the dashboard; porch by porch (Hilda's roof, Dewey Marsh's furnace, Ila Fenn's ramp), each one a short scene, each person guessing or learning who helped; she doesn't tell them whose money; she tells them about the box and the house; she doesn't ask them to come, she just says what Saturday is. Humor with a dog. Ends: Friday night, Hilda on the phone: "I'm bringing the crisp. You can't make it, so I will."
8. **Saturday, Ten O'Clock** — Open house morning: Bryce, the stager and the rooster, Marnie with a clipboard; at ten, cars on the gravel: thirty-one people with covered dishes made from the cards. Bernie says one calm sentence to Marnie. Bryce leaves with his sign. Marnie stays on the lawn with a paper plate; the story of her mother and the newspapers; Greg's apology; Bernie's forgiveness is partial and honest. Then: September; the Thursday Route; Bernie's first recipe card, written in Marnie's kitchen, laminated; the tin with its taped lid; the narrator's question for the listener (about throwing things away vs keeping them, and whether to forgive someone who was only afraid).

Hooks at the end of each chapter as above. Each chapter opens with one natural sentence reminding where we are. Keep the transfer-station flash-forward thread in chapters 1, 4, 5 only. Quote Walt's letter once in chapter 5 (and short echoes in 6–8).
