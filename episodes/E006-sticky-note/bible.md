# E006 — Story bible
Narrator: Josie, first person, past tense, looking back from the following spring (April). Warm, dry humor.

## Characters (8 named — do not add more named characters; unnamed extras are fine: a receptionist, the lender's voice on speaker, a nurse)
**Josephine "Josie" Ledbetter** — narrator, 71. Widow (Earl died three years ago in January, at seventy-one). Born and raised in Sparrow Gap, Tennessee (fictional town, seat of fictional Marlow County). Ran Ledbetter Alterations on Depot Street for forty-one years (hems, wedding gowns, letting out prom dresses at ten at night), closed the storefront four years ago, still takes a few jobs in the sunroom at the back of her house. Silver hair in a low bun, reading glasses on a cord, a pincushion on her wrist when she is thinking. Notices what other people miss: stitches, hems, who is lying about a measurement. Dry, patient, polite to the point of being dangerous. Habit: when she is thinking she smooths the fabric (or the tablecloth). Her signature: "Josephine A. Ledbetter" with a tiny back-stitch tick, like a knot, under the last "r". She began it forty years ago, when Earl joked that a seamstress ought to knot her thread. Only Earl knew why. She signs the same way on alterations receipts. Owns her house outright (a brick ranch on Hollis Street, paid off, worth about three hundred forty thousand dollars). Drives a gray Buick. Gets about two thousand one hundred dollars a month from Social Security and Earl's co-op pension.
**Earl Ledbetter** — late husband, a lineman for the Marlow Electric Cooperative for thirty-eight years. Big hands, patient, hummed hymns on ladders, called her "Needle." Joked "a seamstress ought to knot her thread." Fished with friends on Saturdays.
**Nicole Brandt** — daughter, 44. Office manager at a dental practice in town. Married to Curtis for eleven years, no children (one sentence, gently, no detail). Tired, loving, anxious, a fixer. Calls her mother "Mama" when she is scared and "Mom" when she is managing. Believes she is protecting her. Signed the witness line on the forged power of attorney on December second because Curtis said her mother had signed in November and "felt silly about forgetting." She is used, not a villain. Moves out of the marital house at the end and stays a few weeks in Josie's spare room.
**Curtis Brandt** — son-in-law, 47. "Financial adviser" at a small office in Sparrow Gap called Pike and Brandt Financial. Friendly, groomed, a good listener at funerals. Phrase: "Let's get ahead of it." Calls Josie "Josie, hon." Hands you a pen before you have asked for one. Owes a private lender, Marlow Bridge Capital, two hundred twelve thousand dollars from a failed storage-unit investment, balloon due the first of February. Brings the Sunday ham, sets up the pill organizer, forwards her mail. Never raises his voice.
**Gordon Teague** — notary, 58, owner of Teague Title and Notary on the courthouse square. Curtis's golf partner. Soft hands, a bow tie, fidgets with a pen cap. Stamps what he is handed. Folds when pressed.
**Marisol Vega** — 26, Gordon's assistant and receptionist. Married two years ago (wedding dress altered by Josie at the last minute, receipt signed with the tick, kept in her wedding folder). Careful, frightened of losing the job (she is saving for a house), brave anyway. Writes the sticky note. Wears a green lanyard, keeps a spiral notebook.
**Fern Albright** — 73, next-door neighbor for thirty years, quilting circle, blunt, wears a men's barn coat, drives a Ford truck. Drove Josie to cataract surgery on November eighteenth and took the "pirate" photo. Knows Detective Hatch because he married her cousin (the cousin is never named).
**Detective Raymond Hatch** — 54, Marlow County Sheriff's Office, financial crimes. Slow-talking, tall, tidy, keeps a thermos, does the crossword in pen. "Believing is not proving, ma'am."

## Money and places
- House: Hollis Street, Sparrow Gap, Tennessee. Appraised about three hundred forty thousand dollars. No mortgage.
- Forged power of attorney: a "durable power of attorney" naming Curtis as Josie's agent, dated and notarized Tuesday, November eighteenth, witness line signed by Nicole on December second. The signature of "Josephine A. Ledbetter" has no tick.
- Loan: Curtis used the paper to apply (online and by mail) for a home-equity loan of one hundred eighty-five thousand dollars secured by Josie's house, through Harlan Ridge Lending (fictional, unnamed face: a closing coordinator on speakerphone). The lender required the borrower's own signature at closing. Closing set for Friday, January sixteenth, ten o'clock, in the Teague Title conference room.
- Curtis's debt: two hundred twelve thousand dollars to Marlow Bridge Capital, due Sunday, February first. The loan covers most of it; he planned to call the rest "estate planning."
- "Medicare housekeeping": what Curtis calls the stack on Tuesday, January thirteenth.

## Timeline
- Earl dies three years ago in January.
- Mid-November: Josie tells Nicole and Curtis she has cataract surgery coming up. Tuesday, November eighteenth: Josie's cataract surgery (left eye) at Riverbend Eye Surgery Center, one hour away in Cleveland, Tennessee. Check-in at eleven, procedure one-thirty, discharge at three-ten. Fern drove her. Discharge papers with printed times. Fern's phone photo at three-twelve (time-stamped) of Josie in a clear plastic eye shield and sunglasses, Fern's text: "Pirate." Nicole was at work and brought chicken soup the next evening, Wednesday the nineteenth.
- Tuesday, November eighteenth, two-fifteen: the date the forged paper says Josie signed "in the presence of" Gordon. No one named Ledbetter came into the office that day (Marisol was at the front desk all afternoon).
- Thursday, November twentieth: Curtis dictates the paper to Marisol (typed from a template Gordon gave her), Gordon stamps it Friday the twenty-first with a journal entry made later in his own handwriting. Marisol notices there is no signature of Josie in the appointment book and that the journal entry says "personally known."
- Tuesday, December second: Nicole signs the witness line at her own kitchen table.
- Mid-December: Curtis applies for the loan; lender asks for the live closing.
- Early December (before the loan application): Curtis puts Josie's mail on forwarding to "a safer address" (Nicole's house). Then the pill organizer, a remote thermostat gadget, and "memory jokes" at church (first Sunday of January).
- Tuesday, January thirteenth: Josie, Nicole and Curtis at Teague Title. The sticky note. (Story opens here.)
- Wednesday, January fourteenth: Marisol comes to Josie's sunroom with her sister's bridesmaid dress to be hemmed; explains.
- Wednesday–Thursday: Josie and Fern go to Detective Hatch. Josie plays forgetful.
- Thursday evening, January fifteenth: Curtis at Josie's kitchen on speaker with Marlow Bridge. Josie hears it.
- Friday, January sixteenth, ten o'clock: the closing. Hatch in the next room (Marisol let him in through the file room). Josie records on her own phone in her cardigan pocket (Tennessee is a one-party-consent state and she is a party to the conversation; say "in general terms: she was allowed to record a conversation she was part of").
- Late January: Curtis charged (forgery, attempted theft by deception); Gordon's notary commission revoked, he cooperates; loan canceled; Marlow Bridge is Curtis's problem alone. Nicole moves into Josie's spare room on the weekend of January seventeenth.
- April: Josie's Tuesday mending table opens in the church fellowship hall (Sparrow Gap First Methodist). Story told from here.

## Style and motifs
- The tick under the "r": introduced in chapter 2, paid off in chapter 7. Do not over-explain.
- Josie "plays forgetful": dry comedy (loses glasses, calls the Buick "the car", repeats a question). Never cruel to herself.
- The sticky note is yellow, ballpoint, block capitals: DON'T SIGN. THE FIRST ONE WASN'T YOU. SAY YOU NEED YOUR GLASSES. — M. Back of the note: "I'll explain. Come to Hollis Street? No—I'll come to you."
- Curtis's phrase "Let's get ahead of it" — use at most five times.
- No "true story" language. No real company names.

## Additions made while writing (E006 script, revised after editor review)
- Page four of the January thirteenth stack is not initials: it is a paragraph in which Josie ratifies the November eighteenth power of attorney. Curtis's pretext: the supplement insurance company wants the forms notarized, due Friday (no mention of a Medicare window).
- Curtis is described as someone who "told people for a living how to look after their money."
- Josie drove the Buick to the January thirteenth meeting alone; new reading glasses (post-surgery) were in the glove box since December; the old pair is in her purse. Nicole drove her home that day; Curtis returned the Buick that evening. Wednesday evening Curtis takes the keys "for the ice"; Hatch returns them in a plastic bag (ch08).
- Surgery date: originally set for November twenty-fifth, moved up to the eighteenth by a cancellation. Josie told only Nicole and Fern; Nicole forgot to tell Curtis, who was at a seminar in Nashville that week and picked the eighteenth because Gordon's calendar was empty.
- Marisol: typed the paper Thursday, November twentieth (so Josie could not have signed it Tuesday); on Sunday, November twenty-third, saw Gordon writing the back-dated journal entry; photocopied the paper before filing; later photographed the witness line (December second) and sent it to Hatch. Her receipt is from the last of Josie's old receipt books, two years old (June), eighty-five dollars. The forger worked from a copy of a copy (pension form or mail card), so the tick was lost.
- Weather: it rained in Sparrow Gap until five on November eighteenth. Gordon says it was dry; Marisol "mopped the front mat twice." Fern's drugstore print shows the wet Riverbend parking lot, time on phone three-twelve.
- Nicole was told it was "Mom's affairs, tidying up"; she did not know about the loan until the closing.
- Hatch had Marisol and Josie's papers photographed Wednesday; Josie keeps the discharge packet and print in a clear envelope; Marisol gets her receipt back Thursday night.
- Thursday evening Curtis's phone call (unnamed Marlow Bridge man, speaker) is retold by Josie to Hatch ("motive and a date").
- Closing hiccup (ch07): Gordon, rattled, suggests it may have been Wednesday the nineteenth; Josie answers on the spot (patch on her eye, Nicole's soup, Nicole confirms). Lender cancels: application withdrawn, no lien recorded. Curtis's phone shows "Marlow Bridge Capital"; Nicole whispers "You let me sign it."
- Small details: Fern's truck is named Gladys; Hatch once met Earl at Parksville Lake (Earl gave him a worm); Marisol's new house is a yellow house on Pruitt Lane; Nicole stays ten weeks in the spare room and then rents an apartment above the hardware store (end of March); Earl's brass lineman's button (carried three years) goes into the sewing box with the sticky note; the tick on Josie's receipt for Nicole's coat is noticed by Nicole for the first time.
- Chapter 7 is titled "Look Under the R".
