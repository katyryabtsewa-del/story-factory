# E002 — Story bible

Story time: told by Marlene the following spring, looking back. The events run from late June (the party) back through six years of history, to September of this year, with the epilogue the next April.
Present-day year of the story is unstated; use relative years only ("six years ago", "seven years ago").

## Characters (8 named — do not add more named characters)

**Marlene Dunmore** — narrator, 66. Widow. Born in Kalamazoo, Michigan. Worked thirty-four years as a bookkeeper for the Kellerman County school district, retired at sixty-two. Lives in a small brick bungalow on Orchard Row, in Pelham Landing, Michigan, twelve minutes from her son's house. Calm, precise, dry humor, never raises her voice. Habit: writes everything down in green ledger books, in pencil first and then in pen; hums a hymn when counting. Wears reading glasses pushed up in her gray curls. Bakes lemon bars for every occasion, which Cassidy calls "so retro."

**Gerald Dunmore** — her late husband, died seven years ago at sixty-four (a stroke, in March), a high-school shop teacher. Left Marlene his life insurance (one hundred fifty-two thousand dollars) and a pension. Always said, "Family is who shows up with a casserole and a ladder." Appears only in memory (and one note in his handwriting found in the ledger box in ch. 8).

**Trevor Dunmore** — her son, 38. Regional sales manager for a flooring company. Kind to a fault of cowardice: says "Mom, it's fine" and "Cass, it's fine" and nothing ever changes. Had a bad credit hit when a friend's restaurant failed and a loan he co-signed went to collections. Loves Marlene, hates conflict; never told Cassidy how the house was really paid for. Speaks in soft half-sentences, rubs the back of his neck.

**Cassidy Dunmore** — his wife, 36. Runs an online "home styling" business with a lot of followers; the house is her brand, she calls it "our dream home" and #DunmoreHome. Bright, fast-talking, smiles with her teeth, hates being corrected. Believes (because Trevor let her) that Trevor's job and some "family help" bought the house. Starts rewriting history: Marlene is "helpful," never "family."

**Bernadette Vance** — Cassidy's mother, 63. Divorced, sells cosmetics, moves into the basement suite in August. Sweet voice, sharp eyes, always has an opinion about other people's kitchens.

**Jonah Dunmore** — grandson, 8. Gap in his teeth, loves trains and lemon bars, sends Marlene drawings. Calls her "Gran." Never knows more than he should.

**Naomi Feld** — attorney, 52, real estate and elder law, office above a bakery on Water Street in Pelham Landing. Brisk, funny, wears purple glasses, says "Let's read the paper." Marlene's lawyer since Gerald's estate.

**Odessa Wray** — Marlene's friend of thirty years, 68, retired loan officer at the Harbor Light Credit Union. Blunt, big laugh, drives a 1998 Buick. Confirms the numbers, sits beside Marlene at the party.

## Places and things
- **Pelham Landing, Michigan** — fictional small city near Kalamazoo. Streets: Orchard Row (Marlene), Tamarack Court (the house), Water Street (Naomi's office).
- **The house at 41 Tamarack Court** — four bedrooms, brick and gray siding, two-car garage, finished basement with a mother-in-law suite Marlene paid for (so she could stay over "when I'm old"). Purchased six years ago for three hundred twelve thousand dollars.
- **Harbor Light Credit Union** — lender. Marlene's name alone on the mortgage and the deed.
- **The green ledgers** — three green clothbound ledgers, one page per month, every payment, every repair, every dollar.

## Money (keep exactly)
- Purchase price: three hundred twelve thousand dollars. Marlene's down payment: ninety thousand (from Gerald's insurance). Mortgage in Marlene's name: two hundred twenty-two thousand dollars, thirty years.
- Monthly mortgage payment with taxes and insurance: two thousand four hundred dollars.
- Trevor and Cassidy pay Marlene "family rent" of eight hundred dollars a month by transfer, labeled "groceries" — Trevor's idea so Cassidy would not ask. Marlene covers the other one thousand six hundred a month herself.
- Over six years: seventy-two months. Marlene's share of the monthly payments: one hundred fifteen thousand two hundred dollars. Plus the basement suite (twenty-eight thousand), a roof (nineteen thousand five hundred), a furnace (five thousand eight hundred) = fifty-three thousand three hundred. Total beyond what Trevor paid: one hundred sixty-eight thousand five hundred dollars. Say "about one hundred sixty-eight thousand dollars" in dialogue, exact in the ledger.
- Trevor and Cassidy's rent paid over six years: seventy-two times eight hundred = fifty-seven thousand six hundred dollars.
- Current market value of the house: about three hundred ninety thousand dollars. Marlene's mortgage balance: one hundred ninety-eight thousand dollars.
- The refinance trap: Cassidy and Trevor's mortgage broker's paperwork — "cash-out refinance" for two hundred sixty thousand dollars, new loan in Trevor's and Cassidy's names, needing Marlene to sign a quitclaim deed "for the title company, just a formality." Cassidy wants the cash for a home studio business expansion and to pay off her credit cards (about forty-one thousand dollars).
- Marlene's offer in ch. 7: sell the house to Trevor and Cassidy at three hundred sixty thousand dollars (below market, their own mortgage on their own credit, closing in ninety days); if not, she gives written notice and sells to the open market. Trevor's credit has recovered enough to qualify with a cosigner NOT being Marlene.

## Legal truth in general terms (do not go beyond)
- No written lease. Trevor and Cassidy are month-to-month occupants paying a small family rent. A landlord may end that with proper written notice. A quitclaim deed would hand over her ownership with nothing in return. Naomi explains it in two sentences, no jargon.

## Timeline
- Seven years ago March: Gerald dies.
- Six years ago April: Trevor's credit crash; Marlene buys 41 Tamarack Court. Trevor begs her never to tell Cassidy how it was set up ("She's embarrassed enough, Mom"). Marlene agrees "for a year or two."
- Six years of: Sunday dinners, lemon bars, rent labeled "groceries," ledger entries. Cassidy's business grows; the house is her backdrop.
- Last Thanksgiving: Cassidy changes the plan so Marlene "brings the rolls only."
- January: Cassidy changes the garage code and sets up a visiting calendar app for "Grandma time."
- March: Jonah's birthday party held without Marlene ("it was a small thing").
- May: Cassidy's mother Bernadette is told she can move into the basement suite in August.
- June, last Saturday: the "Six Years in Our Dream Home" party.
- July: the refinance papers; Marlene consults Naomi.
- August: Marlene signs nothing, ends the arrangement in writing; Bernadette's move is postponed.
- Early September: the family meeting at Marlene's table; the offer.
- December: closing. Following April: epilogue.
