# E004 — Story bible

Story year unstated. Loretta tells the story the following June, looking back. Events run from Saturday, January eleventh (funeral) to the end of February; epilogue in June. Use relative time only, never state the year.
Form B (mystery): the key is introduced in chapter 1 and every chapter answers one question. Tone: bittersweet, dry humor from Loretta, tension in the middle, warm quiet ending. NO celebratory scene, NO party reveal, NO lawyer reading documents aloud.

## Characters (7 named. Do not add more named characters)

**Loretta Kessler** — narrator, 68. Widow of eleven days at the start. Born and raised in Port Alder, Pennsylvania. Official court reporter (stenographer) at the Port Alder County Court of Common Pleas for thirty-three years; retired at sixty-three. Still owns her steno machine (a Stenograph with a cracked leg strap she has never replaced). Habits from the job: writes everything down, never interrupts, notices exactly what people say and what they leave out, says "let the record show" half as a joke. Dry, precise, unhurried, funny without trying. Tends to answer rudeness with perfect politeness. Widowed first at forty-one (first husband, a mailman, Dennis, died of cancer; unnamed in the plot beyond "my first husband"). No children of her own. Married Ray nineteen years ago.

**Raymond "Ray" Kessler** — late husband, died eleven days before the funeral, at seventy-four, of heart failure after a short hospital stay (do not dwell). Locksmith for forty-six years, owner of Kessler Lock and Key on Canal Street, the only shop in Port Alder that still cut keys by hand. Big quiet man with a brass key ring on his belt, hummed hymns out of tune, wrote notes on the back of key tags, never said much and meant all of it. Widowed once (Tessa's mother, Joan, died when Tessa was nineteen). Met Loretta when he re-keyed every courtroom door in the courthouse; she was the only person who stayed to watch him work. Made her a brass key for their first anniversary stamped with a tiny L.

**Tessa Kessler** — stepdaughter, 41. Ray's only child. Owns Foxglove Pet Lodge, a pet boarding and grooming business in a rented building on Route 20 outside Port Alder, expanded too fast (second building, a fancy "spa wing") and borrowed from a private lender when the bank said no. Sharp, tired, proud, brittle; talks fast; calls Loretta "Loretta" never "Lori" and never "Mom". Was twenty-two when her father remarried, two years after her mother's death; decided at once that Loretta was replacing Joan, and never let it go. Kept Loretta out of family photos, called her "Dad's wife." Not a monster: ashamed, cornered, and sure she will lose everything her father built. Taught as a girl by Ray to cut keys; hands remember it.

**Dale Voss** — 50, "senior funding partner" at Larkmoor Bridge Funding, a private lender in a glass office park near the highway. Soft voice, expensive coat, never raises it, calls everything "a timing issue." Lent Tessa money against her father's house without checking who actually signed. Walks into the trap by being casual about it.

**Winifred "Winnie" Gault** — 70, Loretta's friend of twenty-six years (they met at the courthouse, when Winnie testified as a school records witness and Loretta lent her a handkerchief) and her neighbor across the lane for nineteen of them. Retired school secretary, flowered snow boots, a thermos of tea in her car at all times, loud whisper. Takes Loretta in; sits in the car outside the bank; arrives with an extension cord and opinions.

**Cecilia Rowe** — 54, branch manager of Merrow Valley Bank on Water Street. Neat, careful, unflappable; has seen every kind of family. Follows the rules to the letter, which turns out to be exactly what helps Loretta. Explains the box rules and, later, the bank's fraud process in plain words.

**Gus Pelletier** — 27, Ray's apprentice and the only employee of Kessler Lock and Key. Tall, anxious, ears red in the cold, loves the work. Ray had told him once, in a different context, "if anything happens, the box key is on my coat." Knows Ray's handwriting and Ray's key blanks. Realizes the lock Tessa had installed was put in by someone who "doesn't know a deadbolt from a doorknob" (a nineteen-dollar builder-grade lock Tessa bought herself, screws half an inch too short, put in in a hurry by the chain locksmith, who told her it was cheap and was overruled; a pry bar would take it off in a minute).

## Unnamed people

- Pastor at the funeral, a woman.
- Funeral director, a man in a gray suit, who hands Loretta Ray's belongings in a clear bag.
- The notary at Larkmoor (a young woman who stamped the forged papers without looking up).
- Church ladies who whisper (a group).
- Tessa's employee at Foxglove, a teenager who feeds the dogs.

## Places and things
- **Port Alder, Pennsylvania** — fictional small city on Lake Erie with lake-effect snow. Streets: Canal Street (Ray's shop), Birchwood Lane (their house), Water Street (bank). Never describe real places.
- **The house, 18 Birchwood Lane** — white two-story with green shutters, deep front porch, a brass mail slot Ray installed. Bought by Ray and his first wife; after the wedding nineteen years ago, Ray and Loretta retitled it jointly as a married couple (husband and wife as one owner, "tenants by the entirety"). Mortgage paid off eleven years ago. Value about three hundred ten thousand dollars. Loretta's name has been on the deed for nineteen years; Tessa never knew or never checked.
- **Kessler Lock and Key**, Canal Street: key-cutting machines, a wall of key blanks on pegs, a bell on the door, the cat that sleeps on the safe.
- **Merrow Valley Bank, Water Street branch:** old marble floor, a vault with a steel door; the safe deposit boxes in the back. Box number four hundred seventeen.
- **The brass key:** small, flat, stamped 417, tied to a key tag in Ray's handwriting that says only "L. when you need it." Found by Loretta in the lining of Ray's good funeral overcoat (the pocket the funeral director did not check), the coat she holds on her lap at the cemetery.
- **Loretta's steno machine** and the case it lives in. She carries it into the meeting.
- **Larkmoor Bridge Funding,** office park off the highway, glass doors, a bowl of mints.

## Money (keep exactly)
- Home-equity loan taken out in Ray's name against the house, ten months ago: one hundred eighty thousand dollars. Larkmoor's rate is steep; the balance with fees and interest is now one hundred ninety-one thousand dollars. Larkmoor sent a "notice of default" letter to the house, and Tessa has been intercepting the mail.
- Tessa used the money: ninety-five thousand on the second building and the spa wing, forty-eight thousand to pay off a previous private loan, thirty-seven thousand on payroll and rent to keep Foxglove open.
- Ray's own repayments after he found out: thirty-one thousand from the shop savings across seven weeks (first week of November to December twenty-third, the day before the ambulance), in payments of three or four thousand that the lender accepted without question. (Changed from "seven months": Ray learned of the loan only in November.)
- The shop and its inventory: Ray's will, in the box, leaves it to Tessa (and gives Gus a first right to buy in or manage). Valued at about ninety thousand dollars.
- Loretta's own money: a modest pension and savings (about sixty-two thousand dollars). Ray never touched it.

## Legal truth in general terms (do not go beyond)
- The house is owned jointly by Ray and Loretta as married co-owners, so when Ray died it became hers entirely, by survivorship, without any will. Tessa had no right to change the locks.
- A loan against the house needed both owners' signatures. Loretta never signed anything. Ray's signature on the paperwork was forged: the loop of the R was wrong, and he never signed in blue ink. The notary at Larkmoor stamped it without checking identification. The loan is therefore not enforceable against the house; Larkmoor's real problem is that it has no valid lien and has been lending without verifying identity.
- Cecilia explains in plain words that the bank can open a fraud review, and that Loretta, as the owner, may decide how to proceed with the person who signed.
- Pennsylvania is a two-party consent state. Loretta never secretly records anyone. She announces openly that she is taking down a verbatim record, as a court reporter, and anyone who stays in the room has agreed. Voss stays.
- No courtroom drama, no police scene, no handcuffs. Possible consequences are discussed, not shown.

## Timeline
- Forty-one years ago: Tessa is born to Ray and Joan.
- Twenty-two years ago: Joan dies, Tessa nineteen.
- Twenty years ago: Ray re-keys the courthouse doors and meets Loretta.
- Nineteen years ago: Ray and Loretta marry; the house retitled in both names.
- Three years ago: Ray rents box 417 at Merrow Valley Bank in both names "for the good papers" (Loretta thought it was the old safe in the shop; she has never seen the key).
- Two years ago: Tessa expands Foxglove.
- Last March: Tessa goes to Larkmoor after the bank refuses. Signs Ray's name on a home-equity paper in April (ten months ago from the funeral).
- November (two months before the funeral): Larkmoor's default notice arrives at the shop address (Ray's business mail). Ray opens it, drives to Foxglove, learns the truth. Tessa breaks down. Ray decides to keep it from Loretta, partly for Tessa and partly because of his heart. He pays what he can. He has Tessa sign a one-page acknowledgement of the debt in his own writing. He writes two letters.
- December: Ray is hospitalized with heart failure; taken to the hospital by ambulance on December twenty-fourth; dies December thirty-first (eleven days before the funeral). Last notebook entry before that: December eighteenth, "Doctor says my heart is tired. I will tell Lo at New Year's."
- Saturday, January eleventh: the funeral. Tessa has a locksmith change the locks while the family is at the cemetery lunch.
- Following week: Loretta stays with Winnie. Sunday January twelfth: bags on the porch. Monday the thirteenth: church basement. Tuesday the fourteenth: Tessa's form letter, Voss's card. Wednesday the fifteenth: the bank (changed from Monday so the whispers chapter fits). Thursday the twenty-third, ten in the morning: the meeting (lock re-keyed at five thirty that morning). Terms signed the same day. Late February: Larkmoor withdraws.
- June: epilogue. Loretta's garden, Tessa cuts a new key.

## The box (what is in it, exactly)
1. A cloth-bound notebook, Ray's handwriting, dated from November: what he found, what he paid, who he spoke to. Plain, short entries. Last line: "I should have told Lo the first night. I wanted Tessa to be the one to say it."
2. Photocopies of the forged paperwork from Larkmoor (the loan agreement with "Raymond J. Kessler," blue ink loops wrong) and Larkmoor's default notice.
3. A single page in Ray's hand, "Acknowledgement," signed by Tessa and Ray and dated in November: Tessa admits she signed her father's name and promises to make it right.
4. Ray's will, simple and recent: the house goes where it already is (Loretta, by survivorship, he notes); the shop to Tessa with Gus's right of first refusal; the shop's savings to pay down the debt.
5. A cream envelope: "For Loretta." A second cream envelope: "For Tessa." Both sealed.
6. His brass key ring with the L-stamped duplicate of the house key (Loretta has hers; this is the spare he cut for Tessa when she was nineteen and never gave her).

## Letters (themes only, writer composes)
- To Loretta: apology for the secret, no excuses, a small joke about her red pen, an ask: "Give her one chance I gave her too late. Then do what you think is right and I'll be proud of it."
- To Tessa: private. Loretta never reads it, and we never see its words. After Tessa reads it, we see only her hands and what she does.

## Style notes for the writer
- Loretta's voice: precise, dry, uses courtroom habits as comedy and as armor ("let the record show"). Never self-pitying.
- The key is introduced in the cold open and returned to at every chapter end. Each chapter ends with a question or small discovery.
- Details of the locksmith trade (pin tumblers, bump keys, key blanks, deadbolt backwards) make it feel real: keep them short, in plain words.
- Use "Missus," "Mister," "Doctor," no abbreviations. Numbers and dates in words.
- No cooking subplot. Loretta eats a sandwich and a lot of tea, that is all.


## Added while writing the chapters (writer's additions, all consistent with the above)
- Ray calls Loretta "Lo". Her full steno-job line: thirty-three years, County Court of Common Pleas.
- The key tag in the box: the spare house key on Ray's brass ring carries a paper tag with one letter, T. It is NOT L-stamped (changed from the earlier note). Ch9: Loretta gives it to Tessa at the third Sunday supper; it no longer fits the door. Final key in June is cut by Tessa on Ray's machine, stamped L and T.
- Why Merrow Valley Bank can open a fraud review: Ray's signature card is the bank's own, and Cecilia compares it with the blue-ink loan paper (the R) in a one-page letter. Cecilia never discusses anyone's account; she says a review "begins with the wire records. We have some." In ch5 she says of Tessa, "I knew her father. I did not know she had been turned away at my own counter" (Merrow Valley itself refused Tessa's loan in March). In ch6 she checks the public county recorder: no mortgage has been recorded against the house since Loretta and Ray's was paid off; Larkmoor funded and never filed, so its lien is only paper. Cecilia is not in the room on the meeting morning; later she and Gus sign as witnesses to the terms.
- Gus keeps in a shop drawer a brass cylinder pinned with a tag "Birchwood", keyed by Ray to Loretta's key years ago when the old lock began to stick; Ray meant to install it when the old one gave out and never did. Gus swaps it into the front door at five thirty on the meeting morning (nine minutes). Gus also made a new leather leg strap with two brass rivets for the steno machine.
- Gus learns of the box key because Ray said two summers ago, "If anything happens, the box key is on my coat."
- Ch4: Ray had Loretta sign the box card three years ago, on the courthouse steps at their Wednesday sandwich lunch, "for the shop insurance". Ray's signature on the bank card is in black ink.
- Ch3: Tessa texts that a buyer wants the house, closing end of February. There was no buyer: Voss told Ray in November that if payments stopped Larkmoor would foreclose and sell the house by the end of February; Tessa dressed that up as a sale (she admits it in ch8). Ch5: Loretta deduces it and notes no title company would close with the second owner never having signed. Tessa had the shop's card account frozen by phoning the processor, and took Ray's truck (returned to the shop in February). Her form letter is headed "Kessler Estate Services", generated from a free online template site. Ch6 (Tuesday, January twenty-first, ten to four by the crooked clock in the shop): Tessa comes to the shop for the ledgers and the safe combination, says she has thirty-eight days, and that "the buyer's man" is coming to walk through the house Thursday at noon; Loretta then sets the meeting for ten that morning.
- Voss's damning exchange (ch7): Voss notices the word "verbatim" and half rises, then decides the two women are no danger and sits back down (he quit cigarettes at fifty; peppermints instead). "We fund first and do the title work later. Who verified the signature? Nobody, in the sense you mean. Tessa told us it was her father's hand, and our notary stamped it. We lend against the house. Who signs is between the family." He leaves "to consult counsel" at ten twelve, leaving a folded peppermint wrapper. Voss later "exploring opportunities in another state".
- Terms Loretta writes on a legal pad (ch8), seven in all: bank fraud review against Larkmoor; Tessa's full written statement, signed before Cecilia at the bank; Loretta will not ask anyone to press charges as long as Tessa keeps every line; shop to Tessa with Gus managing, shop earnings and savings repay what Ray promised (will filed with the register of wills); give up the lease on the second building (Foxglove is rented, nothing to sell), close the spa wing, keep the original Foxglove, see the credit union debt advisor (Monday at nine) to plan repayment of what Tessa actually received less Ray's thirty-one thousand as an unsecured personal debt; Tessa tells the church funeral committee the truth; supper every second Sunday for a year. Tessa and Loretta sign; Gus and Cecilia (not Loretta) sign as witnesses.
- The "very old woman" in the church basement (unnamed) later mails Loretta a card: "I thought as much."
- Time note (resolved): Ray died December thirty-first, eleven days before the funeral; the chapters say only "eleven days" and never give the date of death. Certified death certificates arrived in Wednesday's mail (January fifteenth), which is why the bank visit is Wednesday.

- Money outcome (ch9): the lien is void and Larkmoor cannot touch the house. Tessa still owes what she actually received (one hundred eighty thousand less Ray's thirty-one thousand) as an ordinary debt, on a years-long repayment plan she worked out with the credit union advisor; Larkmoor writes off fees and interest. Tessa gives up the lease on the second building in April and the landlord keeps the improvements.
- Loretta slept at Winnie's for three weeks after the meeting and moved back into the house on February fifteenth.
