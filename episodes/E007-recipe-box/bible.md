# E007 — Story bible
Narrator: Winnie, first person, past tense, telling it the following September from the porch of her house on Elm Row. Warm, wry, plain-spoken, a mail carrier's eye (she notices mailboxes, dogs, who is home and who isn't). She jokes about herself ("I can burn water"). No sermons.

## Characters (8 named; unnamed extras allowed: a dentist's receptionist, a stager's helper, neighbors)
**Winifred "Winnie" Brandvold** — narrator, 70. Retired rural route carrier, Fenmore Post Office, Route Three (sixty-one miles of gravel and county blacktop, two hundred and forty-odd boxes), thirty-four years, retired five years ago. Widow of Walt (died fourteen months ago, in March of last year, heart, at seventy-two). Short, sturdy, white braid pinned up, a canvas jacket she still wears from the route, reading glasses on a beaded chain from a customer. Dry humor, patient, does not hurry. She cannot cook ("I once set off a smoke alarm making toast"), but for thirty-four years customers pressed recipe cards into her hand at Christmas, and she kept every one in a green tin box, which her mother-in-law Ruth's old tin recipe box had started: two hundred and twelve cards, each one with a name and a porch. Drives a faded blue Ford pickup that was Walt's. Two-story white farmhouse on Elm Row, Fenmore, Iowa (fictional town, seat of fictional Larkin County), house paid off, in her name and Walt's before he died, now hers.
**Walt Brandvold** — late husband, retired high school shop teacher (taught thirty-one years at Larkin County High), built things, fixed things, never talked about money or feelings, hummed. Wrote his last letter at the kitchen table three weeks before he died, in carpenter's pencil on lined index card backs and a folded sheet. Called her "Win." His workshop: the converted machine shed behind the house, pegboard, a red steel toolbox with a brass padlock (his father's).
**Greg Brandvold** — son, 46. Claims adjuster in West Des Moines, ninety minutes away. Gentle, tired, conflict-avoidant, grieving his father by organizing logistics. Says "Mom, let's just look at it." Signs where Tessa points. His turn is quiet: he shows up at the transfer station.
**Tessa Brandvold** — daughter-in-law, 41. Certified home organizer, business "Clear Space by Tessa," neat blond bob, clipboard, label maker, soft voice, no sense of humor she'll admit to. Not greedy: afraid. Her mother, Carol, died eleven years ago when a stack of old newspapers fell in the hallway of her house; Tessa found her. She cannot tolerate a full cupboard. Says "I'm just trying to help you let go." She has already hired a stager and a realtor for the house.
**Hilda Voss** — 88, on Route Three since 1991, Hilda's farm is mile marker eleven. Walker with tennis balls, sharp tongue, hearing aid she turns off when bored. Author of card 117 (rhubarb crisp, in violet ink). Her roof was repaired in 2009, and she never knew who paid. Winnie's friend.
**Del Okafor** — 58, attendant at the Larkin County transfer station (the dump), ex-Navy, a thermos and a clipboard, speaks in short sentences, fiercely organized. He's known Winnie from the route for years; gives her the loader, a rake and a tarp.
**Bryce Lindqvist** — realtor, 36, "Prairie Home Realty," white teeth, a vest with his name stitched on it. Arrives Saturday with a lockbox, a sign and a smile.
**Ila Fenn** — 33, single mother of two, rents a small farmhouse on Route Three (mile marker twenty-two), cleans offices in town at night; card 154 (corn fritters). Walt built her a wheelchair ramp for her father in 2016 and never told Winnie; her father has since passed. She arrives Saturday with the fritters.

## Money, objects, places
- Recipe box: a dented green steel tin, Ruth's original wooden box inside it eventually; two hundred and twelve cards in total, tabbed with Walt's masking-tape labels by mile marker. Behind card 117, a slit in the lining: Walt's letter, folded in four, in a sandwich bag (he knew about damp).
- Dotted cards: nineteen cards have a small pencil dot in the top corner. Each dot is a household Walt helped, quietly, between 2004 and last year, never saying so. Examples (use some, not all): Hilda Voss's roof (2009); Ila Fenn's ramp (2016); Dewey Marsh's furnace (2011); the Lund twins' well pump (2019); a church bell rope; a bus-stop shelter.
- Red toolbox in the workshop with a brass padlock; key taped inside the tin lid (found after Winnie dries the box). Inside: Walt's ledger (a green spiral notebook), a Fenmore Savings and Loan passbook "Route Fund," balance eighteen thousand four hundred dollars (all saved from repair-shop jobs for neighbors' tools, mower repairs and cabinet orders over nineteen years), plus a short note: "Don't give it away on a whim. Walk the route first."
- Tessa put the red toolbox, the table saw and the lathe on an online marketplace on Friday night; a buyer ("a man from Ames with a trailer") comes Tuesday afternoon, and Winnie must get it back on Wednesday morning; the buyer turns out to be nice (unnamed, "the man from Ames"), returns it for the forty dollars deposit, and takes a different saw.
- Dumpster: a thirty-yard rolloff Tessa rented for "decluttering," filled Monday. Hauled to the Larkin County transfer station Tuesday at five. Winnie finds out at four fifty-five in the morning (she can't sleep, goes to the kitchen: the tin box shelf is empty; the dumpster driver's receipt is on the counter under a magnet).
- Letter text (key lines to be quoted in chapter 5): "Win, if you are reading this, the box got thrown out, or it got found, either way you're holding it, which means you went looking. Good. Don't be mad at Tessa. Folks who throw things out are usually afraid of something. Go and get the red toolbox before somebody sells it. The dots are porches. You know every one of them better than I do. I never could tell you, because you'd have told them thank you, and they'd have felt small. Walk the route first, Win. Then decide. P.S. Card 117 is the best crisp in the county and you still can't make it."
- Timeline: Walt dies in March last year. Tessa starts "helping" in October (her first visit: the pantry). Wave one: Saturday, April 26, Winnie at the dentist in Decorah? (use "the dentist in town," unnamed). Wave two: May 2-3 (workshop listings, condo brochures; Greg says "just look at it"). Wave three: Monday May 12 dumpster, Tuesday May 13 dawn at the transfer station; Wednesday May 14 the toolbox; Thursday May 15 and Friday May 16 Winnie drives the route with the nineteen dotted cards (using Walt's ledger); Saturday, May 17, ten o'clock: open house, Tessa and Bryce on the porch, the neighbors arrive; Sunday: Tessa and Winnie on the lawn at dusk. Epilogue: September, the Tuesday Route.
- Tessa's plan: list the house in June at three hundred and ten thousand dollars; buy Winnie a two-bedroom unit at a fifty-five-plus community called Willow Pointe in West Des Moines. Greg signed the listing paperwork "to be able to talk about it" on May 9. Winnie is the sole owner so the listing is not legally valid without her; she tells them that calmly, once (the point is not law, it's that she was never asked).

## Style and motifs
- Winnie counts mailboxes the way other people count sheep; she names mile markers.
- Card 117 appears in chapter 1 (flash-forward), chapter 5 (found) and chapter 8 (the first card Winnie herself writes goes right behind it).
- Humor: Winnie is the only person in Fenmore who cannot cook and owns the most recipes. Tessa's label maker. The stager moves a rooster.
- Tessa is never cruel. The audience should be able to dislike her and then understand her. Winnie never shouts.
- No hidden ownership, no lawyer, no document read aloud. The payoff is people.
