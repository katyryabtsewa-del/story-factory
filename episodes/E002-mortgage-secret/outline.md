# E002 — Outline (8 chapters, 1,100–1,200 words each, target ~9,200 words ≈ 60 minutes)

Narrator: Marlene Dunmore, looking back from the following April. First person, past tense. Warm, dry, precise.

## Chapter 1 — Not Really Family
- Cold open (first ~250 words): the last Saturday in June, the patio at 41 Tamarack Court, the "Six Years in Our Dream Home" party, thirty guests, white string lights. Marlene carries her lemon bars through the side gate and hears Cassidy answering a neighbor: "Oh, that's Trevor's mother. She helps out. She's not really family, more like support staff." Laughter. Marlene sets the tray down. One-line promise: by Labor Day she had stopped paying, and the people who laughed would hear every number out loud.
- Who she is: Marlene Dunmore, sixty-six, Pelham Landing, Michigan; thirty-four years as a school-district bookkeeper; green ledgers; Gerald's death seven years ago.
- Odessa arrives and sits beside her; Marlene says nothing. Cassidy's toast thanks "Trevor's hard work and our amazing community" and never mentions her. Jonah finds her under the string lights and takes a lemon bar.
- Hook: Jonah whispers, "Gran, Mommy said you're not on the sign." "What sign?" He points to the new porch plaque: THE DUNMORE FAMILY — EST. HERE.

## Chapter 2 — The Arrangement
- How it began: six years ago April, Trevor at her kitchen table, the collections letter, Cassidy's tears over the house she'd found. Marlene bought it with Gerald's insurance money: ninety thousand down, mortgage in her name only.
- The arrangement: eight hundred dollars a month "family rent" labeled "groceries," because Trevor begged her never to tell Cassidy. "A year or two," she said. The basement suite built for her old age, the roof, the furnace.
- Six good years on the surface: Sunday dinners, Jonah's first steps, the ledgers (pencil, then pen, humming).
- The first cracks: last Thanksgiving "just bring the rolls"; January: the garage code changed; the calendar app for "Grandma time" with thirty-minute slots.
- Hook: Cassidy's tag on a photo: "Home is where WE built it." Marlene opens the green ledger and runs her finger to the bottom of the last column; the total makes her stop humming.

## Chapter 3 — Thirty Minutes on Thursday
- Escalation wave 2: Jonah's birthday in March held without her ("small, just us"); she finds out from a photo Odessa sees online.
- She drives to Tamarack Court and finds her own key no longer fits the side door. Cassidy appears at the window, smiling: "Boundaries, Marlene. It's healthy."
- Scene at Marlene's table with Trevor: "It's fine, Mom," he says three times and rubs his neck. She asks him one question: does Cassidy know whose name is on the deed. He looks at the floor.
- Odessa, the Buick, a pie plate of facts: "Marlene, you're not a guest. You're the bank."
- Hook: a text from Cassidy: "Bernadette is moving into the basement suite in August. Trevor agreed. Please clear your things out by July."

## Chapter 4 — The Suite
- Wave 3: Marlene goes to the basement suite she paid twenty-eight thousand dollars to build, to take her late husband's folding chair and the quilt. Bernadette is already measuring for curtains. "Cassidy said you'd stopped coming down here."
- A pointed Sunday dinner: Cassidy hands her a printed "family guidelines" sheet. Number six: "Extended family visits by invitation."
- The party-night humiliation replayed in her head; she makes tea and opens the ledger and decides nothing yet; she reads Gerald's saying: "family is who shows up with a casserole and a ladder."
- Jonah calls at bedtime and says, in a small voice, "Gran, are you still my grandma if you're not family?" She answers carefully and steadily.
- Hook: Next morning Trevor shows up on her porch with a folder and a smile too bright: "Just some paperwork, Mom. For the refinance. The title company needs you to sign one thing."

## Chapter 5 — One Thing to Sign
- Trevor and the broker's folder; Marlene reads every page at her table while he talks about "lowering the rate." The cash-out refinance, two hundred sixty thousand dollars; the quitclaim deed with a blank "consideration" line.
- Marlene asks for the weekend. Trevor says Cassidy already put down the deposit on the studio. Marlene sees a sticky note in Cassidy's handwriting stuck to the broker's form: "Get M to sign before the Fourth. Don't let her read it — she'll make it a thing."
- Turn: she is not shocked, she is calm. She puts the sticky note into the ledger. She makes the decision and tells the reader what she decided: not revenge, arithmetic.
- She calls Naomi Feld.
- Hook: Naomi's receptionist: "She can see you tomorrow at eight. She said to bring the green books."

## Chapter 6 — Read the Paper
- Naomi's office above the bakery. Naomi reads the ledgers for forty minutes and says: one hundred sixty-eight thousand five hundred dollars, and you're the only name on the deed. Explains in two plain sentences: no lease means month-to-month; a quitclaim deed hands everything over for nothing; nothing in the paper obliges you to sign.
- The plan: sign nothing, end the arrangement in writing with proper notice, offer Trevor and Cassidy the house at a fair price on their own credit so the arithmetic comes out even. "Not a punishment. A correction."
- Odessa verifies the numbers at the credit union with Marlene's permission and brings a payoff statement: one hundred ninety-eight thousand dollars balance.
- Marlene tells no one. The quiet week: Sunday dinner as usual, she brings lemon bars and hums; Cassidy says "See? Boundaries make everything easier." Jonah slips a drawing into her purse: a train with three cars labeled Mom, Dad, Gran.
- Hook: she signs the notice at Naomi's desk; the courier is scheduled for Friday at four. "What happens Saturday?" "The family meeting," Naomi said. "At your table."

## Chapter 7 — The Family Meeting
- Early September, Marlene's table. Trevor, Cassidy, Bernadette (who invited herself). Lemon bars, a pot of coffee, three green ledgers stacked like a small tower.
- She does not shout. She reads out loud, calmly, from the ledger: purchase, down payment, mortgage in her name only, the rent labeled groceries, the roof, the furnace, the suite, the total. Cassidy: "That's not possible. Trevor said—" Trevor says nothing; then says: "Mom paid, Cass. She's been paying the whole time."
- Cassidy first denies, then attacks ("You tricked us into thinking—"), then Bernadette says "I gave notice on my apartment," and the room goes still. Marlene slides over the notice and the sticky note.
- The offer: sell to them at three hundred sixty thousand dollars, own mortgage on own credit, ninety days; or notice stands and she sells on the open market; the rent stops being "groceries" and becomes rent. "I'm not asking for profit. I'm asking for the arithmetic to come out even."
- Trevor stands up for the first time: he tells Cassidy he lied to her for six years and he's sorry, and tells Cassidy that she owes his mother an apology that isn't a "boundary."
- Hook: Cassidy picks up her purse, says nothing, and walks out; Trevor stays and asks if he can wash the coffee cups. Marlene says: "The cups can wait. Sit down, son. We're going to talk about why you never told me you were drowning."

## Chapter 8 — Who Shows Up
- Aftermath. Trevor and Marlene talk until midnight. He starts credit counseling; takes a second job on weekends; Cassidy goes to stay with Bernadette for three weeks.
- Cassidy returns and brings an apology that is not a strategy: a handwritten page. Not a full reconciliation, but a start. She takes down the plaque and takes the "dream home" posts offline, replaced by a post about "a year of real numbers."
- December closing at Harbor Light: Trevor and Cassidy buy the house at three hundred sixty thousand dollars on their own mortgage; Marlene is made whole; she puts forty thousand dollars of it into a college account for Jonah in Jonah's name.
- Marlene uses the rest on a trip with Odessa (Buick, the Upper Peninsula, a lighthouse) and a new green ledger with only one column: "joy."
- Epilogue: April. Sunday dinners again, now on her table. Garage code is hers again. Jonah's tooth gap closed. The plaque in her kitchen drawer, a keepsake. Final thought (short, not preachy): family is not a word on a sign; it's who shows up with a casserole and a ladder.
- Closing words to the listener in her own voice: ask whether they would have offered the house back, or sold it out from under them, and where they're listening from.
