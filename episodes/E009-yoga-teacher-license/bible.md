# E009 — Story bible
Narrator: Marilyn "Mimi" Tolliver, 65, first person, past tense, telling it the following spring from her own back porch. Sharp, dry, Gulf Coast Southern humor (never cruel, never whiny); the kind of woman who answers the phone on the second ring, keeps every receipt, and says "bless his heart" with a straight face. Form A (classic payback), humor, but built on *competence*, not on a hidden deed. No lawyer reveal at the end, no party reading. The turn happens in a tire-shop waiting room (open), a licensing-office counter (plan), a conference room (payback) and customers' porches (new life).

## Characters (8 named, different initials)
**Marilyn "Mimi" Tolliver (née Gaudet)** — narrator, 65. Born in Bayou La Batre, Alabama; office manager of Tolliver Blue Water Pools for thirty-seven years (since 1988), bookkeeper, dispatcher, complaint desk, the person every customer calls. At fifty-two (spring 2013) she passed the state exam and became the company's licensed *qualifying agent* (an inspector had found the old license holder, Dale's retired partner Gene, no longer valid, and Dale could not or would not sit the test: "I don't test well, Mimi, you do it."). Small, neat, reading glasses on a beaded chain, a Buick LeSabre named "Gladys," a box fan in the back office, a coffee mug that says World's Okayest Bookkeeper (gift from the crew). Married forty-one years; one grown daughter in Denver (unnamed; appears only in one short phone call; calls her "Mama"). Salary: thirty-eight thousand a year ("Dale called it 'family compensation'"). Own savings: a credit-union account she funded herself, forty-four thousand dollars, and her own small IRA; the house (paid off, 1996) is in both names.
**Dale Tolliver** — husband, 68. Founder (1988) of Tolliver Blue Water Pools in Dauphin Landing, Alabama (fictional town on Mobile Bay). Big laugh, white teeth, tan, sunglasses on his head, golf shirts with the company logo. A genuine salesman: customers love him; he has never read a permit. Vain, sure "the girl in the back" is furniture; says "Mimi, honey, it's just paperwork." Not violent; his sin is contempt and carelessness. Has never taken the licensing exam. Drives a white Ram truck with his face on the door.
**Skye Calloway** — yoga teacher, 39, runs Still Water Studio in a converted bank building ("the vault is the quiet room"). Shallow but not evil; says "I hold space for that." Told Dale he "has so much unlived life." Realizes, over the weeks, what the company actually is and that Dale is not a rich founder but a man with a cash-flow problem. Leaves him in January.
**Tripp Haverty** — 34, "growth consultant" from Gulf Aqua Partners, a Houston-based group that buys and rolls up pool companies. Dale's partner-in-waiting: they are buying sixty percent of Tolliver Blue Water for one point four million dollars, closing Friday, October third, after "paperwork" on Tuesday, September thirtieth (the license renewal date). Sleek, polite, reads spreadsheets not people; he does his homework on the numbers but never asks who the qualifier is — he assumes it is Dale. Not a cartoon: when he sees the truth, he leaves politely.
**Ray Beaumont** — 59, crew chief, with the company since 1991, big, quiet, soft-voiced, fixes pumps like a surgeon, calls Mimi "Miss Mimi." Has kept every customer's pump history in his head. Wears a cap from the Mobile BayBears. Loyal to the work, not to Dale.
**Odessa Whitfield** — 81, retired school principal, first customer of Mimi's era (pool built 1989), lives on Magnolia Point Road in a pink brick house with a pool she swims in every morning at six ("for my hips"). Sharp as a tack; calls Dale "that boy." Becomes the first customer of the new company.
**Wanda Pickett** — 55, clerk at the State Licensing Board for Pool and Spa Contractors, Mobile regional office (fictional agency), second floor of a state office building. Plain-spoken, has a stack of laminated rules, a candy dish and a "no" that she delivers kindly. Explains the rules in general terms.
**Priya Nair** — 41, family-law attorney with a small office above a bakery in Dauphin Landing. Calm, practical; advises Mimi only; no end-of-story document reveal. She asks the one question: "Did you ever sign a non-compete?"

## Places, objects, money (all fictional, general-terms legal)
- Tolliver Blue Water Pools: low cinderblock office with a blue awning and a fiberglass dolphin on the roof, Highway Ninety-Eight frontage, Dauphin Landing, Alabama. Three trucks, eleven employees, about three million one hundred thousand dollars in yearly revenue. Back office: Mimi's desk, a box fan, the framed license on the wall (a state certificate naming the qualifying agent: **Marilyn G. Tolliver**, number in the file; Dale walked past it twenty thousand times and never read it). Renewal due every September thirtieth, signed by the qualifying agent.
- The rule (in general terms, from Wanda): a pool contractor company must have a qualifying agent on file, a licensed person who passed the exam and answers for the company's work. If the qualifier resigns in writing, the company can keep finishing existing jobs for a grace period of sixty days, but cannot sign new contracts or pull new permits until it names a new qualifier who passes the exam; the exam is given only a few times a year (next sitting: Tuesday, November eighteenth, in Montgomery). A qualifier may serve only one company at a time. A resigning qualifier may qualify a new company of her own right after. There is no non-compete in Mimi's file or hers.
- The buyer's lender requires the license to be in good standing with a named qualifier on closing day. Tripp assumes Dale holds it.
- Dale's plan: sell sixty percent to Gulf Aqua for one point four million dollars, take Skye on a "reset" trip, keep a title ("Founder and Chairman"), and make Mimi sign the annual renewal "like always" on Tuesday, September thirtieth, in front of the buyers, so no one notices she holds it. He tells Tripp on a phone call Mimi overhears: "She's a clerk, Tripp. She'll sign anything I put in front of her. Forty-one years, she's never once read the fine print."
- Dale's three waves: (1) Monday after the tire shop: Tripp arrives, moves into Mimi's office, and Mimi is "reassigned" to the supply room and told to hand over the books; (2) a settlement offer: sixty thousand dollars and the Buick, the house to be sold; plus he takes her off the company health insurance "for the transition"; (3) at the Friday crew meeting he announces "discrepancies in the books" to the crew, implying Mimi has been skimming (a petty-cash tin was short seventy-one dollars in 2019; he never asked about it; she had the receipt: Ray's boots).
- Timeline: late August (Thursday, August twenty-eighth: the tire shop, Greer's Tire and Brake on Highway Ninety-Eight; ballgame on the waiting-room TV; a free-coffee machine; Dale needs front tires on the Ram). Monday, September first (Labor Day: Tripp comes anyway; note: Labor Day is a holiday so they work; make it Tuesday, September second); first week of September: wave one. Second week: the settlement offer, Priya. Third week: crew-meeting accusation (Friday, September nineteenth); the overheard call (Saturday, September twentieth, at the office). Monday, September twenty-second: Mimi drives to Wanda Pickett in Mobile. Tuesday, September thirtieth, nine in the morning: the conference room at the company, with Tripp, two men from Gulf Aqua (Mister Lund, Mister Okafor — not named characters; mention at most as "the two gentlemen from Houston"), Dale, Ray at the door, and the renewal form. Friday, October third: the closing that never happens. A tropical storm ("Tropical Storm Odalys", fictional) brushes the coast Sunday, September twenty-eighth, with wind and rain; pools overflow; the old customers call Mimi's cell, not the office. She and Ray work two nights.
- Aftermath: Gulf Aqua walks away Tuesday afternoon (the deal "paused pending license verification"; officially dead Thursday). Dale files an emergency application with the board; Wanda tells him the rules (kindly). He is allowed to finish existing jobs, cannot sign new ones. His exam is November eighteenth; he fails by four points (he reads the questions aloud, calls Mimi's name once in the parking lot). Nine of the eleven employees leave by November; eight follow Ray and Mimi. Skye leaves Dale in January ("the energy here is heavy") after learning there is no one to pay for the studio's new vault door. Dale sells the trucks and the customer list to a competitor in December for three hundred and ten thousand dollars (not a million point four), pays off the line of credit, and ends up with a small apartment. The settlement: Mimi keeps the house and her own accounts; they split the rest by plain arithmetic; she asks for nothing spectacular. She does not gloat. She does not rescue him either: when he comes to her porch in January and asks her to be the qualifier again "for old times' sake, forty percent," she says no, once, kindly.
- New company: Gaudet Blue Water Service (Mimi's maiden name), a pelican (not a dolphin) on the truck door at Ray's insistence; first customer Odessa; first week sixty-one weekly service stops; by spring one hundred and four customers, five trucks' worth of work in two; Ray is partner at twenty percent; Mimi is again at a desk, with a box fan, but the sign says her name.
- Final scene: April, her back porch, iced tea, Ray's pelican truck pulling in; a short plain line about being read at last ("somebody finally read the fine print — and it was me"? use own words); question to the listener: would you have signed or told him? and where they are listening from.

## Motifs
- "Fine print": Dale's line, her line.
- Box fan and the framed license on the office wall; at the end the frame hangs on her porch wall, new certificate beside it.
- Odessa's six a.m. swims and her "that boy" for Dale.
- Ray's "Yes ma'am, Miss Mimi."
- Mimi never shouts; her weapons are punctuality, receipts, a very polite voice, and "Bless his heart."
- Avoid names/towns: Maple Grove, Larkspur, Fieldstone. Business, agency and town names are fictional.
