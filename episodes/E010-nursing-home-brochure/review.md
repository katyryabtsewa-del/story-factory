Read: bible, outline, idea, ch01-ch08, style-guide, story-formula, variety, VARIETY.md, idea.md of E001-E009 (plus the E006 and E008 bibles for name checks). The chapters were not edited. Line numbers are file line numbers (the `# title` is line 1).

Lint (`tools/lint_script.py`, ch01-ch08): 9461 words, about 61 min, every chapter 1118-1198 words, no banned cliches. Repeated phrases it reports: "the time in the margin" x6, "let me read that back" x5, "let's not make this about paperwork" x4, "card ending four four one seven" x3, "lantern spoon river saddle ash" x3. Overall the story works. The investigative spine is sound, the heroine is smart, and the notebook proof is satisfying. ## Обязательно исправить (required fixes)

### 1. The state inspection is a coincidence, which makes Peg lucky instead of smart
- **Location:** ch06 (Fern scene and the end of the chapter); ch07 lines 47-49.
- **Problem:** The outline and bible say Fern files the complaint on Monday. The script never says so. Ch06 ends Sunday night with nothing said about a filing, a date or a plan. In ch07 two surveyors then walk in at exactly 10:20, in the middle of the conference, with Fern behind them, and Peg shows no surprise ("Fern gave me the smallest nod"). The listener cannot tell whether Peg planned it or got lucky. Complainants also do not get to schedule an unannounced inspection.
- **Fix:** In ch06, after Fern's "Paper. Dates. A person who was there," add that Fern will file on Monday with Peg's permission, and that she "can't promise a date, only that they come without calling". Add one Peg line that she will run the Thursday conference as if nobody would come, and that the notebook has to stand on its own. Then have Peg not know when they would arrive, with Fern simply in the lobby because Peg told her the conference time. In ch07 the surveyors become a bonus rather than a rescue, and Peg is the one who carries the scene. Also consider moving Fern out of "over their shoulders" and into the lobby. Ombudsmen do not ride with surveyors.

### 2. Peg moves in and out of a locked wing with no explanation
- **Location:** ch03 line 51 (keypad, "four numbers with his body between me and his hand"); ch06 line 19 ("I signed myself out at the front desk"); ch06 line 37 (7 a.m. in the lot); ch04 line 63 (the elevator).
- **Problem:** Peg is behind a keypad door, yet she walks to the front desk, the library, the lot and back, with no mention of who opens the door. Gideon thinks she is at "moderate concern," so he would not let her sign herself out to a library. The ch03 detail about him hiding the code is set up and never paid off. In ch04 the elevator opens straight onto Birch Court (Gideon steps out of it), which contradicts "a gray door with a keypad at the end of a second-floor hallway".
- **Fix:** Pay off the code with Peg's own skill. She has heard the keypad's tones, a dispatcher can tell tones apart, and by Tuesday she knows the four numbers. Add one line in ch04 or ch06 ("the lock chirped four notes, and I had sat on a console that taught me to hear which"). Add one line in ch06 where Gideon is glad to let her sign out because she is "so independent". In ch04, change the elevator to "the keypad door buzzed and Gideon came through it".

### 3. The director of nursing's answer is ambiguous at the exact moment the scene turns
- **Location:** ch07 line 43: "My stamp," she said slowly, "is kept at the nurses' station."
- **Problem:** It does not say she did not approve the form. It sounds like an admission of lax stamp control, or a deflection, and it hands Gideon a defense. This is the scene's key beat, and it is muddy. Also, by ch07 line 33 she was in Denver on October eleventh but back in the building from Tuesday the thirteenth. Peg was behind the keypad door, her room was in the building, and the 911 fall happened on Wednesday. "I never saw you before today" strains belief after nine days.
- **Fix:** Give her one clear sentence, for example: "I did not review this, and I did not stamp it. The stamp is kept at the nurses' station where anyone can reach it." That makes her choose a side in public. Change line 37 to "I knew your name from the census, Missus Okerlund. I had not met you." Another option is to have her tell Peg she did not see the fall report either.

### 4. Typo and sentence error in ch04
- **Location:** ch04 line 9: "three aides for the nineteen residents in Birch Court,. They were not unkind."
- **Fix:** Remove the stray comma before the period.

### 5. "Faster than any call light" is wrong, and the numbers are cluttered
- **Location:** ch04 line 61: "The ambulance arrived in eleven minutes, which was faster than any call light on that floor." Peg's own fastest call light was eleven minutes. Also ch05 line 13 ("two fifty-five").
- **Problem:** The sentence is false on the page. Separately, "eleven" now carries eleven seconds, eleven minutes (fastest call light), eleven minutes (stamp before test end), eleven years (Imelda), eleven minutes (ambulance), October eleventh and the clock time. Audio listeners will blur the one that is evidence.
- **Fix:** Make the ambulance "nine minutes" in ch04 and "two fifty-three" in ch05 and in the bible. The sentence then becomes true and one "eleven" disappears.

### 6. Husband's line in ch07 is unclear
- **Location:** ch07 line 59: "Dana," he said. "She's told you the number."
- **Problem:** Which number? The four digits Peg just said? The seventy-five hundred? A listener, particularly on audio, will not know. It is meant as the husband's moment of moral turn, and it is lost.
- **Fix:** Make it concrete. For example: "Dana. That's her card. I asked you whose card it was, and you told me it was ours." This also gives Dana's husband a small reckoning of his own and makes him complicit by silence, not just sympathetic.

### 7. Cyril cannot be at "our table" in ch07
- **Location:** ch07 line 63: "as I passed the dining room, I saw Cyril Abernathy sitting at our table".
- **Problem:** The conference room is on the first floor. "Our table" is in the Birch Court dining room on the second floor, behind the keypad. Peg is leaving, so she has no reason to pass it. Cyril salutes from his table without an explanation of why he is out.
- **Fix:** Either bring Cyril down to the lobby in his wheelchair with Imelda ("he asked to see the front door from the outside"), which is also a stronger image for his "forty-one paces" line, or have Peg stop at the Birch Court door on her way out. The first option is better and costs two sentences.

### 8. Timeline of Lu and Peg does not add up
- **Location:** ch02 line 3: "I trained her at the console in nineteen eighty-four, when she was a girl".
- **Problem:** Peg is seventy, retired at sixty-four after thirty-six years, so she started in 1984. She would be training Lu in her own first year. Lu is also seventy-three, three years older than Peg, so she was thirty-one in 1984, not "a girl". (The bible has the same issue.)
- **Fix:** "I trained her at the console in nineteen ninety, when she was a new hire with a laugh like a screen door." Drop "girl". Update the bible.

### 9. Dangling threads from ch02: the realtor and the doctor's-office "concerns"
- **Location:** ch02 lines 19 and 23-25 (the doctor's-office calls; the realtor "looking at the light"); ch08 lines 23-27 (Dana's confession).
- **Problem:** Both are set up as the "waves" of escalation, then never touched again. In ch08 Dana confesses the card and the fee, but not the realtor, who suggests Dana was thinking about the house. A reader will wonder whether the house was part of it.
- **Fix:** Put both into Dana's "read-back" in ch08: one line each, such as "I called Doctor So-and-so's office. I told them you were forgetting things." and "Her name is Carla. She is a client. I asked her to tell me what the place would bring." Peg's reaction should be one flat sentence, no more.

### 10. Name and catchphrase overlap with recent episodes (variety.md)

Dana's catchphrase "I'm just trying to get ahead of it" (ch01 line 11, ch06 line 51, ch06 final) vs Curtis Brandt's catchphrase "Let's get ahead of it" in E006 (bible limit: five uses).
- **Problem:** `channel/variety.md` says names must not repeat in the last ten episodes. The shared catchphrase is the most noticeable, since both are the villain's tag line and both episodes involve a signing trap.
- **Fix:** Rename the volunteer coordinator (for example "Wren Ostrander") and the daughter (for example "Carrie"). Replace Dana's phrase with something that is hers, such as "Mom, I've already handled it" or "I'm just taking it off your plate". Update bible, outline and VARIETY.md. Do this in a find-and-replace pass over the chapters.

### 11. The "I'd turn Dana in front of everybody" choice is not examined
- **Location:** ch06 line 53-57 (Peg could write a check, does not, and says "I know" gently) and ch07 (Dana is blindsided).
- **Problem:** Peg has just heard the whole motive (debt, bank, balloon) and lets Dana walk into the conference unwarned. That is fine and even strong, but ch06 reads as if Peg's silence is gentleness. The audience may feel Peg is being quietly cruel. The text never lets her see this.
- **Fix:** One sentence in ch06 after "I know": Peg acknowledges that she is not warning Dana, and why ("If I told her, she would tell Gideon, and then ten other people in his building would have their forms rewritten before Monday"). This makes the silence a decision with a cost and keeps the later forgiveness earned.

## Желательно (optional)

### A. Overused signature gestures: judgment
**"The time in the margin" is overused.** Counting variants ("nine ten in the margin," "My own margin said," "wrote the time," "I wrote down the time") there are about ten uses. Four of eight chapters end on the gesture (ch01 line 45, ch02 line 61, ch05 line 61, ch06 line 59), and ch05 has it three times in one chapter. Ending four chapters the same way flattens the hooks and is the single most repeated beat in the script. **"Let me read that back" is borderline overused.** There are five literal uses plus "I'd rather read it back" (ch02), "May I read something back" (ch07), "read it to her" (ch06) and "would you read it back" (ch08), with a chapter title on top. Most of them are well used. The ch08 inversion, where Peg asks Dana to read it back, is the best and earns its place.
- **Keep:** margin: ch01 line 19 (setup), ch03 line 27 (first live use), ch05 line 61 (the 3:52 note), ch07 line 9 (Dana sighs, funny), ch08 line 35. Read-back: ch01 line 23, ch03 line 47, ch07 (the reading itself), ch08 line 25.
- **Cut or vary:** margin: ch01 line 45 (end the chapter on the card instead, e.g. on "I had not thought about it since"), ch02 line 61 (end on a harder hook, see C), ch05 lines 17 and 43, ch06 line 59 (replace with a forward line, see D). Read-back: ch02 line 13 (just "That's the problem as stated"), ch04 line 55 (a caller reading back to the dispatcher is inverted; use "Here's what I have"), ch05 line 13 (let Peg simply list the times), ch07 line 29 (redundant right after her own long reading).

### B. "Let's not make this about paperwork"
Four uses plus the surveyor's reversal. As a runner with a payoff it works. The problem is that ch07 has Gideon say it twice (lines 17 and 51), ten minutes apart. Cut the first and give him "Of course, of course. We have nothing to hide." The second one then lands right before the surveyor's line.

### C. Weak chapter endings: ch02 and ch06
- ch02 ends on the fountain and a banner, a mood and not a hook. Suggest ending on something that sets up the trap, for example Peg noticing that the lobby sign-in book already lists "Okerlund, Margaret, Birch Court" under Expected Arrivals, a day before she ever saw the unit.
- ch06 ends on "wrote the time in the margin" after a soft scene. Suggest ending on the plan stated in one line: "Thursday, ten o'clock. Bring the notebook. Do not warn Dana," which also solves required item 11 and builds the audience's wait for ch07.

### D. Ch07 payoff is rushed and does not use the setup
- ch07 is the main payoff and Peg's read-back is a two-paragraph summary. Several setups are never used. In ch06 Peg writes down Gideon telling the dietary manager Birch Court "had to be filled by the end of December, because the owner's lender had a little list" and sees an open spreadsheet. Imelda's testimony ("nineteen is the one that gets you in the door") is never used, and nobody mentions Cyril's field book. Add Gideon's own sentence about the lender, quoted back to him with the time. It is short and devastating.
- Peg misses the strongest logical point: if the form is right and she is impaired at nineteen of thirty, she cannot legally sign a power of attorney or an agreement. One line in ch07 ("If your form is right, Mister Pratt, I can't sign either of these. If it's wrong, why is it in my file?") is exactly her voice and costs nothing.
- Line 25: "The incident was written up as a slip in his room." Peg never saw the report. Change to "I was told it would be written up as a slip in his room."

### E. Gideon is only a smile until the epilogue
The scenes show him as smarm with a vest; the humanizing paragraph is in ch08 line 7 ("I think he looked at a number, and then at a person"). One small beat earlier would make the epilogue earned: ch05 or ch06 where he takes a phone call from the lender with his hand over his face, or is gentle with a resident for a moment. Also line 15 of ch03 ("none of it was ever the laugh itself") is unclear on first listen; simplify.

### F. Cyril's ending in ch08 has two plausibility problems
- Line 9: "One hundred and four paces from the front door to the kitchen" is far too long for a house (about two hundred and sixty feet). The joke does not read. Make it "Fourteen paces from the front door to the kitchen. All open."
- A man who spent nine years in a locked wing is "home in his own house" by Christmas with a lift chair, but the niece lives in Pueblo and drives up. Either say the niece moved in for the winter, or say he went to her house. Add "his house, which had never sold, because his niece never believed the form" if the house stays.

### G. Dana's read-back in ch08 is told, not shown
Lines 25-27 summarize twenty minutes. This is the emotional climax of the forgiveness arc, and the audience gets nothing but "she did not look away." Add three or four of her own sentences in order (the card, the doctor's call, the fee, the form), as she would say them, with Peg's single correction. It also gives the realtor and the doctor's office somewhere to land (see required item 9).

### H. "Nineteen" appears in too many roles
Score nineteen of thirty, nineteen residents, nineteen minutes (middle call light), nineteen minutes of test (ten twelve to ten thirty-one, never stated), and Imelda's ch05 speech uses "nineteen" in three different senses in one paragraph (line 31). On audio this is confusing exactly where it should be sharp. Suggest the middle call light becomes sixteen, or Imelda's speech replaces "Birch Court has nineteen" with "We have nineteen residents". The title is "Eleven, Nineteen, Twenty-Seven", so changing the call light means changing the title and ch04, ch06 and ch07 mentions. If that is too much, accept it.

The brochure is reached at roughly word 170, which is acceptable, but the first sentence is a gift-bag description. A stronger first line is the dryly specific one that already exists in the idea: "My daughter gave me a nursing-home brochure for my seventieth birthday, and the waiter took the cake back to the kitchen." The promise of a state inspector arrives around word 330. Consider moving it up or splitting it. Also line 15: "what I did not know yet but would know within the hour" is inaccurate, since within the hour she learns only the card number, not the inspection. Replace with "what I did not know yet".

### J. Plausibility of facility details (general terms)
- The seven-and-a-half-thousand "Seven-five" in ch05 line 59 will sound like "seventy-five" on audio. Peg should gloss it once in ch05 ("seven thousand five hundred, in the language of people who are paid for it").
- A locked-unit admission normally needs a physician's order. Gideon never mentions one. Add "the doctor's sign-off will follow," which also connects to Dana's calls to the doctor's office.
- The ombudsman has conflict-of-interest rules. One line in ch08 ("Fern made me fill out a form about family members in the business, and I wrote Dana's name and a date") would pre-empt a sharp listener and is also dry and funny.

- The three-days gap between Fern filing (Monday the nineteenth) and an inspection on the twenty-second is fast but acceptable if Fern says the fall counts as an immediate safety concern. Say so.

That is deliberate and fine, but note it.

## Overall score: 7 / 10

Strengths: a calm, specific narrator; the notebook and timing evidence; the 911 call with the old trainee ("Peg? Is that you, Dispatch Four?"); Cyril's pacing log; the ch05 reveal (stamp time eleven minutes before the test ended, director in Denver); the reversal of "read it back" in ch08; a warm ending without preaching.

Weaknesses: soft ch02 and ch06 (about sixteen minutes with little pressure); a quiet reckoning in ch07 whose key beat (the nurse's answer) is muddy and whose inspection timing looks like luck; thin Gideon until the epilogue; repeated gesture endings; name and catchphrase overlap with E006 and E008.

**The one change that would most improve retention:** in ch06, turn the middle of the story into a visible plan with a ticking clock. Peg tells the listener (and Fern) that Thursday at ten is when the folder gets signed, that Dana does not know what is coming, and that the inspection may or may not arrive. End ch06 on that line instead of "wrote the time in the margin". It makes ch07 something the listener waits for, fixes the coincidence problem (item 1) and the Dana-cost problem (item 11), and removes the weakest chapter ending.
