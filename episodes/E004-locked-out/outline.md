# E004 — Outline (70 min, 9 chapters, 1,150-1,250 words each; total 10,400–11,200)

Form B: mystery of the key, each chapter answers one question and opens another.

1. **The New Lock.** Cold open: the afternoon of Ray's funeral. Loretta returns from the cemetery lunch to find a stranger's van, a new silver deadbolt, Tessa on the porch with a folder: "This is my father's house. It's mine now." Promise: "Tessa had a locksmith change my locks. What she did not know was that Ray had been a locksmith for forty-six years, and that he had left me something she could not pick." Loretta in the cold with a casserole dish, her overcoat, Ray's good coat over her arm. The brass key tagged "L. when you need it," found in the lining at the cemetery. Winnie pulls up. Ends: Loretta follows Winnie to her car and looks at the key and the number stamped on it: 417.
2. **Dad's Wife.** Who she is: stenographer, Ray, the courthouse doors, Joan's death, the nineteen years of Tessa calling her "Dad's wife." Night at Winnie's. Tessa's texts. Sunday: Loretta's belongings in black garbage bags on the porch at Birchwood Lane in the snow, including the steno machine case. Gus arrives at the shop door to help carry. Hook: Gus looks at the new lock and says it is installed backward and Ray would never have allowed it. He then asks if she found "the thing on Ray's coat."
3. **Whispers.** Escalation: Monday, the church ladies; Tessa told them Loretta "worked on a dying man." Tessa's lawyer-sounding letter (not an attorney, a form from a website) claiming the house; accounts frozen at the shop; Tessa takes Ray's truck. Tessa also says there is a buyer for the house. Loretta's calm response: she writes down everything on a legal pad, with dates. Winnie sees a man in a long coat photographing the house: Dale Voss. Hook: he leaves a card in the new mail slot: Lakeshore Bridge Funding.
4. **Box 417.** The bank. Cecilia Rowe explains: box rented three years ago in both names; Ray added her as co-renter and had her sign a card she barely remembers signing at the courthouse "for the shop insurance." Death certificate, ID, two keys needed: the guard key (bank's) and Loretta's. The vault, the long narrow room. She opens it. Inventory. She does not read the letters yet; she reads the notebook first. Hook: the first entry: "November third. Opened the envelope meant for the shop. It was not for the shop."
5. **What Ray Knew.** The notebook told as Ray's entries: Lakeshore's default notice; Ray drives to Happy Tails; Tessa breaks; the forged signature, the wrong loop of the R, blue ink; the Acknowledgement page; the payments; "I should have told Lo the first night." Loretta in Winnie's car, the heater on, hands shaking for the first time. She sees the whole picture: why the locks, why the hurry: Tessa needed the house empty and the notebook gone before the will was read. And Tessa had no idea about box 417. The decision: Loretta does not storm in; she asks Cecilia what the bank can do and what it cannot. Hook: Cecilia says plainly, "Mrs. Kessler, the bank can open a fraud review. But you're the owner. How it ends with the person who signed is your decision, not mine."
6. **On the Record.** Plan: Loretta asks Gus to re-key the real door back with Ray's own cylinder, in the dark. She calls Dale Voss and Tessa to a meeting at the house, Thursday at ten, "to discuss the sale." Preparation: the steno machine repaired by Gus (the strap), Winnie bakes nothing, brings an extension cord. Cecilia agrees to be available by phone, not present; she supplies the bank's signature card comparison. The tension: what if Tessa doesn't come, what if Voss brings someone, what if Loretta cannot say the words. Hook: Thursday, nine fifty-eight, headlights in the snow.
7. **Let the Record Show.** The meeting at the kitchen table (day, snow). Loretta announces she is taking verbatim testimony; anyone who stays agrees. Voss is casual: "Mrs. Kessler, I'm sure we can sort this out. The signature question is a timing issue; we don't verify every notary." Tessa, white, silent, then furious. Loretta lays out the facts: joint ownership, her signature never on anything, the wrong R. Voss says the line that damns him ("We lend on the house; who signs is between the family"). Loretta reads back his statement from her steno tape. Voss leaves "to consult counsel," taking his card and his mints. Tessa remains. Loretta slides across only the cream envelope marked "For Tessa" and leaves the room. Hook: through the kitchen door, the sound of paper, and then nothing for a very long time.
8. **One Chance.** Tessa in the hallway, hands shaking, no apology yet, says only "He knew the whole time." Loretta's terms: she will not pretend it did not happen. Cecilia's fraud review proceeds against Lakeshore, whose lien is void; Tessa will make a full written statement that protects Loretta and the bank; the shop goes to Tessa as Ray wished, with Gus managing, the shop's earnings and savings repay what Ray had promised; Tessa will sell the spa building, close the second wing, and meet with a debt advisor at the credit union — and Tessa and Loretta will have supper every second Sunday for a year. Tessa asks if Loretta is doing this for Ray. Loretta: "Partly. Mostly I'm tired of being the only one who remembers how to be decent." Bittersweet: it is not forgiveness yet. Hook: Tessa hands back the locksmith's cheap lock key on the porch, and asks, small, if she can see the old brass key.
9. **The Key to the Front Door.** Late February to June. Lakeshore backs off and the review closes; the bank's lawyer says the lender will take a loss. Gus runs the shop. Tessa's Sunday visits are awkward, then less so. Tessa sells the spa building and keeps the original one. June: the porch, a new door lock Tessa installs herself and a key she cuts by hand on Ray's machine: brass, with a small L stamped on it, and a small T beside it. What Loretta learned about Ray, about herself, about letting people finish their own sentences. Closing line plus listener question (e.g., "Would you have given her the chance, and where are you listening from?").
