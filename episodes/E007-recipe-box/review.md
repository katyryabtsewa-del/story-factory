# E007 Recipe Box: editorial review

Reviewed: bible.md, outline.md, idea.md, script/ch01..ch08.txt, channel/style-guide.md, channel/story-formula.md, idea.md of E001..E006, episodes/VARIETY.md.

Lint (`.venv/bin/python tools/lint_script.py episodes/E007-recipe-box/script/ch0*.txt`): 9402 words, about 60 minutes, every chapter 1140 to 1200 words (ok). No banned cliches. Only repeat flagged: "the larkin county transfer station" x3, "farm wife at mile marker six" x3 (acceptable). My own grep found no digits, "$", "%", ellipses, dashes, or ALL CAPS. Abbreviation problems the lint missed are listed below ("Misc", "P.S.").

Overall the script is clean, warm and consistent in voice, with good mail-carrier detail. The main weaknesses: the title promise (card 117 and the letter) is not planted in the first minute; ch2 and ch3 have no open question; Winnie loses a whole day (Tuesday afternoon) so the toolbox scene feels manufactured; the Saturday payoff is soft and its key line is not planted; a few numbers do not add up (fund, years, boxes).

## Must fix

### 1. Title and cold open do not match: card 117 and the letter are absent from ch1
- Location: ch01, whole chapter. Card 117 first appears in ch05 ("It was card one hundred seventeen, Hilda's rhubarb crisp"). The word "letter" does not appear in ch01 at all.
- Problem: the bible says "Card 117 appears in chapter 1 (flash-forward)". The title promises "Behind Card 117 Was Walt's Last Letter", but the payoff arrives at about minute 33 and the listener has had no hint of a letter, dots or a secret in the box. The first 60 seconds (about 156 words) are only a rake, a hill of garbage and Del. Strong, but the hook is "she wants a box" without the question "what is in it".
- Fix: in the cold open, after "A green steel tin..." add one or two sentences naming card 117 and the sealed-off question, for example that on that morning she was looking for one card in particular, Hilda's rhubarb crisp, number one hundred seventeen, and that she had no idea yet that her husband had left her a message behind it. (Narrator already uses "what I can tell you now"; a second foreshadow is consistent.) Keep the Saturday-noon promise.

### 2. Dead hours: ch2 and ch3 (minutes 8 to 23) carry no open question
- Location: ch02 (closet, canisters) and ch03 (tags, brochures, dinner), up to the last line of ch03.
- Problem: ch01 ends at the same moment as the flash-forward (empty shelf, receipt, taillights). Listener already knows the tin is gone and that she will go to the dump. Then about fifteen minutes of backstory follow with no returning thread, so the ch01 hook is "spent" and nothing replaces it until Greg's line at the end of ch03. Both chapters are escalation by repetition (Tessa clears something, Winnie says thank you or no, Greg says "she means well" or "just look at it").
- Fix: (a) plant the mystery early, for example in ch02 the Christmas before Walt died, Walt watched Tessa clear his father's old magazines and said only "She's scared of something, Win" (this also fixes item 4 below); (b) have the narrator return to the dump for one sentence at the end of ch02 or in the middle of ch03 ("Del's loader was idling somewhere in my future, but I did not know it yet") or tease the dots, which Winnie has seen but not understood.

### 3. Tuesday afternoon vanishes: why is the toolbox still at risk on Wednesday?
- Location: ch05 final lines ("Today was Tuesday, the thirteenth of May. Which meant the man from Ames was coming tomorrow") and ch06 opening ("I was in the machine shed by six thirty... the toolbox was not under the bench... I found it outside on the grass").
- Problem: (a) In ch03 Tessa told Winnie the pickup date, "he'll pick it up on the fourteenth", and "The man from Ames messaged Tessa and said the fourteenth was still good", so the ch05 "realization" at the calendar is not a discovery. (b) Winnie holds the key on Tuesday morning, the letter says "Go and get the red toolbox before somebody sells it", and she does not walk across her own yard to the shed on Tuesday. Her whole Tuesday afternoon and night are missing. (c) The toolbox is then carried onto the lawn and tarped, with a note, by "Tessa's helpers" while Winnie and Greg are asleep in the house. This makes the smart heroine careless to create a comedy beat.
- Fix: close the gap in ch06's first paragraph. Either she and Greg went straight to the shed Tuesday, the box was under the bench, and she could not open it because she was too tired / Greg talked her into sleeping first (not great), or better: Tuesday she finds the shed padlocked with a new lock from Tessa and the key is with Greg's wife; or Tessa's helpers come at dawn Wednesday before she is up (state a time, say five thirty). And in ch05 rewrite the final hook so Winnie herself remembers the date (not the calendar) and says "tomorrow" aloud.

### 4. Walt's letter is psychic about Tessa
- Location: ch05, the letter: "Don't be mad at Tessa. Folks who throw things out are usually afraid of something." Winnie's comment right after: "He had written that before the clipboard and the canisters and the dumpster, but he had seen something in her, and he had known before any of us."
- Problem: Walt wrote it three weeks before he died (March last year); Tessa's first "help" was the following October. The letter names her and predicts the dumpster and the sale. Listeners 55+ will accept a loving note, but "he saw something in her" is too thin to explain it.
- Fix: plant one scene before Walt's death (see item 2a), or soften the letter: he writes "Don't be mad at whoever clears the house" and then names her only as "Greg's Tessa, who lost her mother under a pile". Alternatively let Greg have told Walt about Carol. One sentence in ch01 or ch02 is enough.

### 5. The key line "I'm busy on Tuesdays" is not earned
- Location: ch08, "I'm not moving, Tessa. I'm busy on Tuesdays." (also in ch08, "Could I ride along on a Tuesday?").
- Problem: no earlier chapter makes Tuesday a day for anything. The Tuesday Route is created after the line is spoken. The line is the planned single sentence of the reckoning, and it lands as a non sequitur. Also, Tuesday is already used as the weekly ritual in E006 (mending table "every Tuesday").
- Fix: plant a regular day in ch06 and ch07 (for example Walt's "shop" absences were always the same weekday: "He was at the shop every Thursday for nineteen years", and the ledger dates confirm it), then deliver the line as "I'm busy on Thursdays" in ch08 and rename the ritual "the Thursday Route". That also solves the E006 overlap (see item 16).

### 6. Open house: why does Winnie allow it, and what does Greg's "nod" mean?
- Location: ch06, "The open house is Saturday at ten... It's my house." / "I know it is."; ch08, "Bryce... stuck a sign in my front yard and a lockbox on my door" and "Then she looked at Greg, who nodded to her, once, and she lowered the clipboard."
- Problem: Winnie is the sole owner (stated in ch04), has said no four times, yet lets a realtor install a lockbox on her door and a sign on her lawn. No reason is given and she has no plan beyond "walk the route". It reads as passivity, not strategy. At the climax Greg's single nod is unexplained: the audience does not know what he is signalling, and the listing paper he signed on May 9 is never cancelled on screen. The scene where the villain's plan collapses is silent.
- Fix: (a) in ch06 or ch07 give Winnie a deliberate choice: she could cancel by phone but decides to let it stand ("A cancelled open house would make Tessa an enemy. A full lawn would make her a question."). (b) In ch08 have Greg speak: he says the line, to Bryce, in front of the neighbors: "Bryce, I'm withdrawing that listing agreement. My mother never signed it." Greg's turn needs to be spoken once, publicly.

### 7. Reckoning is private and mild; there is no moment where the neighbors tell the story
- Location: ch08, whole Saturday section.
- Problem: the formula requires a public, earned reckoning. Here Winnie says the line "softly... so that nobody else would have to hear", Bryce leaves with a plate of cabbage rolls, and Tessa is not challenged. The community arrives in a paragraph and then does nothing except eat. The emotional payoff of the whole piece (Walt's kindness acknowledged in public) is never given a voice. The Saturday section is only about 700 words before Sunday begins.
- Fix: add one short scene on the lawn: Hilda, from her folding chair, tells the crowd, with Tessa standing there, about the three days of humming on her roof; Dewey and Ila add one sentence each. Winnie says nothing about money. Then Bryce's "Is this a block party?" becomes a real turn. Keep Tessa's embarrassment gentle (she should feel what her tidiness cost, without a villain's comeuppance). About 400 to 500 extra words can come from compressing the repeated Monday retelling in ch04 (item 9).

### 8. Ruined cards: neighbors cook from cards that are mush
- Location: ch04 end ("two hundred and twelve cards lay inside in a gray soup, ink bleeding off every one"); ch05 ("The ink had run on nearly all of them"); ch08 ("Every dish had come off one of my cards"); ch08 final paragraph (card in the tin).
- Problem: the cards are ruined, yet in ch08 nobody mentions it, and the dishes are said to come from "my cards". The listener who cared about the tin never learns what happened to the recipes. This is the cheapest payoff in the story and it is missing.
- Fix: on Saturday each neighbor brings a fresh handwritten copy of their own card (Hilda's violet ink again). Then the "route" is rebuilt in a minute of the best warm material available. Tessa can offer to laminate the new cards (you already use lamination at the end).

### 9. Chapter 4 retells chapter 1 almost word for word
- Location: ch01 "On Monday, the day before the dump..." paragraph vs ch04 "On Monday morning a truck backed a big green rolloff dumpster..." through "...and I went to bed." Duplicated beats: Hilda's, soup, "being told I was thin", "I do not open cupboards for a living" (ch01 "because I do not open cupboards for a living"; ch04 "I did not open a cupboard, because I do not open cupboards for a living"), the receipt under the magnet ("Tuesday morning you already know").
- Problem: about 350 words repeated; the listener hears the same joke twice within 20 minutes.
- Fix: in ch01 cut the Monday paragraph down to "On Monday a dumpster appeared in my driveway and I was sent to Hilda's for the day", keep the full scene only in ch04, and delete the second "do not open cupboards" joke. Use the freed words for items 6 to 8.

### 10. Why did Winnie not see the empty shelf Monday evening?
- Location: ch01, "I went to bed without looking in a single cupboard, because I do not open cupboards for a living" vs "I turned on the light over the stove and looked up at the shelf where the green tin had sat for forty years. There was a clean square in the dust".
- Problem: an open shelf over the stove in a kitchen she cooks nothing in, in a house that "echoed" and was "spotless", on the evening after Hilda told her "Go home and look in your cupboards". The joke defends a cupboard, but the tin sat on a shelf. A heroine who is "smart, not lucky" would look at the tin first, particularly after the canisters, tags and the brochures. Also "dust" on a shelf of a "spotless" kitchen.
- Fix: have Tessa put something in its place (a row of her white baskets with labels, a basket of dried flowers) so the shelf looks full at supper, and that is why Winnie notices nothing; then at 4:55 she lifts the basket and sees the clean square. Alternatively let Winnie look, find it present (Tessa had not taken it yet; the tin goes into the dumpster Monday night or early Tuesday by Greg's hand). Also remove "Hilda told me to look in the cupboards and I did not" or explain it ("I was afraid she was right").

### 11. Money in the Route Fund does not add up; account ownership
- Location: ch06, "The deposits ran down the column in small amounts, fifteen dollars, forty, one hundred and twenty, going back almost nineteen years... The last line gave a balance. It was eighteen thousand four hundred dollars." Also "in the name of Walter Brandvold".
- Problem: (a) Small repair fees cannot both fund a full farmhouse reroof (2009), a furnace, a well pump, a wheelchair ramp, a bell rope and a shelter and still leave 18,400 dollars. (b) The ledger's first entry is the Hilda roof in two thousand nine (ch06: "The very first line, and the longest, read Hilda Voss, mile eleven, roof, two thousand nine"), while the passbook goes back "almost nineteen years" (about 2006) and ch07 says "For nineteen years, nineteen households"; two thousand nine to this May is sixteen years. Bible says 2004. (c) An account in the sole name of a man who died fourteen months ago normally cannot simply be "spent" by his widow; one line is needed.
- Fix: (a) say in ch06 that the passbook shows only what was left after the jobs, and that the lumberyard and the hardware store had charged Walt at cost for years (or that he also put in his summer pay), or lower the balance to a smaller sum and keep the idea. (b) Make all years agree: first ledger entry 2006 and first dot Hilda's roof 2009 are not both possible "first line", so either the first ledger line is not Hilda or the span is "sixteen years" everywhere. (c) Add "Walt had put my name on the beneficiary line, in pencil, in the margin."

### 12. Mile marker and box count in ch07 are impossible
- Location: ch07, "I counted two hundred and thirty-eight before I turned onto Elm Row, which is the closest I had come in thirty years to finishing the route."
- Problem: Route Three is sixty-one miles with about two hundred and forty boxes. Winnie drove only from mile marker eleven to mile marker twenty-two (Hilda, Dewey, Lund twins, shelter at nineteen, Ila at twenty-two) and then home. Counting 238 boxes from there home is impossible, and "thirty years" does not match a career that ended five years ago.
- Fix: count a smaller number tied to what she drove ("ninety-one before Elm Row") or say she kept counting out of habit and "lost count somewhere past the grain elevator", and delete "closest in thirty years". Alternatively drop the line (a gimmick used already in ch01).

### 13. Dangling objects and threads
- ch05: "inside it were three index cards and a folded sheet of lined paper". Only the sheet is ever quoted. What do the three index cards say? Either quote them (a good place for a short Walt joke or the recipe for the crisp) or cut them.
- ch03: "On the lathe, someone had added a yellow sticky note that said Sold, pickup Tuesday." and "The lathe sold in an hour. A man wants the table saw too." Never resolved: did a buyer take the lathe on May 6? Is the table saw still there? One sentence in ch06 would close it (for example, Winnie called the lathe buyer, refunded him, and he left a note).
- ch02: Winnie buys back five shirts at the thrift store the next day but never goes for Walt's twelve mugs ("They went in the donation box"); not mentioned again. Suggestion: Tessa brings the mugs back (she bought them back from the church thrift) in ch08, which doubles as her concrete apology.
- ch03: "We could drive over Saturday" (Willow Pointe visit); nothing happens on Saturday.

### 14. Repeated and misplaced facts
- ch02, "She simply had no idea that for fourteen months the cedar and the machine oil had been the only part of the house that still talked back." The same chapter two paragraphs earlier says "I had not touched any of it in ten months", and the scene is January (Walt died in March of the previous year). Fix: "for ten months" or "for the better part of a year".
- ch01, "in forty-three years of marriage". Greg is forty-six. Fix: "forty-eight years of marriage" (Winnie married about twenty-two).
- ch06, "tag" mismatch: "I sold it to the man from Ames for sixty dollars, which was more than Tessa's tag", but the band saw never appears among the tagged tools in ch03 (table saw, planer, lathe, brace and bit). Add band saw to ch03's list.
- ch06, dots versus households: ch06 lists "A rope for the church bell, and a shelter for the school bus stop at mile marker nineteen" among nineteen dots and ch07 has her sit in the bus shelter; but each dot is a card with "a name and a porch", and ch07 says "nineteen households". A bell rope and a shelter are not households. Fix: name the family behind each (the sexton's family, the farm family whose children wait at nineteen) and change "households" to "porches"; or say two dots are shared.

### 15. Name and detail overlap with earlier episodes (variety rule)
- Tessa: E004 villain is stepdaughter Tessa Kessler, 41, whose mother is dead; E007 Tessa is also 41 with a dead mother (Carol). Same given name, age and wound within three episodes.
- Winnie: E004 has Winifred "Winnie" Gault (friend, 70). E007 narrator is Winifred "Winnie" Brandvold, 70.
- Surnames Voss (Dale Voss in E004, Hilda Voss in E007) and Okafor (Imani Okafor E005, Del Okafor E007); Greg (E003 Gregory Pruitt); late husbands who hum in both E004 (Ray hummed hymns) and E007 (Walt hums, same hymn on the roof).
- Fix: rename at minimum Tessa (and ideally Winnie or Hilda Voss), and change one of the two humming husbands (Walt could whistle, or tap a pencil). Style guide also asks for names with different initial letters: Walt and Winnie both start with W.

### 16. Ending device repeats E006
- Location: ch08, "That is how it began, the Tuesday Route" (the ritual that closes the story).
- Problem: E006 closes on "every Tuesday" mending table at the church hall (a weekly Tuesday ritual after a reconciliation). Same structure in consecutive episodes.
- Fix: change the day (see item 5) and make the new ritual something different in kind (for example the route is driven with a delivery of one dish each week, not a table).

### 17. Voice-readiness
- ch02, "Flour, Sugar, Coffee, Tea, Pasta, and Misc. I read the last one twice." and "a thing labeled Misc." "Misc" is an abbreviation (rule: no abbreviations at all); the synthetic voice may say "misk" and pause after the period. Fix: "Odds and Ends" or "Miscellaneous", and then "Winnie reads the last one twice".
- ch05, `"P.S. Card one hundred seventeen..."`. "P.S." is an abbreviation and may be read letter by letter with pauses. Fix: "And down at the bottom... : Postscript. Card one hundred seventeen..." or "And one more thing. Card one hundred seventeen...".
- ch03, Tessa's broken line: "Things like that, they get so, they just." A voice will read it as a stumble; it is a good character moment but unclear when spoken. Fix: "Things like that, they get so heavy, they just fall." (or end with "They just." as a separate short sentence).
- ch01, the three "let me" chapter openings (ch01 "so let me start with the route", ch02 "so let me take you back to the winter", ch04 "so let me do it in order") sound templated. Vary one or two.

## Nice to have

### Pacing and structure
1. ch07 (minutes 45 to 53) has no antagonist or ticking clock. Tessa disappears from the end of ch06 until Saturday morning, and the chapter is a string of four similar beats (visit, memory, "I'll bring X"). Suggest one tension beat: Greg calls Thursday night to say Tessa has put a deposit down on a Willow Pointe unit, or Bryce phones to confirm "twelve agents", or Tessa herself pulls up at Hilda's. Also vary the shape of the visits (Lund twins are told in one summary sentence; give one visit a surprise, not four consent scenes).
2. Ch05 and ch06 hooks are weaker than ch01 to ch04. Ch05's ends on a recap (see item 3). Ch06's "I had eighteen thousand four hundred dollars, nineteen dots, and three days" is good, but a question (will the neighbors even come, what will Tessa do) would be stronger.
3. Mystery thread: the dots appear for the first time at minute 33 in ch05. Show Winnie noticing a tiny pencil dot on one card in ch02 or ch03 when she mentally "reads" the tin and dismissing it. Payoff arrives with extra force.
4. Ch07 opening "Hilda... in 2009" scene is the emotional best of the script; the humming idea (three days, four bars) should be seeded earlier (a word in ch01 about Walt's humming already exists; give the song a name or a tune, so the hymn could be hummed by Winnie at the end).

### Plausibility
5. ch03: Tessa's explanation "He's hauling for his brother until the middle of the month, but he put forty dollars down to hold it" is confusing, and a stranger paying three hundred dollars for a locked toolbox "for the steel" is odd. Simplify to "a man from Ames wants it for the shop, three hundred dollars, forty down, pickup the fourteenth".
6. ch06: the buyer says "The lady said everything out here was an estate cleanup... the owner had passed and the family wanted it gone". This is Tessa selling property she does not own while the owner is alive and has refused in writing or speech. Fine for a story in which Tessa is afraid, not wicked, but have Tessa react to it ("I told him it was Walt's estate. I did not say it was yours") or have Greg, not Tessa, be the one who gave the buyer the date. As it stands, listeners may find Tessa closer to a thief than the "afraid, not greedy" character in the bible.
7. ch05: the key "taped inside the lid" cannot "slide across the lid and clink" unless the tape failed. Add "the wet had finally loosened the tape".
8. ch05/ch01: Greg arrives at the dump after reading the kitchen note: "The kitchen light was on when I got there." He lives ninety minutes away; Winnie left the house at about five. For him to reach the house, then the dump, and stand ten minutes after seven, he must have left West Des Moines before four thirty. Give him a reason (he had not slept after Monday, Tessa told him at midnight what was in the dumpster) or say he stayed in town Monday night. Also, "yellow rain jacket still creased from the store" implies an early-morning shopping stop; one clause explains it.
9. ch07, Hilda: "I turned my hearing aid off so I wouldn't spoil it" contradicts "I heard humming". Fix: "I turned the hearing aid up so I wouldn't miss it" or have her describe feeling the tune through the roof.
10. ch06 passbook: the Route Fund "never on a whim", but ch06 also shows Winnie selling a band saw (after saying in ch03 "I am not selling Walt's tools"). Fine as humor, but make it a visible choice, to be consistent.
11. Brand check: "Prairie Home Realty" (echo of a well-known radio show title and probably a real company name), "Willow Pointe" and "Clear Space by Tessa" sound like real businesses. Style guide asks for explicitly invented, rare names; consider "Larkspur Home Realty" or similar, "Willow Pointe" to something rarer.

### Repetition and style
12. "Hilda... You're thin" repeated three times (ch01, ch04, ch08). The ch08 callback is good; the first two are the same joke twice in twenty minutes. Cut one (see Must fix 9).
13. "Pond" simile twice (ch03 "quiet the way a pond is quiet before a front moves in"; ch08 "calm as a pond"). Dog-yard / dog behind a fence images are used in ch02, ch03, ch07; the hound is the best, the others thin out. "I do not hurry" and "without a word" (ch02, ch05 twice) recur.
14. "Walt had written to me from beyond the grave" (ch05) is a stock phrase; also "the first thing he told me was that I couldn't cook" is not accurate: the P.S. is the last line of the letter. Fix: "the last thing he told me" or "the closing line".
15. ch07, "twin sentences finishers" ("who are sixty-four, farm the same quarter section, and finish each other's sentences") and "a face a man makes when handed a package he assumed belonged to somebody else" are stock; the second is good, the first is a cliche.
16. ch07, a small moral summary ("every one of them thought they were the only one... carried a secret kindness around the county") is mildly explanatory. It is short and earned; consider cutting the last sentence of that paragraph.
17. Narrator's insistence on being fair to Tessa ("I want you to know that", "I want to be fair to her", "I will give her credit") appears three times in ch01 and ch02. Keep the first, cut the others.

### Characters
18. Greg vanishes from Thursday to Saturday and his apology is three sentences in a summary paragraph ("He said he had been so busy handling things that he had forgotten to ask a single one"). Give him one line of dialogue in the party scene and a real first-person action (see Must fix 6).
19. Tessa's apology: she says "I couldn't", never "I'm sorry". Winnie's forgiveness being partial is right, but the listener waits for Tessa to say the word and for one concrete act (the twelve mugs, a re-lettered label "Misc" thrown away, the label maker left in the glove box is already a nice touch).
20. Villain not a cartoon: well handled (ch03 newspaper moment, ch06 "Her voice shook, just a little"). The seed for Carol is good; consider making the Sunday confession less expository ("I read labels for a whole year afterward" is strong; "I cannot see a full shelf without feeling that something is going to fall" says what the story already showed).

## Overall score: 7 / 10

Warm, distinctive voice and setting, a likeable non-villain, a lovely central object, and clean voice formatting. Held back by a soft first minute relative to the title, a flabby ch02 to ch03 stretch with no open question, a manufactured toolbox scare, and a Saturday climax that is private and short.

## The one change that would most improve retention

Rebuild the first minute and the Saturday scene around the same object: in ch01 name card 117 and the unknown "something behind it" so the listener spends the next half hour waiting for the letter, and in ch08 let the neighbors bring fresh handwritten copies of their ruined cards and tell Walt's story out loud while Tessa stands on the lawn and Greg ends the listing in public. That gives the first ten minutes a question and the last ten minutes the public, earned payoff the formula requires.
