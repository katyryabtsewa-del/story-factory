# E003 — Story bible

Story year: 2026 (never state the year in narration). Engagement announced in January; the wedding is Saturday, September nineteenth (September 19, 2026 is a Saturday). Nora tells the story looking back from the following November, the week before Thanksgiving. 8 named characters, no more.

## Characters
**Nora Pruitt** — narrator, 67. Widow. Born and raised in Madison, Wisconsin. With her husband Sam she ran Pruitt's Table, a family restaurant on Willow Street, for thirty-four years (Friday fish fry, Sunday pot roast, brown butter apple kringle, cheese curd gravy fries). Sold the building nine years ago when Sam got sick. Calm, dry humor, tidy, never raises her voice, keeps every receipt. Habit: smooths the tablecloth with the flat of her hand before she says something important. Wears Sam's old wristwatch, too big, on a leather band. Cooks from a recipe box of index cards in her late mother-in-law's handwriting.

**Sam Pruitt** — late husband, died eight years ago, at sixty-one, of a long illness (do not dwell on it). Worked the grill, hummed Frank Sinatra, called every customer "chief." Sold the restaurant "so we could have our last good year" and made Nora promise to keep cooking.

**Gregory Pruitt** — son, 39. Loan officer at a bank in Madison. Grew up doing homework in the restaurant's back booth; was teased at school for smelling like fryer oil ("Fish Fry Greg"); has been ashamed of it for thirty years and never said so. Neat, nervous, wants to be admired by the Ashfords. Knows exactly who Marco is (Marco taught him to crack an egg at nine) and that Corso is Marco's; he never told Courtney, and hopes Marco will stay silent. Calls her "Mom" in a patient voice, as if talking to a customer. Lives in a new condo with Courtney. Not cruel by nature, weak when pressured.

**Courtney Ashford** — Gregory's fiancée, 31. Event-marketing manager. Polished, fast-talking, curates everything (a wedding hashtag, a color palette called "sea salt and sage"). Says "elevated" and "authentic" a lot, never means either. Calls Nora "Nora, sweetheart," and uses "cute" as an insult. Her friends are "foodies"; she wants a wedding nobody could call "homespun."

**Richard Ashford** — Courtney's father, 64. Owns a regional chain of hardware stores (Ashford Hardware, thirty-one stores). Large, plain-spoken, started as a stock boy, eats seconds, hates fuss. Paying for the wedding. Quietly notices how Gregory and Courtney treat Nora.

**Marco Reyes** — chef and owner of Corso, the best restaurant in Madison, and of Corso Events, the catering company. 49. Started at Pruitt's Table at nineteen as a dishwasher; Nora taught him to cook; he ran her kitchen for fifteen years. Warm, loud in the kitchen, tender with Nora, calls her "Chef" or "Missus Pruitt" (when he wants a favor) When she sold the building nine years ago, Nora invested one hundred eighty thousand dollars (the sale proceeds, after the debts) to help him open Corso and received forty percent of Corso Hospitality LLC in writing; he insisted on every paper. Nora has never once attended a Corso event as an owner; she takes a yearly check, goes to the owners' meeting in January, and eats in the restaurant's back booth.

**Priya Nair** — Corso Events manager, 34. Efficient, kind, handles the Ashford contract; has a clipboard and a tablet; recognizes Nora from the photo on the wall of the Corso office (young Marco and Nora in front of Pruitt's Table).

**Edith Larkin** — Nora's neighbor and oldest friend, 71, retired church secretary, large reading glasses, blunt, brings lemon bars to every crisis. Knows every detail.

## Money
- Nora's sale proceeds invested in Corso: one hundred eighty thousand dollars. Her share: forty percent of Corso Hospitality LLC (restaurant plus catering). Her yearly check last year: about ninety-four thousand dollars.
- House gift she planned for Gregory and Courtney: forty thousand dollars toward a house, in a check in a card. At the end she keeps it and starts the Pruitt's Table Scholarship at Madison College with it, for kids who want to cook.
- Wedding: about one hundred eighty guests. Corso's contract: about ninety-one thousand dollars, paid by Richard Ashford.

## Places and details
- Madison, Wisconsin. Nora's house: a brick bungalow on Spaight Street with a yellow kitchen and a huge old stove (a 1962 six-burner Wedgewood) Sam rebuilt.
- Pruitt's Table, Willow Street: closed nine years ago; now a bike shop. The back booth where Gregory did homework.
- Corso: restaurant on Williamson Street, brick, copper pans. Corso Events office has a photo wall.
- Wedding venue: Harbor Point Estate, a lake house with a lawn and a white tent on Lake Mendota (invented, do not describe real places).
- The engagement lunch: Courtney's choice, a bright restaurant called Verdant (generic, invented), in March.
- Table fourteen: next to the swinging kitchen doors.
- Contract clause (section nine): "No outside food or beverage. Family members of the couple may not enter service or kitchen areas." Added by Courtney herself.
- Signature dishes: brown butter apple kringle, Friday fish fry beer-battered cod with potato pancakes, cheese curd gravy fries (the "Sunday supper gravy"), pot roast with horseradish cream, sour cream raisin pie. Marco's wedding menu includes (without announcing the source) a pot roast "Wisconsin braised short rib," Nora's brown butter kringle as the dessert course renamed "Brown Butter Apple Tart," sour cream raisin pie bites at the late-night station.

## Timeline
- January: engagement announced at Gregory's condo; Nora cries and brings a pie.
- March: engagement lunch at Verdant; Nora offers to make the wedding cake and a rehearsal dinner. Courtney: "It would be a little… embarrassing, honestly. Our guests are very food-literate." Gregory says nothing, then follows up with a text that evening: "Mom, please don't bring food to the wedding. Not the kringle either. It's just not that kind of wedding. I hope you understand." Courtney asks her to "just say you worked in food service" if asked.
- April: Nora at the invitation stage; table fourteen assigned; Gregory phones to ask about her knees and stairs ("the house must be a lot for you"). Nora keeps the card with the forty thousand dollar check in the drawer under the telephone; only Edith knows about it. Gregory never knows about the money until the terrace.
- First Sunday in May (May third): supper at Nora's. Courtney steps out to the porch; Gregory, low, tells Nora the caterer is Corso Events, "Marco's place", and asks her to leave the old days out of it (Courtney does not know). Nora does not promise.
- Monday, May fourth: Nora meets Marco and Priya at Corso (back booth); clause nine shown. Nora: "Don't change one thing." Marco gives his word to say nothing, with one condition: if anyone makes a fool of Nora in front of her guests, he may do as he sees fit. Nora will not ask it and does not expect it.
- August: tasting. Courtney and Gregory taste at Corso without Nora; Marco serves the menu built from Pruitt's Table recipes and lets Courtney approve them thinking they are "his." Gregory knows the gravy at once, goes white, says only "thank you for doing this" to Marco and, when Courtney steps away, begs him with one word ("please"); Marco tells him to eat his dinner. That night Nora takes the check out of the card (into the drawer) and writes the pan gravy recipe and one line on a fresh index card, which goes into the card.
- Saturday, September nineteenth: the wedding. (Monday before: Richard phones Nora about the dress and the seating chart; Gregory phones fishing whether she has talked to anyone about the catering.)
- November: Thanksgiving; Nora cooks.

## Wedding day
Ceremony at four on the lawn. Cocktail hour. Dinner at six: first course, beet and burrata salad; second, "Wisconsin braised short rib" (Nora's pot roast); dessert, "Brown Butter Apple Tart" (the kringle). At the head table Gregory smells the short rib, recognizes it, goes pale and does not eat it. His toast is a panicked cover-up: "And to my mother, Nora, who for once let the professionals do the cooking." Laughter from Courtney's friends. To control things he then invites Marco out for a bow. Marco takes the microphone, uses Nora's one condition ("the professional is sitting at table fourteen") and tells them everything. Richard Ashford stands first. Guests ask Nora for recipes. Courtney leaves the head table; Gregory follows. Later, outside, Gregory says: "I'm sorry." Nora: "I know you are. That's not the same as being finished."

The kitchen is in the big house behind the tent; a covered canvas walkway joins it to the tent, and the swinging doors are in the tent wall where the walkway meets it.

## Ending (November)
Courtney brought a store-bought pie and a handwritten apology note about "cute." Gregory takes the Saturday dish shift at Nora's table every other week. Nora hosts Thanksgiving for fourteen; Richard brings a case of mustard and a folding table; Courtney peels potatoes; Gregory eats the kringle and cries a little. Nora's scholarship; Saturday classes at Corso for people over fifty-five ("Pruitt's Table Saturdays"); Friday fish fry returns to Corso for one night a month.

## Additions made while writing (ch. 1-8)
- Gregory believes Nora lent Marco "a small sum, long repaid"; he never knew about the forty percent or the yearly check.
- Marco started at Pruitt's Table in the fall of nineteen ninety-six; Sam witnessed the Corso papers at the kitchen table. Marco was in Spain most of April and Priya signed the Ashford contract alone, so Priya spotted "Missus Nora Pruitt, mother of the groom" on the family list; she and Nora meet for the first time in late May.
- Contract clause nine: Priya's margin note "Added at client's request, Courtney Ashford". At the wedding Priya points out that section nine binds the client, not Corso, and the kitchen door is open to Nora as an owner. Nora's share of profit on the Ashford contract goes to the cooks' bonus pool.
- Courtney's own backstory: Richard's daughter, teased in school as one of "the hardware people"; she wrote clause nine herself.
- Table fourteen companions: two of Gregory's bank colleagues and a quiet cousin of Richard's (all unnamed, to keep the cast at eight). Courtney's mother appears unnamed in the front row.
- The reveal: Gregory himself invites Marco to the microphone ("Come take a bow!"); Marco uses the condition Nora gave him and says so. Nora's three sentences are in ch. 6. Marco says he has known Gregory since Gregory was nine.
- At the terrace Nora gives the couple the card without the check (she bought the card in January; the check came out in August): inside is her pan gravy recipe card and the line "When you want to learn the real thing, come to Spaight Street on a Saturday and bring an apron." That line replaces Sam's note "Sam would have wanted you to have a kitchen". She tells them of the check there; Gregory never knew. The forty thousand dollars goes to the Pruitt's Table Scholarship at Madison College, signed Monday after the wedding.
- Narration is on the Sunday before Thanksgiving, so Thanksgiving dinner for fourteen is still ahead; Richard has already delivered the mustard and folding table, Courtney is due Wednesday to peel potatoes. The kringle crying scene happened "last Saturday" at a Pruitt's Table Saturdays class at Corso (classes began in October; twenty-two students; Gregory washes dishes every other Saturday). First-Friday fish fry at Corso also began in October.
