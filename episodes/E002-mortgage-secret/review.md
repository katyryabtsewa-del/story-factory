# E002 review: "For Six Years I Secretly Paid My Son's Mortgage"

Reviewer: story editor, fresh read of idea, bible, outline, ch01 to ch08, style guide, story formula, and E001 idea (plus E001 ch12 for phrase overlap).
Chapters were not edited.

## Lint (tools/lint_script.py)
- Total 9,477 words, about 61 minutes. All chapters are 1,147 to 1,196 words, so length is on target.
- No banned cliches, no digits, no dashes, no ellipses, no abbreviations found.
- Repeated five-word phrases (three times or more):
  - "one hundred sixty-eight thousand five hundred" (three times)
  - "the house at forty-one Tamarack Court" (three times)
  - "rubbed the back of his neck" (three times)
  - "sat at my kitchen table" (three times)
- The money and place repeats are acceptable. The neck rub is the only one worth varying (see Optional).

## What works (short)
- The cold open is strong. By about word 70 we have the humiliation, and by about word 230 the one-line promise.
- The hooks at the end of ch1 (the plaque), ch3 (the Bernadette text), ch4 (the folder) and ch5 (bring the green books) all land.
- The sticky note is at about minute 35, a proper midpoint turn.
- Cassidy gets a human beat in ch7 ("what was left looked younger and frightened").
- "I am making it a thing" is a good callback.
- Legal detail mostly stays in general terms.
- Names have distinct initials. The bible money figures are arithmetically correct: 72 x 800 = 57,600; 72 x 1,600 = 115,200; 28,000 + 19,500 + 5,800 = 53,300; total 168,500.

## Required fixes

### R1. What Cassidy knew contradicts itself (ch3, ch5, ch7)
- **Problem:**
  - The sticky note says "Get M to sign before the Fourth. Don't let her read it, she'll make it a thing." That proves Cassidy knows Marlene's signature is needed, so she knows Marlene's name is on the paper.
  - Yet in ch7 she says "That's not possible. Trevor said it was a loan, and a little family help," and "you tricked us into thinking we owned it."
  - The bible says she believes Trevor's job plus "family help" bought the house. A broker would also have run a title search on a house she supposedly thought was theirs.
  - Plausibility hole: a business owner would not go six years without ever seeing a mortgage statement, a tax bill or an insurance bill.
- **Fix (keeps the bible):**
  - Make her willfully incurious rather than ignorant.
  - Plant one line, in ch3 (after Trevor's silence about the deed) or in ch5, that Trevor told Cassidy his mother is "on the paperwork for now, because of my credit, and helped with the down payment." Cassidy never asked for more.
  - Rewrite the ch7 line so it names that gap. For example: "You said she was on the paper for my credit. You said she helped." Marlene's reply "Nobody tricked you. Nobody told you" then becomes sharper.
  - Cassidy stays a person who chose not to look, not a cartoon.

### R2. The refinance trap does not add up, and Marlene never asks the bookkeeper's question (ch5, ch6)
- **Problem:**
  - Quote: "Marlene Dunmore appeared nowhere on it, except as a signature at the bottom of one particular page." A new loan of two hundred sixty thousand dollars would have to pay off Marlene's existing loan, and nobody mentions it.
  - Trevor's cover story, "we put the deed back," is never tested with "And my mortgage?"
  - Naomi says the deed hands everything over "for nothing in return." That is slightly off, because her loan would be paid off. What she would lose is the equity.
  - Cassidy's cards are "A few cards. It's not a lot," but the bible has forty-one thousand dollars.
- **Fix:**
  - In ch5, let Marlene find the broker's closing worksheet and reconcile it line by line: two hundred sixty thousand minus a one hundred ninety-eight thousand payoff leaves sixty-two thousand. Forty-one thousand of that goes to credit cards. The rest is the studio and fees.
  - Then she says plainly that the house is worth about three hundred ninety thousand, so she would lose about one hundred ninety-two thousand dollars of equity plus the ninety thousand she put down.
  - Adjust Naomi in ch6: "nothing in that paper pays you for your equity."
  - This is the "paperwork read aloud" beat done properly. It also makes the heroine smart, not lucky.

### R3. Cold-open promise versus what actually happens to the payments (ch1, ch6, ch7, ch8)
- **Problem:**
  - ch1: "By the first week of September, I would stop paying for that house."
  - ch7: "the rent that has been called groceries will be called rent until then." Rent stays at an unstated amount, so it is unclear whether she keeps covering the sixteen hundred until December.
  - ch8: "His rent came every month after that, and it was never labeled groceries again." This implies the same eight hundred.
  - The notice has no stated effective date or length, and it conflicts with a ninety-day closing and with Bernadette's October first date.
  - A bookkeeper would not stop paying her own mortgage and wreck her credit, so "stop paying" needs a precise meaning.
- **Fix:**
  - In ch7, one sentence: "From the first of October, the rent is the real cost of the house, twenty-four hundred dollars, and it is paid to me." Use the full monthly cost, not "market rent," so the figures stay in the bible.
  - Name the notice period in plain words. For example, say it gives them until the end of the ninety days, and avoid statutes.
  - Reword the ch1 promise to "I would stop subsidizing that house."

### R4. The reckoning is private, and ch1 promised otherwise (ch1, ch7, ch8)
- **Problem:**
  - ch1: "the people who laughed on that patio, or the ones who mattered most among them, would hear every single number, out loud and in order."
  - In ch7 the audience is Trevor, Cassidy and Bernadette at Marlene's table. None of the thirty party guests ever hears a number.
  - The only public correction is a summary sentence in ch8: "she posted a long piece called 'A Year of Real Numbers.'" That is told, not shown.
  - The formula wants the reckoning "public and earned."
  - The setting also overlaps E001, where the reckoning was the family at her own kitchen table with papers read aloud.
- **Fix, either option:**
  - Option A. Add a short scene in ch8, replacing the posting summary, where Cassidy corrects the record in person to the same kind of audience. Example: at the post-closing gathering, or at a neighborhood event, she says to the woman with the white wine: "Marlene is the reason we have a house." One or two lines, in a warm key.
  - Option B. Let Odessa and Naomi sit in at ch7, and soften the ch1 promise to "the people who mattered."
  - Option A is better for retention.

### R5. Ch7's reading repeats ch2 almost word for word (ch2, ch7)
- **Problem:**
  - ch2 ("Here is how the numbers worked") already gives every figure: ninety thousand down, two hundred twenty-two thousand mortgage, two thousand four hundred, eight hundred "groceries," twenty-eight thousand suite, nineteen thousand five hundred roof, five thousand eight hundred furnace.
  - ch7 re-lists all of them, and ch6 states the total again. By ch7 the listener has heard "one hundred sixty-eight thousand five hundred" three times.
  - The payoff scene therefore delivers no new information. Even the writer's own "total at the end of ch2" choice is a hook, but it costs the reveal here.
- **Fix:**
  - Keep ch2's total. In ch7, cut the recap to one or two sentences, for example "You have heard the arithmetic, so I'll give you the dates."
  - Then read what the listener has not heard: ledger notes and dates like "Garage code changed. Not by me," the February entry beside Cassidy's "Home is where WE built it," the equity (about one hundred ninety-two thousand), and the closing worksheet from R2.

### R6. Marlene's long passivity is unexplained, and the quiet-plan stretch has no jeopardy (ch3, ch6)
- **Problem:**
  - ch3: Odessa tells her "You are the bank." Marlene's own reply is "In April I did nothing at all... I gave a very good impression of a woman who was being patient."
  - She was also told to clear the suite she owns (ch3, the May text) and still takes no step until the folder arrives in ch5.
  - Then ch6 compresses July and August into a montage ("Through July and August, I went to Sunday dinner every week"). Nothing threatens the plan. The formula asks that the quiet plan carry tension that "can fall apart."
  - The two-month wait between consulting Naomi and signing the notice also has no stated reason.
  - Motivation is off in ch6: after Marlene refuses to sign and Cassidy "lost her deposit," Cassidy "grew warmer and warmer, the way a person does who thinks she has won." That does not follow.
- **Fix:**
  - Give her a reason. In ch3, after Trevor's "Soon," add that she gave him until the end of June to tell Cassidy himself. The party was the last Saturday in June, so the deadline and the humiliation coincide.
  - In ch6, give the July and August wait one reason from Naomi, for example an appraisal first and a notice that lands at the start of a month.
  - Add one real pressure beat in July or August. For example, the broker phones Marlene directly, or Bernadette "casually" asks her to sign at Sunday dinner, or Cassidy comes back with a new, friendlier paper. Marlene almost hums at the table and has to cover it.
  - Fix Cassidy's warmth to match, for example "Cassidy grew sweeter, the way a person does when she means to ask again."

### R7. Bernadette's thread contradicts itself and is never resolved (ch6, ch7, ch8)
- **Problem:**
  - ch6: Marlene tells Trevor "Bernadette should hold off on moving into the basement suite." That tips her hand and breaks Naomi's "You tell no one."
  - ch7: Bernadette still says "I gave notice on my apartment. They're renting it to somebody else on the first of October."
  - ch8: she appears only as "Bernadette still has opinions about my kitchen." Where she lives is never said.
  - Also in ch8, Cassidy "went to stay with Bernadette for three weeks," which is past the October first date.
- **Fix:**
  - Cut the ch6 "hold off" line. Let the move slide to "September" because the paperwork is not finished, so Bernadette's notice for October first makes sense and the stakes in ch7 are real.
  - In ch8, add one clause on her housing, for example that she kept her apartment because the landlord had not yet re-let it, or found a place near the school.

### R8. Small factual and continuity breaks
- **ch8, date:** "On the twenty-second day, a Tuesday." The meeting was a Saturday. Twenty-two days later is a Sunday, and a Tuesday would be day seventeen or twenty-four. Change to "twenty-fourth day."
- **ch3, the wooden train:**
  - It is "wrapped in a bedsheet" on her dining table, but a few paragraphs later she is on the step "with a train set in my car."
  - It is then never delivered until December (ch8), nine months after the birthday, with no explanation. Jonah never asks about it, even in the ch4 phone call.
  - ch8: "Underneath the engine, when I finished the sanding, I had found a line carved." But ch3 says she "spent the winter finishing" it, so she would have found the carving then.
  - Fix: put it in the car in ch3, say in ch4 or ch6 why she is holding the gift ("I kept it at home; a gift handed over a rekeyed door is not a gift"), and plant the underside before ch8, for example in ch3: "I had left the underside alone, where Gerald's pencil marks were."
- **ch7, four cups:** "a plate of lemon bars on the good china, four cups, and four clean spoons," but the opening says one of the three guests "had not been invited." Either she expected Bernadette ("four cups, because I had a feeling about Bernadette," which also makes Marlene sharper) or change to three.
- **ch7, Jonah:** Odessa has taken Jonah to the depot museum "with orders not to bring him back before five." Cassidy would not hand over her child the day after receiving a legal notice. Add one clause saying Trevor agreed, or have Marlene arrange it with Trevor on the phone.
- **ch8, Jonah:** Cassidy "went to stay with Bernadette for three weeks." Say where Jonah was (with Trevor, with Marlene on weekdays), or the line reads as abandonment.
- **ch8, closing numbers:**
  - "I walked out with a check for the rest, which came out very nearly even with what I had put in." The check is never stated.
  - Compute for the listener: three hundred sixty thousand minus one hundred ninety-eight thousand is one hundred sixty-two thousand, against "one hundred sixty-eight thousand five hundred." A careful listener will add the ninety thousand down payment and find a gap.
  - Fix: say "a check for one hundred sixty-two thousand dollars." Have Naomi or Marlene explain in one sentence what "even" means: the ninety thousand down, plus the principal paid down (about twenty-four thousand), plus the fifty-three thousand three hundred in improvements is about one hundred sixty-seven thousand, against one hundred sixty-two thousand. The rest of the payments were the cost of the roof over their heads.
- **ch7, cosigner and credit:**
  - "Trevor's credit has recovered enough to qualify" contradicts "with a cosigner who is not me."
  - Cassidy's forty-one thousand dollars of card debt is also unaddressed.
  - Suggested wording: "Trevor's credit has recovered most of the way, and with a cosigner who is not me it will qualify."
  - There is no mention of how the buyers find a down payment. One clause, such as "I will count part of the closing toward it," or a mortgage that covers the full price, would settle it.
- **ch8, the older brother:**
  - Cassidy's older brother cosigns at the closing ("looking as proud as a man at a christening"). He is introduced there for the first time.
  - Fix: seed him earlier with one clause, either in ch7 ("Cassidy has a brother, you know") or in the ch8 apology scene. Show Cassidy asking him, which is also her first humble act. Keep him unnamed, since the bible caps named characters at eight.

### R9. Real brand in a villain-adjacent role (ch2)
- **Quote:** "a calendar app called Family Hub."
- **Problem:** "Family Hub" is a real, well-known product name, and here it is the villain's tool. This runs against the rule on real brands and the style guide's note on invented names.
- **Fix:** Use an obviously invented name such as "Hearthline" or "Nestwise."

### R10. Voice-readiness
- **ch1:** "behind a trellis wound with white string lights." A synthetic voice may say "wownd." Use "wrapped in."
- **ch1 "It read:", ch3 "It read: 'Bernadette is moving...'", ch4 "It read, 'Extended family visits...'":** Past-tense "read" may be pronounced "reed." Use "It said."
- **"I read it twice":** it appears in ch1, ch4 and ch7. It is also a near-verbatim line from E001 ch12 ("I read it twice, and then I set the phone face down"). Vary two of the three.
- **ch2 and ch3:** "cul-de-sac." This is a common mispronunciation for synthetic voices. Use "the circle at the end of the street."
- **"Orchard Row":** the bible name, and "Row" can come out wrong. Leave it unless the voice QC flags it.
- **ch7, the number reading:** the one hundred sixty-eight thousand paragraph is a long list of figures by ear, hard to follow. R5 also fixes this.

## Optional

### O1. Repeated images and beats
- **The clock "since my wedding":** ch3 (struck the half hour), ch5 (ticked on the wall), ch7 ("the clock struck the quarter hour"). Keep one or two, not three.
- **The weather image:** ch3 uses "a sort of weather" and, a few lines later, "the way you follow a weather front." Cut one.
- **"the way ...":** the similes with "the way" are dense in ch3 and ch5 ("the way a boy slides a bad report card," "the way a salesman lays out samples," "the way you close a window on a bird"). Trim a couple.
- **"rubbed the back of his neck":** ch1, ch3, ch5. As a motif it is fine, but vary one.
- **Peaches:** ch1 (twice), ch3 (three times), ch8. Cut the ch1 "Eat a peach" or one of the ch3 mentions.
- **Purple:** Odessa's purple jacket (ch3) and Naomi's purple glasses (ch6, four times) are easy to confuse by ear.
- **Smiling "with every tooth":** appears in ch1 and ch3. Fine once, second time vary.

### O2. The late husband's note repeats an E001 beat (ch8)
- E001 uses a letter from the late husband, read aloud, as part of the payoff. E002 ends with a "note I had never seen" from Gerald under the ledger cover. It is also a convenient found letter with a slightly aphoristic line ("Pay attention to who brings the ladder").
- The "casserole and a ladder" motto is stated twice (ch4, ch8) but never shown.
- **Fix:** Replace the note, or add to it, with an action. In the epilogue Trevor arrives at her door with a ladder to clean her gutters and Cassidy sends a casserole. Keep the note if the owner likes it, but then drop the line "Pay attention to who brings the ladder."

### O3. Trevor's lie about the deed is never dealt with (ch5, ch8)
- In ch5 Trevor tells his mother to her face: "it's a technical step. Then the loan goes through, and then, you know, we put the deed back." That is an active lie, not just silence.
- In ch8 the midnight talk covers six years of shame but not that lie. Marlene forgives too smoothly.
- **Fix:** One exchange, for example: "And the folder, Trevor?" "I knew it wasn't right, Mom. I just didn't know how to say no to her either."

### O4. The dangling train drawing (ch6)
- Jonah's crayon train is labeled Mom, Dad and Gran, and "The engine... did not say anything at all." This is never paid off.
- **Fix:** In ch8, give the engine a label ("Grandpa"), or have Jonah add a new drawing.

### O5. Cassidy's turn is quick and slightly too articulate (ch8)
- The handwritten note ("I didn't know who I was without the house") reads like a therapist's summary, and it comes only twenty-four days later.
- **Fix:**
  - Roughen it. Make it shorter and clumsier, so it sounds like her and not a lesson.
  - Seed her insecurity earlier. For example, in ch4 let Bernadette mention that Cassidy grew up in "four rentals before she was twelve." That makes both Bernadette's drive and Cassidy's collapse earned, and keeps the villain from being a cartoon.

### O6. Pacing: ch2 backstory (about minutes 8 to 15)
- ch2 is almost all summary: the arrangement, six good years, the rolls, the code, the app. Each beat is short and vivid, but there is no present-time scene between the ch1 hook and ch3's key scene.
- **Fix:** Cut about a hundred words from the "good life" paragraph and give the Thanksgiving "rolls" call a few lines of live dialogue. Or move the January garage-code scene into a short present-time moment.
- The ch2 ending (the total) is the weakest hook of the eight. Add a one-line question ("and nobody knew what to do with it") or make the number sharper in some other way.

### O7. Smaller notes
- **ch6, the notice copy:** "with a copy to your son's work address." That is mildly punitive, and it undercuts "not an unkind word" and "Not a punishment." Cut the work copy.
- **ch6, Cassidy's lost deposit:** "Cassidy lost her deposit on the studio." Say how Marlene knows (Trevor told her).
- **ch5, the sticky note luck:** "Perhaps Trevor had missed it." This is one coincidence. It is earned by her reading every page, but one more line on why Cassidy attached it to the cover sheet (a note meant for Trevor) would help.
- **ch7, odd image:** "he stood at the edge of a diving board he had been standing on since he was nine years old." Mixed metaphor, and the later "stand up" repeats the idea. Simplify.
- **ch2, mortgage realism:** a two thousand four hundred dollar payment on a two hundred twenty-two thousand loan implies taxes and insurance over a thousand dollars a month. Plausible for some Michigan counties, and fine to leave as the bible has it. If the owner wants, add "taxes in this county being what they are."
- **ch4, guidelines count:** "There were eight of them," but the text lists only five. Cosmetic.
- **Legal detail:** staying "in general terms" is mostly met. Naomi's two sentences in ch6 are exactly right. Do not add statutes or notice periods by number.

## Check against rules
- **Fiction only:** no "true story" language in the text. The description needs the disclaimer at publish time.
- **Real people and brands:** only R9 ("Family Hub"). Buick, Kalamazoo area, Lake Superior and the Upper Peninsula are fine as positive or neutral settings.
- **Graphic content:** none.
- **Uniqueness against E001:**
  - Villain differs: a daughter-in-law and her mother, versus E001's son and daughter-in-law who forge a competency claim.
  - Humiliation differs: a public patio party versus E001's doctor's office.
  - Mechanism differs: title, ledger and a month-to-month notice versus a trust clause.
  - Overlaps worth softening: the reckoning at her own kitchen table with papers read, a note from the late husband, and a grandchild who tips her off. See R4 and O2.

## Pacing
- **Cold open:** hooks inside the first minute.
- **Chapter endings:** all eight end on a hook. The ch2 ending is the weakest (a number that she already knows).
- **Sags:** mild in ch2 (about minutes 8 to 13) and in the second half of ch6 (about minutes 41 to 46, the July and August montage).
- **Reckoning:** earned, but not public and not fresh (R4, R5).
- **Ending:** warm without preaching. Only the closing line of Gerald's note and "as a reminder of what a word on a sign can cost" come close to a moral, and both are mild.

## Writer's deliberate choices (assessment)
- **Notice signed at the end of August and couriered the first Friday of September:** fine. It makes the Saturday meeting tidy. Needs the wait explained (R6) and an effective date (R3).
- **Ledger total revealed at the end of ch2:** works as a hook. It costs ch7 unless ch7 gets new content (R5).
- **Unnamed older brother as cosigner:** workable and keeps the cast at eight. Needs a seed (R8).
- **Wooden train gift:** a good warm object. Needs the continuity fixes in R8.

## Overall score: 7 out of 10
The voice, the structure and the bookkeeper motif are good, and the text is nearly ready for narration. The main problems are logic (what Cassidy knows, the refinance mechanics, the unexplained passivity) and a reckoning that repeats ch2 in a private room.

## The one change that would most improve retention
Rebuild ch7 so it is the first time the listener learns something new and so a witness from the party hears it. Cut the recap to a couple of sentences, then read three things the listener has not heard: the broker's worksheet (her own mortgage paid off, sixty-two thousand left over, forty-one thousand of it to cards), the dated ledger notes beside Cassidy's posts, and the equity figure. Then let Cassidy publicly correct herself, in person, in ch8. That turns the cold-open promise ("every single number, out loud and in order") into a payoff instead of a repeat.
