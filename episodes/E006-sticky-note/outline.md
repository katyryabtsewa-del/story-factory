# E006 — Outline (8 chapters, ~1,150 words each; 60 min). Form B: a small mystery that opens on the sticky note and unwinds, with a quiet trap at the end.

Rules for the writer: first person, past tense, Josie telling it looking back from April. Keep humor dry and warm, a seamstress's eye for detail. Curtis is pleasant and never raises his voice; Nicole is loving and used, not cruel. No hidden-ownership trick, no lawyer reveal, no party scene, no bank scene. Chapters 2+ begin with one natural line that re-orients the listener. Do not use the word "Previously". The signature tick is introduced in chapter 2 and paid off in chapter 7. Use "Let's get ahead of it" at most five times total.

1. **The Sticky Note** — Cold open (150–300 words): the conference room at Teague Title and Notary, the pen in her hand, the stack Curtis calls "Medicare housekeeping," Nicole saying gently in front of strangers "Mom, your glasses are in your purse, remember?" Josie feels the yellow square under the clipboard clip. Promise in one line (in the voice: "By Friday morning every person in that room would know exactly what they had done, and I would not have raised my voice once.") Then the room: Gordon with his pen cap, Marisol at the copier not looking at her, Curtis patting her hand ("Josie, hon, it's initials on page four, let's get ahead of it"), Nicole with a worried face. She reads the note by tilting the clipboard. "I need my reading glasses," she says; "they're in the car." Nicole offers to get them; Josie says she'd like the air. In the Buick, in the freezing gray lot, she reads the back of the note. She has never been in that office before today. Ends: the note says "The first one wasn't you" and she realizes there is a first one, dated a day she can name to the hour.
2. **A Tick Under the R** — She goes back inside with the glasses she "found," says she's too tired to sign today and wants to read it all at home on Friday; Curtis's smile slips for half a second and comes back; Nicole is worried and relieved. Drive home (Nicole drives, they talk about soup). Who Josie is: Earl, thirty-eight years on the poles, Ledbetter Alterations, forty-one years of hems, the signature tick and the joke about knotting the thread. Curtis's small helpful things over the last month: mail forwarded "so you don't have to deal with it," the pill organizer, the thermostat, the "memory jokes" at church. Wave one. That night she sews by lamplight and counts what she knows. Ends: a knock at the sunroom door at seven the next morning, Marisol Vega in a gray parka holding a garment bag: "Mrs. Ledbetter, I need a hem."
3. **Marisol's Receipt** — Wednesday, January fourteenth. Josie lets Marisol in; pins a bridesmaid dress while the girl talks, because it is easier for her to talk to the top of Josie's head. Marisol's story: November twentieth, Curtis dictates a "durable power of attorney" with Josie's name; Gordon stamps it the twenty-first, writes the journal entry himself two days later, "personally known," date back-dated to November eighteenth, two-fifteen; nobody named Ledbetter came in on the eighteenth; Marisol was at the front desk. She saw the signature and thought something was wrong with it. The wedding folder: she takes out a three-year-old alterations receipt ($85, hand-signed with a tick) and a photocopy of the notarized paper she made before Gordon filed it. The tick is missing. Marisol's fear: her mother-in-law's rent, the house fund, the job. Josie says only: "Nobody does anything today." The loan: closing Friday at ten, one hundred eighty-five thousand dollars against the house on Hollis Street, and the lender needs her own signature. Ends: Marisol at the door: "He told Mr. Teague that Mrs. Brandt signed the witness line. Mrs. Brandt is your daughter."
4. **Forgetful** — Josie's decision (calm): she does not call Nicole. Fern next door; Fern's reaction (a barn coat and a thermos); drive to the Marlow County Sheriff's Office; Detective Ray Hatch, tall, thermos, crossword in pen. He listens, looks at the receipt and the photocopy, "Believing is not proving, ma'am. I need them to say it out loud, with you in the room, on a day when it matters." Plan born: she will be a forgetful old woman for two days; she will sign nothing that isn't Friday's; she will ask harmless questions. Wave two: that evening Curtis brings a ham and a "memory screening appointment" brochure; takes her car keys "for the ice"; calls her "Josie, hon." She loses her glasses on purpose twice and finds them in the refrigerator, to the family's quiet horror and her own private delight. Ends: Hatch's text at nine at night with a photo of the witness line: "Nicole Brandt, December 2."
5. **The Witness Line** — Mid-turn: seeing her daughter's handwriting on the forged page. Josie's grief and her mother's mind: Nicole at six, at nineteen, at her father's funeral. Thursday morning Nicole comes with soup (the same chicken soup she brought on November nineteenth, the day after the surgery); Josie tests her gently, "Did I sign anything in November?" Nicole, crying: "Mama, you signed it in the office, you were fine, you just forgot, Curtis said you'd be embarrassed if I made a fuss." Nicole did not see her sign and was told not to ask. Josie sees it: her daughter has been talked into believing her mother is losing her mind, because it is the one story that explains the paper. She does not tell her. Hatch's reasoning: if Nicole warns Curtis, the closing never happens and he tries again later with something quieter. Josie holds her tongue, the hardest thing she did that week. Thursday evening: Curtis in her kitchen, on speaker thinking she is napping in the sunroom: the Tri-County man, two hundred twelve thousand, "February first," "after Friday it's all in order." Josie hears every word. Ends: she walks quietly to her sewing table and threads a needle, "because that is what my hands do when the rest of me is very angry."
6. **Rehearsal** — Thursday night into Friday morning. Fern, Marisol, Hatch at the kitchen table (or by phone and in the sunroom): the plan. The recorder (her phone in the cardigan pocket; she is a party to the conversation); how Hatch gets into the file room through Marisol; the questions Josie will ask like a confused old woman ("Remind me, hon, what was the first paper again? Where was I when I signed it?"). The proof she already has: the Riverbend Eye Surgery Center discharge papers, in the kitchen drawer with the rubber bands, with printed times; Fern's photo, time-stamped three-twelve, Josie in a clear plastic shield and sunglasses, Fern's text "Pirate" (humor). Josie realizes, laughing, that Curtis chose the date Gordon's calendar was free, not hers. Marisol at midnight texts: Gordon knows someone talked. "Is it still on?" Josie writes back: "It's still on. Bring your receipt." Friday morning: she dresses carefully (Earl's button in her cardigan pocket, the one she has carried three years), puts on her reading glasses, and puts the phone in her pocket. Ends: Curtis on the porch at nine-fifteen, in a good coat, with a to-go coffee for her: "Ready to get ahead of it, hon?" "I think I'm ready," she says. "I only hope I remember why."
7. **Page Four of Four** — The closing. Conference room, the lender's coordinator on speakerphone, Gordon with his pen cap, Nicole next to her, Curtis across. Josie plays confused, asks questions, takes her time; Curtis explains, kind and tight; he describes the first signing for the lender and for her ("you came in on the eighteenth, you wore your blue church dress, you signed twice because the first pen smeared"); Gordon nods along and says "Two-fifteen, I remember." The lender's coordinator asks them to confirm the borrower's identity and in-person signing of the earlier power of attorney. Josie: "Gordon, was it raining?" "It was dry." Then she takes her glasses off, puts the Riverbend papers on the table, the photo on Nicole's side, and says the date out loud. "On the eighteenth of November I was lying in a recovery chair in Cleveland, Tennessee with a plastic shield taped to my face. Nicole, you brought me chicken soup the next evening." She takes the receipt out; Marisol, at the door, with her notebook, says "I kept a copy." The tick: Josie signs her name on a blank page slowly and slides it toward the lender's speakerphone, the notary and the daughter: "Look under the r." The forged page has no tick. Curtis tries the old story (Mom is confused). Nicole: "She just read a hospital paper out loud, Curtis." The coordinator says the loan is canceled and recorded. Detective Hatch comes in from the file room, quietly. Gordon takes his pen cap off the table and starts to cry. Curtis asks, "Nicole, honey, let's not do this here." Nicole takes off her wedding ring? No: she stands up, says nothing, and goes to stand by the window. Ends: Hatch says "Mister Brandt, I'd like you to come with me for a conversation," Josie hears the stool of Gordon's chair scrape, and understands she hasn't raised her voice once.
8. **The Needle** — Aftermath with a mild, factual summary (no jail graphics): Curtis charged with forgery and attempted theft by deception, out on bond, in plea talks; Gordon's commission revoked, cooperating; the loan void; Tri-County is Curtis's own problem. Nicole in Josie's spare room, ashamed, not able to look at her for four days; the quiet conversation on the fifth (no speeches). Josie hasn't forgiven everything; she has decided to let her daughter come to the table. The mending table in the church fellowship hall every Tuesday; Marisol working for the county clerk now (Hatch put in a word), buying a house with a porch; Fern's quilt. The sticky note in the sewing box. One Saturday Nicole arrives with a torn winter coat, sets it on the sunroom table and asks if Josie still takes jobs; Josie hands her a needle and says, "Hold the thread like this." A short wise line (people who love you can be wrong; ask for your glasses when you need time). End with a question to the listener: would you have told Nicole earlier, and have you ever been handed a pen when you needed time; where are you listening from.
