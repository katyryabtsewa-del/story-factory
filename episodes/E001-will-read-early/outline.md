# E001 — Outline (12 chapters, 1,100–1,300 words each, target ~14,000 words ≈ 90 minutes)

## Chapter 1 — The Spreadsheet
- Cold open (first 150–250 words): September, Walter's one-year memorial lunch in Diane's own house. Carrying a tray of coffee cups past the den, she hears her children dividing her life: Brittany reading from a tablet, "The lake cabin, the jewelry, the house. She won't last five years, and the house alone is three-forty." Kevin laughs. Rachel asks about Grandma's sapphire ring. One-line promise: two months later Diane invited all three of them to hear her will read early, at Thanksgiving, and only one sentence in it mattered.
- Who she is: Diane Whitaker, sixty-nine, Harlan Creek, Ohio; thirty-one years running the Blue Kettle with Walter; Walter's oak table; the pies.
- Walter's death fourteen months ago, quick and quiet; the empty chair.
- The children introduced through the memorial lunch: Kevin and Brittany arriving late in a leased SUV, Rachel from Indianapolis, Tommy who came early and set up the chairs, Olivia in the kitchen helping with the pie.
- Diane walks into the den with the tray as if she heard nothing. Brittany turns the tablet face down too fast.
- Hook: later, clearing up, Diane sees the tablet screen light up on the arm of the couch: a spreadsheet titled "Mom — Exit Plan."

## Chapter 2 — Little Colored Dots
- Re-orient: the weeks after the memorial.
- Brittany starts "helping Mama Di declutter" on Saturdays. Small colored sticker dots appear on the backs of furniture: blue, green, yellow. Diane realizes each color is a child.
- Rachel asks to borrow the sapphire ring (Diane's mother's) "to wear to a wedding" and does not bring it back.
- Contrast: Tommy fixes the porch step without being asked and stays for supper; Olivia bakes a sour cherry pie with Diane and asks about the Blue Kettle; warm scene at the table.
- Diane counts the dots one night and finds a blue dot on the underside of Walter's kitchen table.
- Hook: Kevin calls, soothing voice: "Mom, Brittany and I are coming Saturday. We need to talk about your future. We're bringing someone."

## Chapter 3 — The Man With the Laser Tape
- Saturday: Kevin and Brittany arrive with a realtor friend in a quarter-zip sweater who "just wants to look." He measures rooms with a laser tape and says "great bones" four times.
- Over coffee Kevin pitches the sixty-thousand-dollar "car wash franchise opportunity." Diane asks three bookkeeper questions he cannot answer. She says no.
- Kevin's mask slips for a second ("Dad would have said yes"). Brittany smooths it over and hands Diane a glossy folder for Larkspur Commons Senior Living: "We booked you a tour Thursday. Just to look."
- Diane goes on the tour to keep the peace; sharp, funny observations; a resident there tells her, "My kids booked me a tour too. That was four years ago."
- Hook: back home, Diane finds a page the realtor left under the fruit bowl: a draft listing for 214 Linden Street, "Coming soon — spring."

## Chapter 4 — The Stove
- Late October, routine checkup with her doctor of twenty years.
- The doctor gently asks about leaving the stove on and getting lost driving to church. Diane learns "a family member called with concerns." It was Brittany.
- Humiliation: Diane, who ran a diner kitchen with six burners going for thirty-one years, sits on the paper sheet and feels small for the first time in her life.
- Then she sits up straight and asks for a full cognitive evaluation, "the longest one you have, on paper."
- Drive home past the old Blue Kettle building; memory of Walter at the grill, how he wrote everything down.
- Hook: a certified letter is waiting in her mailbox, from a law firm in Columbus.

## Chapter 5 — Certified Mail
- The letter, "on behalf of concerned family members": suggests she sign a durable power of attorney naming Kevin, or the family "may pursue court-supervised options."
- She reads it twice at Walter's table, wiping the clean counter.
- Phone calls: Kevin ("It's for your protection, Mom"), then Rachel, nervous, who almost says something and doesn't.
- Tommy comes for Sunday supper; she nearly tells him and decides not to drag him in yet.
- Diane resolves to call Harold Greene in the morning.
- Hook: that night, a car in the driveway at nine o'clock: Olivia, on a school night, with the old family iPad and red eyes. "Grandma, you need to see this."

## Chapter 6 — Operation Sunset
- Midpoint. Olivia explains: the old iPad is still signed in to her father's account; she saw the group chat named "Operation Sunset."
- Diane reads it at the kitchen table while Olivia eats leftover pie: the Larkspur Commons plan, "list the house in March, spring market," "Tommy gets nothing extra, he's had enough free dinners," "The stove story will work, she's sixty-nine, nobody questions it," and Rachel: "I don't feel good about the doctor thing."
- Diane's quiet reaction; the line about Tommy hurts most.
- She photographs every message, tells Olivia not to delete anything and not to tell anyone, and promises to protect her. Olivia asks if she is in trouble; Diane: "Sweetheart, you're the only one in that chat who isn't."
- Diane decides, calmly. She will not yell, and she will not beg.
- Hook: "The next morning I put on my good coat and drove to see the only man Walter ever trusted with a secret."

## Chapter 7 — Harold's Office
- Harold Greene's office above the hardware store; bow tie, coffee, fishing photos with Walter on the wall.
- Diane lays everything out. Harold reminds her of the Whitaker Family Trust from six years ago, after Walter's heart scare: house and savings inside, Diane sole trustee, revocable. "You can change it any day you like, Diane. That's rather the point."
- They plan: the protective clause (the exact sentence from the bible), the house to Tommy with her right to live there, the Blue Kettle Scholarship, Rachel left as she is. Harold explains that a share treated as if the child died first passes to that child's children. Diane realizes what that means for Kevin and Olivia.
- Harold hands her nothing yet but mentions a sealed envelope from Walter: "For Diane, if the kids ever start circling. Read it to all of them." Diane does not open it.
- Hook: Harold: "Walter made me promise to read it out loud. All of them in one room. So the question is, Diane, how do you want to get them there?"

## Chapter 8 — Playing Along
- November 3: the amendment signed in Harold's office with a notary and two witnesses. Second week of November: the cognitive evaluation comes back completely normal; the doctor writes a letter.
- Diane plays along: tells Kevin she is "thinking about the papers" and wants the whole family together at Thanksgiving to talk it through.
- Then her idea, said sweetly on the phone: since everyone is so interested in her will, Harold will come to Thanksgiving and read it, "so there are no surprises later." Kevin is delighted. Brittany calls her "Mama Di" three times in one call.
- Tension: Brittany insists Kevin bring the power of attorney papers to sign "after pie." Rachel calls and asks, too casually, whether Harold is really coming.
- Diane and Olivia plan the menu like a diner order, Blue Kettle style.
- Hook: Olivia texts her a screenshot from the chat. Brittany: "Champagne's on me. Thanksgiving is payday."

## Chapter 9 — Thanksgiving Morning
- Thanksgiving morning: Diane up at five, the turkey, three pies, Walter's table extended with both leaves.
- Tommy arrives early to help and asks why Harold Greene is coming to dinner. Diane says, "Because Harold has never missed my pie." Tommy studies her and lets it go.
- Rachel arrives early, alone, nervous; tries twice to say something in the pantry and cannot.
- Kevin and Brittany arrive dressed up, with champagne and a leather folder. Brittany comments on how much easier life will be at Larkspur Commons, "no more stairs, Mama Di."
- Dinner: grace said by Tommy; Brittany's sing-song voice; Kevin carving Walter's way, badly; the empty chair at the end.
- Hook: over pie, Diane taps her glass with a fork. "Harold, I think it's time."

## Chapter 10 — One Sentence
- Harold puts on his reading glasses. He reads the trust: created six years ago, the house and investments inside, Diane trustee. Kevin relaxes, Brittany smiles.
- Then the amendment of November third. Harold reads the one sentence. Silence. Kevin laughs and says it doesn't apply to anyone here.
- Harold lays out, one by one: the Columbus law firm's letter, the doctor's note of Brittany's phone call, the screenshots of Operation Sunset, the cognitive evaluation.
- Kevin: "Where did you get those?" Olivia stands up: "From me." Diane stands next to her.
- Harold explains calmly what "as though he had died before her" means: Kevin's share goes to his daughter, in trust until she is twenty-five. Kevin goes completely still. Brittany erupts; Harold answers her with one dry sentence.
- Rachel quietly takes the sapphire ring out of her purse and sets it on Walter's table, crying. Harold notes she signed nothing and filed nothing; her share is unchanged.
- Hook: Harold takes out a sealed envelope. "There is one more item. Walter asked me to read this out loud, to all of you."

## Chapter 11 — Walter's Letter
- Harold reads Walter's letter (Diane quotes it in pieces, with her reactions). Walter remembers Kevin's first bike, Rachel's spelling bee, Tommy taking apart the toaster; reminds them their mother fed half the town for thirty-one years and kept the books to the penny; "nothing in this world ever got past her, so if you are hearing this, you have already lost." He asks them to be the people they were at this table. Last line: "Diane, the pie is better than mine. It always was."
- Reactions: Tommy in tears; Kevin staring at the table; Brittany gathering her coat.
- Kevin and Brittany leave; Kevin threatens to "get a real lawyer." Harold: "Please do. Have him call me. I'll be fishing, but I'll call him back."
- Rachel stays and washes dishes with Diane; a hard, honest talk. Diane: forgiveness is a recipe that takes time.
- Tommy learns about the house and says he doesn't want a house, he wants his mother around for another thirty years. Diane: "Then you'll get both."
- Hook: "What happened to Kevin over the next six months, I could not have planned if I had tried."

## Chapter 12 — My Door Is Open
- Christmas: a smaller, warmer table: Diane, Tommy, Olivia, Rachel and her son, Harold. Kevin and Brittany stay away; Olivia is allowed to come.
- Winter and spring: Kevin's debts surface, the boat is sold, the big house goes on the market, Brittany goes to stay with her sister for a while. Kevin starts going to a financial counselor.
- April: Kevin comes alone one Sunday with a store-bought pie and an apology that sounds real. Diane lets him in. "My door is open, Kevin. My checkbook is not."
- The Blue Kettle Scholarship's first winner announced at Harlan Creek High; Olivia is accepted to a summer culinary program.
- June: Diane and Olivia drive to the Grand Canyon, Walter's dream trip, with a sour cherry pie in a cooler. A short, warm reflection: being underestimated at sixty-nine is a kind of freedom.
- Closing: speak to the listener in Diane's voice, ask what they would have done about Kevin, would they have let him back in, and where they are listening from. No "like and subscribe."
