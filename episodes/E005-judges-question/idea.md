# E005 — idea

**Title:** My Son Tried to Have Me Declared Unfit So He Could Sell My Orchard. The Judge Asked Me One Question.

Length: 60 min

**Logline:** Vera Halvorsen, 69, retired school bus driver in a small southern Oregon town, sits in a probate courtroom at nine o'clock on a Tuesday morning while her son Brent asks a judge to take away control of her pear orchard, "for her own protection." His petition lists six calls she made to the sheriff about "prowlers" that, he says, never existed. The judge puts the petition down and asks Vera one question about those calls. Her answer, and one number she gives without being asked, is the whole case.

- **Form D (one day, real time):** the story is told through one Tuesday-morning hearing, in the courtroom, with flashbacks to the five weeks before (the process server at the farm stand, the unannounced visit from the court visitor, the calls, the truck). Opens in the courtroom.
- **Villain:** son Brent Halvorsen (44), regional manager at a land-development outfit's partner brokerage; not a cartoon, a man who signed a paper he had no right to sign and then tried to make his mother fit it. Second pressure: developer's man Tucker Rand (soft, smiling, "just a timing matter").
- **Humiliation:** the petition read aloud in open court: a list of her "incidents", delivered in a flat voice by Brent's lawyer; Brent patting her hand and saying "Mom, nobody's blaming you."
- **Payoff mechanism (NOT hidden ownership, NOT a lawyer reveal):** witness from the past plus public records. Vera counts: she drove a school bus for thirty-seven years and counts heads twice, so she counts everything. The "prowlers" were a survey crew of two men and a white truck, seen on her back acres on six evenings; she called the sheriff each time and gave the plate. Her answer to the judge's one question names the plate, the times and the number of men, and the sheriff's call log matches it to the minute. The truck belongs to the company hired by the developer. Brent had signed an option agreement promising the developer access and "authority to deliver" the land. Judge asks Brent one follow-up question and he cannot answer without admitting the deal. No document is read aloud at a party; nothing is hidden.
- **Ending: open / new life.** The petition is dismissed. No jail. Vera puts the orchard under a conservation agreement with a local land trust (her choice, not a trick), starts a small u-pick day for the kids she used to drive, and has an unanswered voicemail from Brent she has not yet decided to return.
- **Allies:** neighbor Pearl Dunbar (71, farm stand, loud, plumbing-van driver's widow), sheriff's deputy Danny Ocampo (36, rode her bus as a boy), court visitor Imani Okafor (calm, unannounced visit, keeps her opinion for the judge). The lawyer the court appointed is young and unnamed. No grandchild-ally, no lawyer reveal.
- **Variety:** different from E001 to E004 on villain (son but with a developer), setting (Oregon, late March, courtroom and orchard), scene (courtroom hearing, not a party), mechanism (public records + witness), form D, tone (tense with dry humor), ending (open / new life), heroine (school bus driver, not a cook or stenographer).
