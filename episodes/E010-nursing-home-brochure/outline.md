# E010 — Outline (60 min, 8 chapters, 1,100–1,200 words each)

Form A with an investigative spine. Skeleton: cold open → who she is → three waves → midpoint (the file and the overheard call) → quiet plan → the read-back → new life. Dry humor throughout; Peg never raises her voice. No hidden deed, no lawyer reveal, no document read aloud at a party.

1. **The Brochure** — Cold open at the Elk Horn Supper Club, Saturday evening: Peg turns seventy; Renata gives a gift bag; inside is a glossy brochure for Silverleaf Terrace, a sticky flag on page nine, and a "reservation confirmation." Renata says "Mom, I just want you safe." The table goes quiet; her son-in-law studies his plate. Peg's one-line promise: "Before Halloween a state inspector would be standing in that building's lobby, and I'd be the reason, and I want to be fair to Renata: for a while I truly believed she meant well." Who she is: thirty-six years on the radio, the calm voice, Hank, the green notebook. She answers: "Thank you. I'd like to see it properly. The brochure says a person can stay a week." Ends: at home she reads the confirmation: reservation deposit, two thousand five hundred dollars, card ending four-four-one-seven. It was her card.
2. **Card Ending 4417** — Next morning at Lu's kitchen (cats, coffee): the earlier waves in short scenes: the kettle left on once; Renata taking over her pharmacy and mail; the "concerns" Renata told the doctor's office about; the realtor "friend" who stopped by in September to "just look at the light." Peg calls the bank, replaces the card without a word to Renata. Lu: "You want to fight her?" Peg: "I want to know what's true. A call can sound like one thing and be another." Humor: Lu's retired-dispatcher gallows jokes. Peg calls Silverleaf, books the respite week (Gideon's warm voice: "Welcome home, Miss Peggy"). Renata calls, delighted she is "being so mature about it." Ends: Peg packs one bag, her glasses, and the notebook, and writes the first line: "Sunday, 9:40 a.m., arriving."
3. **Welcome Home** — Sunday, October eleventh. Check-in, the dead fountain, the "Welcome Home" banner, the smell of boiled carrots and hand sanitizer. Gideon in his vest gives the tour and the screening himself ("I'm certified, it's just a little chat") in his office, 10:12 to 10:31; five words, the clock, the date (she misses by a day); he turns the form over before he scores. Then, "Because we're short on beds in the Garden Wing, I'll put you in Birch Court for your stay." The keypad door, the laminated "Today is" cards. Peg's room, the window with a view of a parking lot. Ends: at the first dinner a man in a plaid blanket slides a surveyor's pencil across the table and says "Forty-one paces to that door, ma'am, and in nine years nobody has opened it for me from the inside."
4. **Eleven, Nineteen, Twenty-Seven** — Monday and Tuesday: Peg times the call lights (eleven, nineteen, twenty-seven minutes), counts staff, listens to the building the way she once listened to a phone line; Cyril introduces himself and his pacing log; Imelda, one aide for twenty-four residents after ten, pink scrubs, rosary. Peg helps carry a tray and gets told off kindly. Daytime: Renata's cheery phone call. Night: 2:40 a.m., a crash; Cyril on the floor of the hallway; Imelda's hands full with another resident. Peg does what she was trained to do: calm voice, checks him, calls nine-one-one from the hall phone; the dispatcher on the line is a woman Peg trained twenty years earlier. Ends: the elevator opens and Gideon comes in wearing a fleece vest over pajamas, and says: "Who authorized that call?"
5. **What the Form Says** — Wednesday: Gideon's lecture ("we call the administrator first; residents do not make calls"), the ambulance bill, Cyril goes to the hospital and comes back with a cracked wrist. Peg asks for her file as a respite guest. Imelda in the supply room: "He needs six more beds filled by Christmas. He does the test himself." Thursday morning the file: nineteen of thirty, "moderate concern, recommend secured memory care," a nurse's signature stamped 10:20 a.m. on October eleventh. Peg's notebook: the test ended at 10:31. The director of nursing, she learns, spent that Sunday at a conference in Denver (name tags, a lanyard on a hook). Midpoint decision, calm. In the parking lot Renata arrives with a folder: the residency agreement for Birch Court and a financial power of attorney "for convenience." Peg, from the open window of her room, hears Renata on the phone: "Seven-five, my husband says the bank wants it by the thirtieth; she signs Thursday and I get it by Friday." Ends: Peg writes the time in the margin: "Thursday, 3:52 p.m. Renata. Seven-five. The thirtieth."
6. **Odette** — The decision to tell someone who can do something. Peg's call to Lu: "Who do we know?" Lu knows Odette from her church's casserole committee. Saturday at the Alder Bench library community room: Odette, granola bar, canvas tote; Odette explains in general terms (resident rights, the state health department, unannounced visits, "I'm not a lawyer and I'm not a cop, I'm a person who walks into buildings and asks"). Peg shows the notebook; the read-back habit; Odette is quiet; Cyril's pacing log (Imelda smuggled a photo); Imelda herself, in her car in the lot at seven in the morning, decides to talk ("Eleven years. I will say it to anyone with a badge"). Odette files the complaint Monday. Peg returns for her last days and tells Gideon she'll stay for the "family care conference" he has set for Thursday to "finalize." Humor: Peg learns to pretend to forget her glasses. A night visit from Renata, with leftover birthday cake and real tears: her husband's trucking loan, the balloon; Peg nearly gives in. Ends: Renata says "I'm only trying to get ahead of it," and Peg answers gently, "I know," and writes the time down after she leaves.
7. **Read Back** — Thursday the twenty-second, ten a.m., a conference room with a bowl of mints and a "Welcome Home" mug. Present: Gideon, Renata, Renata's husband, the director of nursing, Peg. Gideon presents the "assessment results" and the agreement; Renata pushes the pen. Peg asks politely if she may read something back. She does: times, the five words, the clock, the line "turned the form over," the call-light times, the fall at 2:40. She asks the director of nursing where she was on the morning of October eleventh; the director admits (stiffly) she was at a conference in Denver and never saw Peg. Renata looks at Gideon. At ten-twenty the receptionist knocks: two state surveyors in the lobby with a laptop and Odette behind them; Imelda in the doorway in her pink scrubs, hands shaking. Gideon: "Let's not make this about paperwork." The surveyor: "Actually, it's going to be about paperwork." Peg says to Renata, quietly, "Card ending four-four-one-seven." Renata's husband, who has said nothing all morning, puts his hands flat on the table: "Renata. She's told you the number." Peg leaves with her bag; Cyril salutes with a surveyor's pencil. Ends in the parking lot: Renata, behind her, says "Mom," and Peg doesn't turn around, yet.
8. **Thursdays** — What happened next on ordinary mornings: the state's findings (altered assessments across fourteen residents, the hold on new admissions to Birch Court, fines, Gideon and the administrator "no longer with the company," the deposit refunded, Cyril to the Garden Wing and later home with his niece and a lift chair); Hearthstone loses its contracts; Renata's husband sells two trucks; the balloon is renegotiated with a bank officer who had read the news; December sixth, Renata at Peg's kitchen table without a folder: the real talk; Peg's condition: Renata contacts every family she placed, and comes on Thursdays to sit with residents. January: Peg's thirty-six hours of training; April: the first Thursday round, a badge on a lanyard, a hallway and a call light she answers in under two minutes; the house, the aspens, the notebook, Hank's line. Last lines + question for the listener (would you have forgiven Renata, or only let her sit in the hallway?) and where they are listening from.
