# E006 — idea

**Title:** "Don't Sign. The First One Wasn't You." A Sticky Note Under the Clipboard Stopped Me With the Pen Already in My Hand.

Length: 60 min

**Backlog:** idea 7, rewritten per `channel/variety.md` item 7 (not Christmas, not a granddaughter, not a lawyer reveal: January, a notary's young assistant, a seamstress's private signature mark). Ideas 11, 10 and 9 skipped this night for variety limits (form A would be 3 of 5; hidden ownership; heroine who cooks).

**Logline:** Josephine "Josie" Ledbetter, 71, retired alterations tailor in a small Tennessee town, sits in a title-and-notary office on a gray Tuesday in January with a pen in her hand. Her son-in-law Curtis calls the stack in front of her "Medicare housekeeping." Her daughter Nicole sits beside her and gently reminds her that she forgets things now. Under the clipboard is a yellow sticky note in unfamiliar handwriting: "Don't sign. The first one wasn't you. Say you need your glasses." Josie has never seen that office before. The "first one" is a power of attorney with her name on it, dated a day she never went anywhere near the place.

- **Form B (mystery with a quiet trap):** opens on the sticky note; the story unwinds who wrote it, what "the first one" is, why Curtis needs her live signature on Friday, and whether her own daughter is part of it. Told by Josie in the past tense with dry, warm humor (a seamstress notices what other people miss).
- **Villain:** son-in-law Curtis Brandt (47), "financial adviser" with a smile and a phrase ("let's get ahead of it"). Owes a private lender two hundred twelve thousand dollars due the first of February. Used a forged power of attorney to start a home-equity loan of one hundred eighty-five thousand dollars on Josie's paid-off house; the lender needs the borrower's own signature at closing, so he needs Josie to sign "the Medicare papers" that are really the loan closing. Second wrongdoer: Gordon Teague (58), the notary, Curtis's golf partner, who stamped the first paper without Josie in the room.
- **Daughter Nicole (44), dental office manager:** signed the witness line on the forged paper because Curtis told her Mom had signed in November and "was embarrassed about forgetting." She has been talked into believing her mother is slipping. Not a villain: a person used. Josie does not tell her until the room, and the not-telling is the hardest thing she does.
- **Humiliation / opening scene:** NOT a party. The conference room; Nicole saying in front of strangers "Mom, your glasses are in your purse, remember?"; Curtis patting her hand; Josie handing over her dignity along with her pen while everyone watches her stall. She says her glasses are in the car and walks out to read the note.
- **Payoff mechanism (not hidden ownership, not a lawyer reveal, not documents read at a party):** (1) Josie's private signature habit: for forty years she signs with a tiny back-stitch tick under the last letter, "a knot in the thread," a joke from her late husband Earl; (2) Marisol Vega, Gordon's twenty-six-year-old assistant, kept a three-year-old alterations receipt from her wedding dress with Josie's hand-signed tick; she saw the forged paper had none, and on November eighteenth nobody named Ledbetter came into the office; (3) Josie's own cataract surgery was November eighteenth: discharge papers with times, plus a photo Fern took of her in the shield, time-stamped "pirate"; (4) Josie plays forgetful for days so that Curtis and Gordon describe the November signing out loud in detail ("you wore your blue church dress, you signed twice, the first pen smeared") while she records, legally, as a party to the conversation; (5) Detective Raymond Hatch of the sheriff's office financial crimes unit sits in the next room.
- **Ending: forgiveness (conditional, slow) and a new small life.** Curtis charged (forgery, attempted theft by deception) and in plea talks; Gordon's notary commission revoked and he cooperates; the loan is cancelled; Nicole leaves Curtis, ashamed, and shows up at Josie's sunroom with a torn coat. Josie hands her a needle. Josie starts a Tuesday-morning mending table at the church hall; Marisol gets a better job. The sticky note lives in her sewing box.
- **Allies:** Marisol Vega (26, notary assistant, brave and frightened, bridal-gown story), Fern Albright (73, neighbor, blunt, drove her to surgery, quilting circle), Detective Raymond Hatch (54, slow-talking, Earl's old fishing acquaintance through Fern). No grandchild-ally. No lawyer. No bank scene this time.
- **Setting:** Sparrow Gap, Tennessee (fictional), January, ice and gray rain; Teague Title and Notary on the courthouse square; Josie's sunroom sewing table.
- **Variety:** vs E001–E005: villain (son-in-law, financial adviser), place (Tennessee, January, title office), scene (sticky note at signing), mechanism (forged power of attorney + signature mark + notary assistant + medical alibi + recorded trap), form B, tone (warm cozy-mystery with dry humor), ending (conditional forgiveness, new life), heroine (alterations tailor, not a cook).
