# E008 — Story bible
Narrator: Dot, first person, past tense, telling it the following summer from a screened porch. Dry, brisk, funny without trying, a school nurse's habits (takes a pulse of a room, keeps a bandage in every pocket, can tell a faked stomachache at forty paces). No sermons. Form D: three days, Wednesday to Friday of Thanksgiving week, with a short epilogue.

## Characters (8 named)
**Dorothy "Dot" Ellery** — narrator, 68. Retired school nurse (thirty-three years, Harrow Consolidated School, East Harrow, Maine; fictional town on fictional Lake Wassumkeag). Widow of Frank (died six years ago, stroke, at seventy). Small, quick, short gray hair, a navy fleece with the school's mascot (a heron) on it, a tackle box of bandages and a thermometer she still carries. Dry humor, notices who looks pale. Lives at the lake house year round (turkey is bought in Harrow, a forty-minute drive away). Drives a green Subaru wagon with two hundred thousand miles. Cooks plainly and well, but she does not bake pies ("pies were Frank's department, and Frank is gone, so we buy them"; Frank baked the pies).
**Frank Ellery** — late husband, a boat builder and lake caretaker, built the lake house himself in 1989 from salvaged cedar, put the deed in Dot's name alone ("so nobody can ever sell your roof out from under you") and added in the deed a clause: if she ever sells, the neighbor to the north, Amos, gets first refusal. Left notebooks of dock measurements and a lopsided canoe.
**Craig Ellery** — son, 44. Regional sales manager in Portland, divorced, cheerful, hurried, genuinely loves his mother in the way a man loves a house he plans to inherit. Says "Mom, it's no big deal." Has a plan he calls "the rebrand": turn the lake house into a short-term rental ("Ellery Lakeside Stays") and move his mother to a place called Pinecrest Commons in town "where someone can check on her." Has not asked her. Never cruel; just careless and sure she will go along.
**Tyler Ellery** — grandson, 17. Hockey player, hoodies, speaks in single syllables, loves his grandmother more than he can say. Part of the group chat that planned a December "lake party."
**Brooke Ellery** — granddaughter, 15. Sharp, funny, wants to be a nurse, always has her phone. She is the one who breaks ranks: she finds the listing and calls Dot at midnight, and on Friday she is the first one out of the car.
**Amos Thibodeau** — neighbor to the north, 74. Retired lobster-boat mechanic turned lake handyman, wears a plaid hat with earflaps in every season, speaks rarely and slowly, has wanted Frank's cove for thirty years and never once asked. His granddaughter's family (two small kids) lives with him and needs room.
**Nan Ouellette** — realtor and notary in East Harrow, 52, runs a one-woman office above the hardware store, drives a pickup with a plow, answers her phone on the first ring. Will close on a holiday weekend because "I'm Catholic, not a monk."
**Dana Whitcomb** — Craig's girlfriend, 41, an event planner who owns a share in a lakeside rental called Pine Ledge Lodge in the next town, forty minutes away. Polished, brisk, a little scared of Dot. She is the reason for the Thanksgiving "change of plans." Not a villain; she realizes mid-weekend that she was told "Craig's mom is thrilled."

## Places, objects, money
- The lake house: a cedar and green-trim house on Wassumkeag Cove, three bedrooms, screened porch, dock, a wood stove Frank installed, an old red canoe, a mudroom with forty years of boots. Deed in Dot's name alone since 1989. Assessed "worth around six hundred and forty thousand" by Nan; sold for six hundred and fifty thousand to Amos and his son-in-law Leo Gagnon (Leo's daughter Jenna, Amos's granddaughter, and her family, two small children and a puppy, move in). Money: one hundred thousand certified check at the table Friday (cleared 7:40), the rest through the title company the following Tuesday; Amos's savings from selling his boat-repair shop two years ago plus a credit union loan pre-approved since October. Deed clause: first refusal "at the price the grantee names"; Amos exercises it (signs an acknowledgment). Dot's own doctor gave a clean cognitive screening in September (letter kept in the tackle box; Nan has a copy). Dot takes only the canoe, Frank's notebooks, her quilt, six sacks of boots and her furniture/clothes; the WOOD STOVE STAYS with the house (sold with it).
- Pine Ledge Lodge (next town, forty minutes): where Craig, the kids and Dana spend Thanksgiving (a heated indoor pool, "a chef," two hundred dollars a night per room).
- The plan Dot finds: on Thursday afternoon, Craig's laptop-synced tablet in the lake house kitchen (he leaves his old tablet there) shows a draft listing: "Lakeside Cedar Retreat, sleeps eight, six hundred dollars a night," old photos from July (taken while Dot was at her sister's in Vermont; quilt off, white comforter on) of her bedroom as "Primary suite" with a "king bed" (it is a queen), a calendar block Dec 31 to Jan 4 (four nights, six hundred a night, Whitcomb Events, deposit two thousand four hundred dollars), and an email thread to Pinecrest Commons asking for a room; Pinecrest offers one from January fifteenth; until then Craig meant to keep her at his place in Portland ("settled by New Year's"). Tablet: Dot sees it Wednesday and assumes it is old; it wakes with a notification Thursday evening. Chat timestamp one forty Thursday afternoon. Craig also messaged Gary Hale to come at ten thirty. Also a group chat among Craig, Tyler and Brooke: Tyler: "Grandma's house is going to be insane for New Year's." Craig: "She won't even be there, she'll be settled by then."
- Legally Craig cannot act without her: she is sole owner; he has no power of attorney. His plan depends on her saying yes "the way she always does." (Say this calmly once. The point is not the law, it is that he never asked.)
- Timeline: Monday, Craig calls ("small change of plans"); Wednesday: Dot drives to the lake house with a twenty-two-pound turkey as she has for thirty-eight years; the family was to arrive that afternoon; instead Craig's call: they will stay at Pine Ledge "because the kids want the pool," Dot is welcome to come Friday. Thursday: Thanksgiving alone, a long phone call she decides not to take, the tablet discovery mid-afternoon. She cooks the whole dinner anyway and takes a plate to Amos; Amos says a few words about Frank and the cove and the clause. Thursday night: Brooke's midnight call ("Grandma, I'm sorry, I didn't know about the Pinecrest part"). Friday: Nan arrives at ten minutes to six; Craig texts 5:50 ("coming early") and 7:40 ("leaving early, be there by ten", Dot replies normally); Brooke offers to slow the car, Dot says do not; 8:00 Leo and the credit union manager; signed 8:48; Nan gives the Mill Street key (heirs accepted by phone, closing in two weeks, occupancy agreement); truck at nine (Kyle); truck and Leo leave 10:15, Leo is not at the house later; 10:35 the family's silver crossover arrives with pies and Gary Hale. Dot sits on the bench at the head of the dock facing the house; Jenna on the porch; heron left at about nine forty and does not return. Card on the mantel is for Amos's family; separate envelope for Craig. Craig apologizes awkwardly on the dock (not full forgiveness). Epilogue in July: Dot lives in the Mill Street farmhouse with a porch; Craig comes every Sunday with a pie he baked himself badly (listing and Pinecrest cancelled, guests' deposit refunded from his pocket); Tyler built a cradle for the canoe in her yard; Brooke volunteers at the clinic.
- Money: sale six hundred and fifty thousand dollars cash; Dot buys the Mill Street farmhouse (vacant, listed by Nan for an estate, asking two hundred and thirty) for two hundred and ten thousand cash from Frank's life insurance; offer made Thursday night, accepted Friday morning; the rest she invests and gives a college fund to each grandchild (eighty thousand each) with a condition (a summer job).

## Motifs
- Dot's tackle box of bandages; she checks everyone's pulse, including a heron on the dock. Dry jokes about turkey, the heated pool, and family group chats.
- Frank's line on the deed: "so nobody can ever sell your roof out from under you." She sells it herself and makes the point that a house is a place to be loved in, not an asset.
- No lawyer reveal at the end: the payoff is a small-town closing at a kitchen table and children arriving at a door that no longer opens for them.
- Names and towns are fictional; no real businesses. Avoid "Maple," "Cobb Hill," "Larkspur," "Fieldstone."
