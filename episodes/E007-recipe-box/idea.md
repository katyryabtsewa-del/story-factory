# E007 — idea

**Title:** My Daughter-in-Law Threw Out My Recipe Box. Behind Card 117 Was Walt's Last Letter.

Length: 60 min

**Backlog:** idea 9, rewritten per `channel/variety.md` item 7 (form B/E: the box is a mystery left by the late husband, no villain-document, no lawyer reveal, no hidden ownership). Skipped this night: 10 (hidden ownership of the lake house, grandchildren as allies, Thanksgiving), 11 (hidden ownership of the business), 1 (overlaps E001, house-trust mechanism), 2 (hidden ownership, DIL villain again). The heroine does NOT cook: she is a retired rural mail carrier who collected recipes from her route and never made one (limit: one cooking heroine in five; E003 was a restaurateur).

**Logline:** Winifred "Winnie" Brandvold, 70, retired rural route mail carrier in fictional Fenmore, Iowa, wakes on a rainy Tuesday in May to find her kitchen "decluttered" by her daughter-in-law Tessa, a professional home organizer: the old tin box with 212 recipe cards, collected from thirty-four years of porches on Route Three, went into a rented dumpster that is already at the county transfer station. Winnie drives there at dawn in her late husband Walt's rubber boots. Behind card 117, wet and half-pulped, she finds a letter in Walt's handwriting: "Win, if you are reading this, Tessa threw out the box. Don't be mad at her. Then go and get the red toolbox before she sells it."

- **Form E (warm, no true villain) with a mystery thread (B):** opens at the transfer station at dawn (flash-forward), then back one week. What is in the letter, why red toolbox, why the dots on certain cards.
- **Tone:** warm, nostalgic, dry humor ("I can burn water; the box was never about cooking"). Winnie is a woman who knows every porch, mailbox and dog in the county.
- **Tessa Brandvold (41), the antagonist, not a monster:** certified home organizer with a small business ("Clear Space by Tessa"), cold, efficient, kind in a clipboard way. Her mother died under thirty years of hoarded newspapers when Tessa was twenty-nine; she cannot see a full cupboard without panic. Escalation in three waves: (1) she "reorganizes" Winnie's kitchen and Walt's closet while Winnie is at a dentist appointment; (2) she lists Walt's workshop tools on an online marketplace and brings brochures for a fifty-five-plus condominium near her and Greg in West Des Moines; (3) the dumpster, and the plan to list the house in June with a stager and a realtor, with Greg's reluctant "yes."
- **Greg Brandvold (46), son:** claims adjuster, gentle, conflict-avoidant, grieving his father and hiding it in logistics. Signs where Tessa points. His turn: he drives out to the transfer station and stands in the rain without a word.
- **Payoff mechanism (not a document at a party, not a lawyer):** Walt's last letter (written three weeks before he died, tucked behind card 117) explains the pencil dots in the corners of nineteen cards: households on Route Three that Walt, a retired shop teacher, quietly helped for twenty years (a wheelchair ramp, a furnace, a roof, a winter fuel bill) without telling anyone, not even Winnie. The red toolbox holds his ledger and the passbook of the "Route Fund," eighteen thousand four hundred dollars saved from his workshop repairs, with a request: keep checking on the porches. Winnie, who knows every porch, takes over. The nineteen households, one by one, show up (the open house that Tessa organized for Saturday morning turns into a church-basement-style potluck on Winnie's lawn: thirty route neighbors arrive with dishes made from the recipes on her cards, many of them hers). The realtor leaves; Winnie says one calm sentence ("I'm not moving, Tessa. I'm busy on Tuesdays.").
- **Ending: partial reconciliation / new life, no scandal.** Tessa tells Winnie about her mother; stays on the lawn with a paper plate; asks to be taught the route. Greg apologizes. Winnie keeps the house, starts the "Tuesday Route" (visiting the dotted porches), and writes her first recipe card herself, in Tessa's kitchen, a very bad one that Tessa laminates. Walt's letter lives in the tin box, with the lid taped.
- **Allies:** Hilda Voss (88, route customer since 1991, sharp, uses a walker, card 117's author: rhubarb crisp), Del Okafor (58, transfer-station attendant, ex-Navy, helps her dig). No grandchildren in the plot. No lawyer. Setting: Fenmore, Iowa (fictional, Larkin County), May, rain; Winnie's two-story white farmhouse on Elm Row; Route Three gravel roads, mailboxes, porches.
- **Variety:** vs E002–E006: place (Iowa, May, rural gravel route), scene (a decluttered kitchen and a dumpster, at dawn at the transfer station), mechanism (late husband's letter + toolbox + community of porches), form E with mystery thread, tone (nostalgic cozy with humor), heroine (retired mail carrier, non-cook), villain type is a daughter-in-law as in E002, but cold-organizer motive (fear, grief), not greed, and she is redeemed.
