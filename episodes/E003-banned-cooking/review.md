## Обязательно исправить (must fix)

1. **Gregory and Marco: the biggest continuity hole.** Location: ch03 L7 ("You wouldn't know him."), ch03 L25 ("Gregory knew Marco had opened a place"), ch04 L61 (the tasting), ch06 L21–25 (Gregory invites "Chef Marco Reyes", shakes his hand).
   - Problem: Gregory is 39. Marco joined Pruitt's Table when Gregory was about ten, and ran the line and then the whole kitchen for twenty years. Gregory grew up beside him. He knows Marco opened a restaurant. Yet he says of the caterer's chef, "You wouldn't know him", sits at a tasting with Marco standing at the end of the table, and neither man says "Marco!" or "Greg!". Courtney also never learns they know each other. A listener will notice this at once, and the whole secret rests on it.
   - Fix (pick one and make it explicit):
     - (a) Best: Gregory knows exactly who Marco is. He picked or accepted Corso because it was the best, and quietly asked Marco at the tasting not to mention Pruitt's Table. Marco kept that promise too, and that is why Marco is "an old family friend" nowhere in Courtney's mind. This makes the toast more deliberate and gives the mid-story real tension.
     - (b) Minimum: add two sentences in ch03 and ch04 explaining why they have not met in years (for example Gregory left the restaurant at eighteen and has not seen Marco since Sam's funeral; Marco did the tasting in his whites and Gregory only half-recognized him). Even this is thin.
   - Also change Gregory's line in ch03 L7 so it does not claim ignorance he cannot have.

2. **Gregory does not recognize his mother's pot roast.** Location: ch02 L41 (he ate two helpings of the same pot roast with horseradish cream in May), ch04 L61 ("reminded him of something, and he could not think what"), ch06 L9 ("that is not how I grew up eating").
   - Problem: three months after eating that dish at his mother's table, he cannot place the dish or the gravy, and then at the wedding he says it is "not how I grew up eating". That is not plausible for a man who calls it the best pot roast in Wisconsin (ch05 L29, via Richard). The heroine's trap depends on him being dim, which weakens it.
   - Fix: let Gregory recognize it, at least at the wedding. Add one beat in ch05 or ch06 where he tastes the short rib at the head table, stops, and goes pale. His toast then becomes a panicked cover-up, which is crueler, more human, and gives the villain more depth than a "drunk on the sound of himself" gag.

3. **Marco never actually promises silence.** Location: ch04 L13–15 versus ch06 L39.
   - Problem: Nora says "nobody says a word" (ch04 L13). Marco replies "Then I'm cooking your food" and nothing else. In ch06 he says "I promised her I would say nothing, and I am breaking that promise". The promise was never made on the page, so the betrayal has no weight and the listener may feel it is a cheat.
   - Fix: in ch04 give Marco one explicit line, for example "Not a word. You have my word, Chef." Then Marco's break in ch06 is a real choice.

4. **Nora's stated plan contains no reveal, so the payoff is luck, not her plan.** Location: ch04 L11 ("Then, whatever happened next would be the truth, told by them.") versus ch06 L21–39.
   - Problem: her plan is silence plus a good meal. The reveal exists only because Gregory happens to invite Marco and Marco happens to break his word. The brief requires a heroine who is smart, not lucky. She should have a contingency.
   - Fix: in ch04 add a conditional so her intelligence is on the page. For example Nora says "If they thank you, take the thanks. If anyone makes a fool of me, you may do as you see fit, but I will not ask it." Or she tells Marco to be near the doors during the toast. Then ch06 L23 ("I did not arrange it") is a nicer, ambiguous line, and ch07 L53–55 ("Fire me?" / "Give yourself a raise") lands as approval of a risk she allowed.

5. **Marco's motive in the toast does not match what was said.** Location: ch06 L39 ("I could not stand there and let anyone call her a woman who worked in food service").
   - Problem: nobody in the tent has said "food service" at that moment. Gregory said "professionals" and "by the kitchen". Only the friend with the fork necklace heard it (ch05 L17–21). A listener will not link it.
   - Fix: have Marco answer the toast itself, for example "You said she let the professionals cook. Gregory, the professional is sitting at table fourteen, and I would like everyone to know it." Keep "food service" for one line at the end if wanted.

6. **Who knows about the forty thousand dollars: Gregory answers a question nobody asked.** Location: ch02 L39 ("I did not mention it to anyone but Edith"), ch07 L31–37.
   - Problem: Nora hands over the card, Courtney opens it and finds a recipe card with no check, and Gregory says "The money. Mom, I didn't ask for it." Nobody has mentioned any money. By the bible and ch02, Gregory has never heard of it.
   - Fix: add one line before Gregory's reply, for example Nora: "There was a check in that card for forty thousand dollars. I took it out last night." Then his line works.

7. **The card: contradictions about when the check came out and what is in it.** Location: ch02 L37 (check plus note "Sam would have wanted you to have a kitchen"), ch05 L23 ("I did not yet know what I would do with it"), ch07 L33–35 ("I wrote this in January", but the card now holds a recipe card and a new line), ch07 L57 (scholarship signed Monday, seven signatures).
   - Problem: ch05 says she is still undecided. In ch07 the card is already emptied and refilled, and the Monday scholarship appointment must have been arranged earlier. "I wrote this in January" cannot be true of a note about Saturdays written later. The kitchen note from ch02 disappears without comment.
   - Fix: choose a clear sequence. Suggested: before the wedding Nora removes the check, puts it in the drawer, and writes the recipe line (one sentence in ch04's summer section). In ch05 L23 say "I had taken the check out and I did not yet know whether I would give them the card at all." In ch07 change "I wrote this in January" to "I bought this card in January." One sentence in ch07 should say Sam's note about the kitchen is now the recipe line.

8. **The money payoff has no stakes for the villains.** Location: ch02 L37, ch07 L37–39, ch08 L19.
   - Problem: Gregory and Courtney never knew about the forty thousand dollars until the terrace, so "keeping the gift" costs them nothing they had counted on. The listener also has not seen them want money. The only hint, "the house must be a lot for you" (ch02 L33), is never paid off. It reads as a setup for selling her house or moving her out, then vanishes.
   - Fix: either (a) have Nora mention to Gregory in ch02 or ch04 that she has "something for the house", and let Courtney's reaction show what she assumed; or (b) cut the "house must be a lot for you" thread, or pay it off on the terrace with one confession line from Gregory.

9. **Duplicated scene: ch01 end and ch02 opening.** Location: ch01 L39–41 (Nora carries half the kringle to Edith across the street; Edith's glasses pushed up; "a very large piece"; "most expensive insult") versus ch02 L3–5 (carries half a kringle across Spaight Street again; Edith brings lemon bars to Nora's porch; they sit in Nora's yellow kitchen; Edith "cut herself a very large piece").
   - Problem: the same morning is told twice with contradictory geography (Nora goes to Edith's house vs Edith comes to Nora's kitchen) and the same cut-the-big-piece gag. The listener is also told twice that this was in March.
   - Fix: keep Edith's scene in ch01 (it has the best line), and open ch02 directly on the back-booth memory (ch02 L9–15) or on Edith's question (L7). Delete ch02 L3 and fix L5 so Edith simply asks the question.

10. **The ch04 hook is spent in the first sentence of ch05.** Location: ch04 L69 (Richard: "why the mother of the groom is at table fourteen") versus ch05 L3 ("I told him to leave my seat exactly where it was. He grumbled, but he agreed.").
   - Problem: the cliffhanger is answered before the chapter starts, with no tension. Also her reason for keeping the seat is never given (the best reason: she wants to be at the doors, where the food comes through).
   - Fix: open ch05 on the wedding morning and move the call's resolution into one line of Nora to Richard in ch04 itself, or leave ch04 on a different question. Give her one sentence of reason ("I wanted to smell the kitchen") when she insists on the seat.

11. **Rhythm: ch01–ch02 hook repeats.** Location: ch01 L43 ("said the name of the caterer... I knew that name") and ch02 L45 ("It's Corso Events.").
   - Problem: two consecutive chapter endings are the same hook. The second one adds only the word Corso, and the listener already knows Nora recognizes it. ch02 as a whole is backstory (Edith, back booth, seating, phone call, card) with no new event until the last line. This is the main sag, roughly minutes 7–14.
   - Fix: end ch01 on the humiliation (for example the smoothing of the tablecloth and "Of course") and let ch02 end on the Corso reveal. Move the "knew that name better than my telephone number" line to the start of ch03, or cut it. Give ch02 one new on-page cruelty (see Nice to have 1).

12. **Opening minute: the hook comes late and is mild.** Location: ch01 L3–9.
   - Problem: the first 60 seconds (about 156 words) cover rolling dough, a text, a blue heart, and "I did not cry". The text is the mildest humiliation in the story. The promise ("every guest in the tent knew exactly who had cooked their dinner... whose name was on the caterer's paperwork") arrives at about word 270, close to the two-minute mark. The formula wants the sharpest point plus the promise within 150–300 words, and the toast line is far sharper.
   - Fix: move the promise paragraph (L9) up to follow the text (after L5 or L7), so it lands before 0:60. Or open on one sentence: "My son banned my kringle from his wedding by text, with a little blue heart, and nine months later his own guests stood and applauded me." Then go to the dough. Also reconsider "whose name was on the caterer's paperwork": her name is not on it (she is an LLC member), so say "who owned the company that was cooking".

13. **Terrace scene is too tidy and over-explained.** Location: ch07 L17–29.
   - Problem: within about five minutes both villains confess everything in full sentences: Gregory ("I was a coward. I'm still a coward. I just got caught.", "ashamed of being ashamed"), Courtney ("I wrote section nine myself" and her whole backstory). It reads like a therapy session and repeats ch02 (Fish Fry Greg, braces). The reckoning feels earned in the tent but then softened at once.
   - Fix: cut Gregory's monologue by half and let one concrete detail carry it (the back booth, or letting people think she was retired from something fancy). Let Courtney say less on the terrace (the note in ch08 L17 can carry the rest). Keep "That's not the same as being finished."

14. **Moralizing near the ending.** Location: ch07 L23 ("protecting a son's pride is not the same as protecting him from what it costs"), ch08 L17 ("forgiveness is not a switch"), ch08 L25 ("Most of the people who have hurt me were not cruel... nobody had ever shown them what happens when you do not flinch... the truth got up in front of a hundred and eighty people and told itself").
   - Problem: the brief asks for a warm ending without preaching. ch08 L25 is a full paragraph of thesis statement. Also "I will tell you that forgiveness is not a switch" is a lecture sentence.
   - Fix: delete ch08 L25 except possibly the one sentence "I do not believe my son was ever ashamed of me. I believe he was afraid of the room." Delete the "forgiveness is not a switch" sentence (the action of handing her a peeler already says it). Delete the ch07 L23 aphorism or turn it into an action.

15. **Final question contradicts the story.** Location: ch08 L29.
   - Problem: "would you have stayed silent the way I did, or would you have stood up and said it yourself?" But Nora did stand up, spoke three sentences (ch06 L49), and Marco spoke for her. The question also asks two things plus "where are you and what is on your stove". The silence-versus-speaking theme is the center of the episode, and the ending muddles it.
   - Fix: ask one clear dilemma, for example "Marco broke his promise to me and I let him. Was he right?" or "Would you have asked your chef to keep quiet, or told the truth at the lunch?" Keep the location question.

16. **Arithmetic: "four words".** Location: ch08 L5 ("I wrote back four words. Bring an apron. Come early.").
   - Problem: that is five words. A listener who counts will notice.
   - Fix: "five words" or "two short sentences".

17. **Abbreviations that the voice will mispronounce or pause on.** Location: ch03 L17 ("Missus P."), ch03 L45 ("C. Ashford").
   - Fix: "Missus Pee" is awkward; write "Mrs P" nowhere. Use "Missus Pruitt" in L17 ("and sometimes Missus Pruitt when he wanted a favor") and "C. Ashford" becomes "Courtney Ashford".

18. **Unquoted written text and signs are hard to hear.** Location: ch02 L37 (the note), ch03 L45 (the clause), ch08 L17 (the note), ch08 L21 (sign).
   - Problem: the style guide forbids italics, so these lines are printed without quotes or markers. A synthetic voice reads them as narration, and the clause in ch03 L45, the plot's key legal line, runs into the sentence "It said, No outside food or beverage. Family members of the couple may not enter service or kitchen areas." without a clear boundary.
   - Fix: put them in quotation marks and add a short attribution ("The clause read, quote,...").

## Желательно (nice to have)

1. **Escalation is too thin before ch06.** Courtney appears on the page at the lunch (ch01), in one line at supper (ch02 L41), second-hand at the tasting (ch04 L51), and in the distance at the wedding (ch05 L51). Gregory's only on-page slights are silence and a phone call about her knees. Between minute 12 and minute 38 nobody does anything to Nora. Suggested fix: one more direct humiliation between ch02 and ch04 (for example a text from Courtney asking her to skip the family photos, or a dress code note that "mothers wear beige", or "cute" about her recipe notebook at the May supper). "Cute" appears on the page only once (ch01 L17) but is called her knife in ch07 L27 and in the ch08 note, so use it a second time earlier.

2. **No jeopardy to the plan during the summer.** Location: ch04 L43–61. The summer is summarized ("nothing at all happened"), the tasting is told second-hand by Priya on the phone. It is the quietest stretch. Fix: dramatize one risk, for example Courtney asks to meet the chef's family, or Gregory phones Nora asking if she knows "a Marco", or Richard visits Corso. If Gregory knows Marco (Must fix 1), this is automatic.

3. **Conflict of interest is glossed.** Location: ch04 L21–23. Priya asks "is this legal?" and Nora answers "Where is the harm?". But Nora owns forty percent of a vendor paid about ninety-one thousand dollars by Richard Ashford (bible), so she profits from her own son's wedding contract, and Marco told Courtney the recipes were "from his family" (ch04 L55). A careful 55+ listener may stop and ask. Fix: one line in ch04 where Nora says her share of the Ashford contract goes back to Richard or to the staff bonus pool, and one line from Richard in ch07 on the dance floor. This also makes Nora more clearly honest.

4. **Clause nine argument is shaky.** Location: ch07 L5 versus ch04 L13. The clause restricts the client's family, and Corso, as the vendor, can waive it at any time. Priya's "strictly speaking you are an owner" is cute but legalistic, and Nora's own joke ("I'd hate to break section nine") suggested she thought she was bound. Fix: have Priya say "Section nine binds the client, not us. It is our kitchen and Marco says the door is open."

5. **Priya and the 40% owner.** Location: ch03 L35. An events manager of two years who has never met the owner of forty percent of her employer, though Nora attends an owners' meeting every January, is a stretch. Fix: say the January meeting is only Nora and Marco and an accountant, in the restaurant, at a time when Priya is on the road.

6. **The Richard thread is warm but under-used.** He senses something (ch05 L33–37) and Nora lies to him in answer to his direct question ("I've heard they're very good"). Nora also asks him to leave the seating alone. A one-line payoff on the dance floor (ch07 L45) would reward him: "You said you'd heard they were very good." Also his question "Do you know anything about this caterer?" is the one lie she tells her only ally, so let her feel it.

7. **Gregory's "drunk".** ch06 L21 says "drunk on the sound of himself" but he took one sip of champagne (L7). Say "giddy" or add a second glass.

8. **Priya's report and the "I knew it" line.** ch06 L43: the cousin in lavender says "I knew it. It tastes like Sunday." She did not know. Change to "I told you it tasted like Sunday."

9. **Microphone claim.** ch06 L47: a woman in her late sixties waves off the cordless microphone to speak to 180 people in a tent with music setup. Fine for character, but add that the tent was dead quiet.

10. **Kitchen geography.** ch05 L39 puts swinging doors in a canvas tent wall; ch07 L51 describes a "tiled blaze of steel and steam" with forty cooks. A tiled kitchen implies a building. Say the kitchen was in the house behind the tent, and the doors lead to a covered walkway.

11. **Bible versus script.** Bible says the meeting with Marco was "late May"; ch03 L27 says "the Monday after the pot roast" (first Sunday in May). Also the bible says the story year is "this year" (2026), but September twentieth, 2026 is a Sunday; the script says Saturday and works for 2025. Do not state the year in narration, and fix the bible. Bible says Nora "keeps the check", ch07 says the card held no check; update the bible.

12. **Title mismatch.** Idea title: "Then the Caterer Recognized Me." In the script nobody recognizes her except Priya (from a photograph, ch03 L29–31) and Marco has known her for thirty years. Adjust the title to "Then the Caterer Stood Up and Said Who I Was" or give Marco a recognition-style line in the toast. Check this with `channel/titles-and-covers.md` before publish.

13. **Tics and repeated images.**
   - Narrator throat-clearing: "I want you to understand / I want you to know / I would like it noted / I would like to tell you / I will tell you" appears about eleven times (ch01 L27, ch03 L9, ch04 L9, ch05 L11, L23, ch06 L23, L41, ch08 L17 and more). Cut half.
   - "yellow kitchen" appears seven times in ch01 to ch08; "a very large piece" twice; Edith's "reading glasses pushed up / on top of her head" three times (ch01 L41, ch02 L5, ch04 L27); "a hundred and eighty" five times in ch06 to ch08; "very food-literate" three times.
   - The tablecloth gesture is defined as "before I say anything that matters" (ch01 L29) but ch06 L19 uses it after the toast, when she says nothing. Use it only before she speaks, or note once that it is also what she does when she is angry.
   - Similes beginning "the way you..." (ch03 L3, ch06 L3, L17) cluster.

14. **"Chief" versus "Chef".** Sam says "chief" (ch03 L15) and Marco calls Nora "Chef"; in ch08 L9 "The retired bus driver calls him chief" and L21 "Chief, I used to eat at your place". The voice will blur them, and the listener cannot tell which is meant. Suggest Sam's word be "boss" or "friend", or add a clause ("chief, spelled with an i, was Sam's word").

15. **Pronunciation risks for the synthetic voice.** Test: Priya, Reyes, Nair, Corso, burrata, kringle, Spaight, Mendota, Racine, Wedgewood. Consider phonetic spelling in the text only if the test fails (for example "Pree-ya"). Tongue-twisters: "sea salt and sage" and "sprigs of sage" (ch02 L23, ch05 L7); "Courtney and Gregory" in a thin font.

16. **Unattributed rapid dialogue.** ch04 L29–35: "With horseradish cream," I said. / "Under another name." / "Wisconsin braised short rib." The second and third lines have no tags, and the speaker alternates. Add "Edith said" and "I said".

17. **Names and real places.** "Verdant" and "Corso" are common restaurant names; the style guide asks for rare, clearly invented names (Verdant is only mildly villainous). None of these is a villain, so no hard-rule violation.

   - ch01 L13: the recipe notebook she carried to the lunch is never used again.
   - ch08 L21: "You must be the lady from the wedding" implies the story spread. Say one line about phones or a video.
   - ch07 L5: Richard "went out and came back, and he said the choice is yours" is unclear (what choice?) and conflicts with L39 (he stood at table one for twenty minutes).

## Does the reckoning work?
- Public: yes, Marco's toast with 180 guests and a standing ovation led by Richard (ch06 L21–43). Earned: yes in the tent, because Gregory invited the chef himself. Weakened by the terrace (Must fix 13) and by villain consequences being light (Must fix 8).
- Ending warm without preaching: warm, yes; preachy in ch08 L25 and the final question (Must fix 14, 15).

## Overall score: 7 / 10
Clean, voice-ready prose, real warmth, and a satisfying public reveal. It loses points for the Gregory-and-Marco plausibility hole, a heroine whose plan has no reveal of its own, a thin middle (minutes 12 to 38), and a gently preachy ending.

## The one change that would most improve retention
Make Gregory aware of who Marco is and of the dishes (Must fix 1 and 2), so that the middle of the story has live jeopardy: at the tasting and again at the head table Gregory knows, says nothing, and then covers his panic with the toast. This repairs the plausibility hole, gives Marco's promise a cost, gives the villain depth, and replaces the passive stretch from ch04 to ch05 with a question the listener cannot put down. If that is too large, the minimum change is to move the promise into the first sixty seconds (Must fix 12) and add one dramatized risk during the summer.
